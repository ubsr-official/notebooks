# Political Parties (NCERT Class X Political Science: Chapter 4)

## What Are Political Parties?

A political party is a group of people who come together to contest elections and hold power in the government. They agree on policies and programs for society to promote collective good. Since different views exist on what is good for all, parties try to persuade people why their policies are better than others.

Political parties reflect fundamental divisions in society and involve partisanship - they are known by which part they stand for, which policies they support, and whose interests they uphold. Every political party consists of three components:
- The leaders
- The active members
- The followers

## Functions of Political Parties

1. **Contest elections** - Parties select candidates to fight elections. In some countries like the USA, party members choose candidates, while in others like India, top party leaders select them.

2. **Formulate policies** - Parties put forward different policies and programs for voters to choose from. They reduce a vast multitude of opinions into a few basic positions.

3. **Make laws** - Parties play a decisive role in lawmaking. Though laws are formally debated in legislatures, most members follow their party leadership's direction.

4. **Form governments** - Parties recruit leaders, train them, and make them ministers to run the government according to their vision.

5. **Play opposition role** - Parties that lose elections voice different views, criticize government policies, and mobilize opposition to the government.

6. **Shape public opinion** - Parties raise and highlight issues. They have members and activists spread across the country and sometimes launch movements to resolve people's problems.

7. **Provide access to government** - Parties help ordinary citizens access government machinery and welfare schemes. People often find it easier to approach local party leaders than government officers.

## Why Do We Need Political Parties?

Political parties are necessary for modern democracies because:

- Without parties, independent candidates would be unable to make promises about major policy changes.
- Government might form but with uncertain utility and no accountability for running the country.
- Even in non-party elections (like some panchayats), the village typically splits into factions that function like parties.
- Parties gather diverse views, bring representatives together, and help form responsible governments.
- The rise of political parties is directly linked to the emergence of representative democracies.

## Types of Party Systems

Different countries have developed different party systems based on their historical and social contexts:

1. **One-party system** - Only one party controls the government (e.g., China). This is not considered democratic as it doesn't permit competition for power.

2. **Two-party system** - Power typically alternates between two main parties, though other parties may exist (e.g., USA, UK).

3. **Multi-party system** - Several parties compete with a reasonable chance of forming government alone or in alliance (e.g., India).

No party system is universally ideal. Each country develops a system based on its social and regional divisions, political history, and electoral system. India has evolved a multi-party system because its vast social and geographical diversity cannot be easily represented by just two or three parties.

## Party System in India

India has a multi-party system where alliances or fronts form when several parties join hands to contest elections. Examples from 2004 included the National Democratic Alliance, United Progressive Alliance, and Left Front.

### National Parties

These are country-wide parties with units in various states that generally follow policies and strategies decided at the national level.

Every party must register with the Election Commission, which offers special facilities to established parties, including unique election symbols that only their official candidates can use.

According to the Election Commission of India's notification in 2023, there are six recognized national parties in India:

1. **Aam Aadmi Party (AAP)** - Formed in 2012 following the anti-corruption movement, focusing on accountability and good governance. Currently forming governments in Punjab and Delhi.

2. **Bahujan Samaj Party (BSP)** - Formed in 1984 under Kanshi Ram's leadership to represent the bahujan samaj (dalits, adivasis, OBCs, and religious minorities). Main base in Uttar Pradesh.

3. **Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP)** - Founded in 1980, emphasizes cultural nationalism (Hindutva) and ancient Indian values. Currently leads the ruling NDA government at the Centre.

4. **Communist Party of India-Marxist (CPI-M)** - Founded in 1964, supports socialism, secularism, and democracy while opposing imperialism and communalism. Strong presence in West Bengal, Kerala, and Tripura.

5. **Indian National Congress (INC)** - One of the world's oldest parties, founded in 1885. A centrist party supporting secularism and welfare of weaker sections. Led the UPA government from 2004 to 2019.

6. **National People's Party (NPP)** - Formed in 2013, the first political party from Northeast India to attain national party status. Focuses on education, employment, and empowerment.

### State Parties

Besides national parties, there are state-level parties recognized by the Election Commission. Over the last three decades, the number and strength of these parties has increased significantly.

Some state parties are all-India parties that have succeeded only in certain states (like Samajwadi Party and Rashtriya Janata Dal), while others are focused on state identity (like Biju Janata Dal, Sikkim Democratic Front, and Telangana Rashtra Samithi).

The rise of state parties has made Parliament more politically diverse and strengthened federalism in India.

## Public Participation in Political Parties

Research on public engagement with political parties shows mixed trends:

- Political parties in South Asia, including India, don't enjoy high levels of public trust.
- Despite this, participation in party activities is relatively high in India compared to many advanced countries.
- The proportion of people reporting party membership in India has steadily increased over three decades.
- The number of people feeling "close to a political party" has also increased in India during this period.

## Challenges Facing Political Parties

Political parties face several challenges that affect their functioning:

1. **Lack of internal democracy** - Power tends to concentrate in the hands of a few leaders. Parties often don't maintain membership registers, hold organizational meetings, or conduct internal elections regularly.

2. **Dynastic succession** - Top positions in many parties are controlled by members of one family, which is unfair to other members and brings inexperienced people to power positions.

3. **Money and muscle power** - Parties focused on winning elections tend to nominate candidates who can raise money or have criminal backgrounds that can help win elections.

4. **Lack of meaningful choice** - Ideological differences between parties have declined over time, not offering voters significantly different policy choices.

## Reforming Political Parties

Several efforts and suggestions have been made to reform political parties:

1. **Constitutional amendment to prevent defection** - MLAs and MPs who change parties now lose their legislative seats.

2. **Supreme Court order on candidate information** - Candidates must file affidavits disclosing property details and pending criminal cases.

3. **Election Commission requirements** - Parties must hold organizational elections and file income tax returns.

Other reform suggestions include:

- Laws to regulate parties' internal affairs, ensuring membership registers, constitutions, and open elections.
- Mandatory minimum representation for women candidates (about one-third of tickets).
- State funding of elections to reduce the influence of money power.

Beyond legal measures, two other approaches can help reform parties:

1. Public pressure through petitions, publicity, and agitations from citizens, pressure groups, and media.
2. Reform-minded citizens joining political parties to improve them from within.

The quality of democracy depends on public participation. As the text concludes, "The problem of bad politics can be solved by more and better politics."

## Important Definitions

- **Political Party**: A group of people who come together to contest elections and hold power in the government, agreeing on policies and programs for society.
- **Partisan**: A person strongly committed to a party, group, or faction, with a tendency to take sides and inability to maintain a balanced view.
- **Ruling Party**: The political party that runs the government.
- **Defection**: Changing party allegiance from the party on which a person got elected to a different party.
- **Affidavit**: A signed document submitted to an officer, where a person makes a sworn statement regarding personal information.
- **One-party system**: A system where only one party controls and runs the government.
- **Two-party system**: A system where power typically alternates between two main parties.
- **Multi-party system**: A system where several parties compete for power with multiple parties having a reasonable chance of forming government alone or in alliance.
- **National Party**: Country-wide parties with units in various states that follow policies and strategies decided at the national level.
- **State Party**: Parties recognized by the Election Commission that have significant presence in specific states.
