# Life Processes - Class 10 Biology Notes (CBSE)

## Quick Definitions List

**Life processes** - Essential maintenance functions that keep organisms alive

**Nutrition** - Process of obtaining and utilizing food for energy and growth

**Autotroph** - Organism that makes its own food (plants, some bacteria)

**Heterotroph** - Organism that depends on others for food (animals, fungi)

**Photosynthesis** - Process by which plants make food using sunlight, CO₂, and water

**Chlorophyll** - Green pigment that captures light energy for photosynthesis

**Stomata** - Tiny pores on leaf surface for gas exchange (singular: stoma)

**Guard cells** - Cells that control opening/closing of stomata

**Enzyme** - Biological catalyst that speeds up chemical reactions

**Digestion** - Breaking down complex food into simpler, absorbable forms

**Peristalsis** - Rhythmic muscular contractions pushing food through digestive tract

**Villi** - Finger-like projections in small intestine increasing absorption area (singular: villus)

**Respiration** - Breakdown of glucose to release energy in form of ATP

**Aerobic respiration** - Respiration using oxygen; produces more ATP

**Anaerobic respiration** - Respiration without oxygen; produces less ATP

**ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate)** - Universal energy currency of cells

**Glycolysis** - First step of respiration; breakdown of glucose to pyruvate in cytoplasm

**Mitochondria** - Powerhouse of cell; site of aerobic respiration

**Alveoli** - Tiny air sacs in lungs where gas exchange occurs (singular: alveolus)

**Haemoglobin** - Red pigment in RBCs that carries oxygen

**Transportation** - Movement of materials (food, oxygen, wastes) throughout organism

**Plasma** - Liquid component of blood carrying dissolved substances

**RBC (Red Blood Corpuscle)** - Blood cell containing haemoglobin; transports oxygen

**WBC (White Blood Corpuscle)** - Blood cell involved in immunity and defense

**Platelets** - Blood cell fragments that help in blood clotting

**Artery** - Blood vessel carrying blood AWAY from heart (thick walls, high pressure)

**Vein** - Blood vessel carrying blood TO heart (thin walls, valves present)

**Capillary** - Smallest blood vessel; site of exchange between blood and tissues

**Double circulation** - Blood passes through heart twice in one complete cycle

**Atrium** - Upper chamber of heart (thin-walled, receives blood)

**Ventricle** - Lower chamber of heart (thick-walled, pumps blood)

**Xylem** - Plant tissue transporting water and minerals (unidirectional, upward)

**Phloem** - Plant tissue transporting food (bidirectional)

**Transpiration** - Loss of water vapor from aerial parts of plant

**Translocation** - Transport of soluble products of photosynthesis in phloem

**Excretion** - Removal of harmful metabolic waste products from body

**Nephron** - Functional/filtration unit of kidney

**Glomerulus** - Cluster of blood capillaries in nephron for filtration

**Bowman's capsule** - Cup-shaped structure collecting filtrate from glomerulus

**Urea** - Main nitrogenous waste in humans (from protein breakdown)

## 1. Introduction: What Makes Something Alive?

### Criteria for Life

**Observable indicators of life:**
- Movement (running dog, growing plant)
- Breathing (visible or invisible)
- Growth over time
- Molecular movement (essential but invisible)

**Critical Concept:** Visible movement alone is NOT enough
- Sleeping animals are alive (breathing continues)
- Non-green plants are alive (still grow)
- Molecular movement is NECESSARY for life
- Example: Viruses show no molecular movement outside host cells (debate: alive or not?)

### Why Molecular Movement is Essential

**Living organisms = organized structures:**
- Body → Tissues → Cells → Organelles → Molecules
- Environment constantly causes breakdown/damage
- Must continuously repair and maintain structures
- **Repair requires moving molecules around constantly**
- If organization breaks down → organism dies

### The Four Essential Life Processes

**All organisms must perform:**

1. **NUTRITION** - Obtaining food/energy from outside
2. **RESPIRATION** - Breaking down food to release energy (ATP)
3. **TRANSPORTATION** - Moving materials throughout body
4. **EXCRETION** - Removing harmful waste products

**Why needed:**
- Maintenance requires ENERGY
- Energy comes from OUTSIDE the body
- Food (carbon-based molecules) is energy source
- Must convert food to usable form (ATP)

### Size Matters: Unicellular vs Multicellular

**Unicellular organisms (bacteria, amoeba):**
- Entire surface in contact with environment
- Simple diffusion sufficient
- No specialized organs needed
- Direct exchange with surroundings

**Multicellular organisms (humans, trees):**
- Most cells NOT in direct contact with environment
- Diffusion alone INSUFFICIENT
- **Specialized organs/systems required:**
  - Digestive system (nutrition)
  - Respiratory system (gas exchange)
  - Circulatory system (transportation)
  - Excretory system (waste removal)

**Key Principle:** As body size ↑, complexity ↑, need for specialized systems ↑

## 2. Nutrition

### Definition and Importance

**Nutrition = obtaining and utilizing nutrients for:**
- Energy production (powering all life processes)
- Growth (increasing body size)
- Repair (maintaining structures)
- Synthesis of body components (proteins, etc.)

### Two Modes of Nutrition

**Based on food source:**

#### A. AUTOTROPHIC NUTRITION (Self-feeding)

**Organisms:** Green plants, some bacteria (cyanobacteria)

**Process:** Photosynthesis

**Requirements:**
- Raw materials: CO₂ + H₂O
- Energy source: Sunlight
- Catalyst: Chlorophyll (green pigment)
- Location: Chloroplasts

**Products:**
- Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) - immediate energy source
- Oxygen (O₂) - byproduct

**Photosynthesis Equation:**
```
6CO₂ + 12H₂O + Light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ + 6H₂O
     (Chlorophyll)
```

**Simplified:**
```
Carbon dioxide + Water + Sunlight → Glucose + Oxygen
                (Chlorophyll)
```

---

### Detailed Photosynthesis Mechanism

**Three Main Steps:**

**Step 1: Light Absorption**
- Chlorophyll in chloroplasts absorbs light energy
- Green light reflected (why plants look green)
- Red and blue light absorbed most effectively

**Step 2: Light Energy Conversion**
- Light energy converted to chemical energy
- Water molecules split (photolysis)
  - 2H₂O → 4H⁺ + O₂
- Oxygen released as byproduct
- Hydrogen used in next step

**Step 3: Carbon Dioxide Reduction**
- CO₂ reduced to carbohydrate (glucose)
- Uses hydrogen from step 2
- Uses chemical energy from step 2
- Produces glucose

**Important Detail Often Missed:**
- Steps need NOT occur simultaneously
- Desert plants: CO₂ uptake at night (stomata open when cool)
  - Forms intermediate compound
  - Stored until day
  - During day: light energy processes intermediate → glucose
- This prevents water loss in hot desert days

---

### Chlorophyll and Chloroplasts

**Location of Photosynthesis:**
- Occurs in chloroplasts (cell organelles)
- Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll
- Found in green parts (mainly leaves)

**Experiment to Prove Chlorophyll is Essential:**

**NCERT Activity 5.1 - Variegated Leaf Starch Test:**

**Materials:** Variegated leaf (money plant/croton), iodine, alcohol, water

**Procedure:**
1. Keep plant in dark 3 days (uses up stored starch)
2. Keep in sunlight 6 hours
3. Pluck leaf, mark green areas, trace on paper
4. Boil leaf in water (soften)
5. Boil in alcohol (removes chlorophyll)
6. Wash, dip in iodine solution
7. Observe color

**Results:**
- Green areas → Blue-black color (starch present)
- Non-green areas → Brown/yellow (no starch)

**Conclusion:** Chlorophyll ESSENTIAL for photosynthesis (only green parts made starch)

**Why This Test Works:**
- Iodine test for starch (blue-black = positive)
- Alcohol removes chlorophyll (easier to see color change)
- Boiling kills cells, stops reactions

---

### Stomata and Gas Exchange

**Structure:**
- Tiny pores on leaf surface (mainly lower epidermis)
- Surrounded by guard cells
- Guard cells control opening/closing

**Functions:**
1. **CO₂ intake** for photosynthesis
2. **O₂ release** from photosynthesis  
3. **Water loss** through transpiration

**Opening/Closing Mechanism:**

**OPEN (daytime, photosynthesis occurring):**
- Guard cells take up water
- Guard cells swell and become turgid
- Pore opens
- CO₂ enters, O₂ exits

**CLOSED (nighttime, water stress):**
- Guard cells lose water
- Guard cells become flaccid (limp)
- Pore closes
- Prevents water loss

**Critical Detail:** Why guard cells swell to open pore
- Guard cells are kidney-shaped
- Inner wall (facing pore) is thicker than outer wall
- When turgid, cells curve outward
- Creates opening between them

**Gas Exchange in Other Plant Parts:**
- Stems: Lenticels (loose cells allowing gas diffusion)
- Roots: Root hairs (large surface area for absorption)

---

### Experiment: CO₂ is Essential for Photosynthesis

**NCERT Activity 5.2:**

**Setup:**
- Two identical plants in dark 3 days
- Plant A: KOH solution nearby (absorbs CO₂)
- Plant B: No KOH (control)
- Both sealed with vaseline (airtight)
- Both in sunlight 2 hours

**Starch Test Results:**
- Plant A: NO starch (no CO₂ available)
- Plant B: Starch present (had CO₂)

**Conclusion:** CO₂ is ESSENTIAL for photosynthesis

---

### Other Raw Materials for Plants

**From Soil (absorbed by roots):**

**Water (H₂O):**
- Used in photosynthesis
- Maintains turgidity
- Medium for chemical reactions
- Transport medium

**Minerals:**

1. **Nitrogen (as NO₃⁻ or NO₂⁻)**
   - Protein synthesis
   - Chlorophyll formation
   - Nucleic acids (DNA, RNA)
   - Deficiency → yellowing leaves, stunted growth

2. **Phosphorus (as PO₄³⁻)**
   - ATP formation
   - DNA/RNA structure
   - Cell membrane component
   - Deficiency → purple leaves, poor growth

3. **Magnesium (Mg²⁺)**
   - Central atom in chlorophyll
   - Deficiency → chlorosis (yellowing)

4. **Iron (Fe²⁺)**
   - Chlorophyll synthesis
   - Deficiency → interveinal chlorosis

**Nitrogen Fixation:**
- Atmospheric N₂ cannot be used directly by plants
- Converted to usable forms by:
  - **Rhizobium** bacteria (in legume root nodules)
  - **Cyanobacteria** (blue-green algae)
  - Lightning (natural fixation)
  - Industrial fertilizers

---

### Storage of Food in Plants

**Immediate Use:**
- Glucose used for respiration
- Provides energy for cellular processes

**Storage:**
- Excess glucose converted to **STARCH**
- Starch = insoluble, doesn't affect osmotic balance
- Stored in: leaves, roots, stems, fruits, seeds
- Examples:
  - Potato: tubers (stem storage)
  - Carrot: tap root
  - Rice, wheat: seeds

**Why Starch, Not Glucose?**
- Glucose = soluble → affects osmotic pressure
- Large amounts of glucose would cause water uptake
- Cells would burst
- Starch = insoluble, safe storage form

**Parallel in Animals:**
- Animals store excess glucose as **GLYCOGEN**
- Glycogen stored in liver and muscles
- Similar branched structure to starch

## 3. Heterotrophic Nutrition

### Types of Heterotrophic Nutrition

#### 1. HOLOZOIC NUTRITION (Complete Food Intake)

**Definition:** Ingestion of complex solid/liquid food, internal digestion

**Steps:**
1. **Ingestion** - taking in food
2. **Digestion** - breaking down to simpler forms
3. **Absorption** - uptake of digested food
4. **Assimilation** - utilization by cells
5. **Egestion** - removal of undigested waste

**Organisms:** Most animals (humans, amoeba, insects)

**Example: Amoeba**
- Uses pseudopodia (temporary finger-like projections)
- Engulfs food particle
- Forms food vacuole
- Enzymes secreted into vacuole
- Digestion occurs inside vacuole
- Digested food diffuses into cytoplasm
- Undigested waste expelled from cell surface

**Example: Paramoecium**
- Fixed shape (unlike amoeba)
- Food enters at specific spot (oral groove)
- Cilia sweep food toward oral groove
- Food vacuole forms
- Moves through cell as digestion occurs
- Waste expelled at specific point (anal pore)

#### 2. SAPROPHYTIC NUTRITION (Dead Organic Matter)

**Definition:** Feeding on dead/decaying organic matter

**Mechanism:**
- Secrete digestive enzymes OUTSIDE body
- Enzymes break down complex substances
- Absorb simpler products

**Organisms:** Fungi (mushrooms, bread mold, yeast), some bacteria

**Examples:**
- Bread mold on stale bread
- Mushrooms on dead logs
- Decomposers in ecosystem

**Ecological Importance:**
- Decomposition
- Nutrient recycling
- Soil formation

#### 3. PARASITIC NUTRITION (Living on Host)

**Definition:** Obtaining food from living host organism (usually harmful)

**Types:**

**Ectoparasites** (external):
- Lice, ticks, leeches
- Attach to host surface
- Feed on blood/tissue

**Endoparasites** (internal):
- Tapeworm, roundworm
- Live inside host body
- Absorb digested food from host

**Plant Parasites:**
- **Cuscuta (Amar-bel)** - total parasite
  - No chlorophyll (yellow/orange stems)
  - Cannot photosynthesize
  - Sends haustoria (suckers) into host
  - Absorbs nutrients from host phloem
  
**Partial Parasites:**
- Some photosynthesis, but also parasitic
- Example: Mistletoe

## 4. Nutrition in Humans

### The Human Digestive System

**Alimentary Canal = long tube (8-9 meters):**
```
Mouth → Oesophagus → Stomach → Small Intestine → Large Intestine → Anus
```

**Associated Glands:**
- Salivary glands
- Liver
- Pancreas

**Key Principle:** Different regions specialized for different functions

---

### 4.1 MOUTH - Mechanical and Chemical Digestion Begins

**Structures:**

**Teeth** - mechanical breakdown
- Incisors (cutting)
- Canines (tearing)
- Premolars and Molars (grinding, chewing)
- Humans = 32 teeth (adults)

**Tongue** - manipulation and mixing
- Muscular organ
- Mixes food with saliva
- Rolls food into bolus
- Contains taste buds
- Helps in swallowing

**Salivary Glands** - chemical digestion starts
- 3 pairs: Parotid, Submandibular, Sublingual
- Secrete saliva (1-1.5 L/day)

**Saliva Composition:**
- Water (99.5%)
- **Salivary amylase** enzyme
- Mucus (lubrication)
- Antibacterial agents (lysozyme)
- Electrolytes

**Salivary Amylase Function:**
- Enzyme that breaks down starch
- Works best at pH 7 (neutral/slightly alkaline)
- **Starch → Maltose** (simpler sugar)
- Active only in mouth (inactivated in stomach acid)

**NCERT Activity 5.3 - Saliva Action on Starch:**

**Procedure:**
1. Two test tubes with 1% starch solution
2. Tube A: Add saliva
3. Tube B: No saliva (control)
4. Wait 20-30 minutes
5. Add iodine to both

**Results:**
- Tube A: No color change (starch broken down)
- Tube B: Blue-black color (starch still present)

**Conclusion:** Saliva contains enzyme that digests starch

**Formation of Bolus:**
- Chewed food mixed with saliva
- Formed into soft, slippery ball (bolus)
- Ready for swallowing

---

### 4.2 OESOPHAGUS (Food Pipe) - Transportation

**Structure:**
- Muscular tube ~25 cm long
- Connects mouth to stomach
- Passes through chest cavity
- Behind trachea (windpipe)

**Function:** Transport food from mouth to stomach

**Peristalsis - The Transport Mechanism:**

**Definition:** Rhythmic, wave-like muscular contractions

**How It Works:**
- Circular muscles behind food contract
- Pushes food forward
- Muscles ahead of food relax
- Allows food to move
- Continues along entire alimentary canal

**Critical Detail:**
- Peristalsis = involuntary (not under conscious control)
- Controlled by autonomic nervous system
- Occurs whether we're upright or upside down
- This is why you can swallow while doing headstand!

**Rings of Cartilage:**
- Present in throat region
- Keep air passage open
- Prevent collapse during swallowing

---

### 4.3 STOMACH - Protein Digestion and Acid Treatment

**Structure:**
- Large, J-shaped muscular bag
- Capacity: ~1.5-2 liters
- Located on left side of abdomen
- Highly elastic walls

**Three Regions:**
1. Cardiac region (near oesophagus)
2. Fundus (upper curved part)
3. Pyloric region (near small intestine)

**Gastric Glands (in stomach wall) secrete:**

**1. Hydrochloric Acid (HCl)**
- Makes pH ~2 (highly acidic)
- **Functions:**
  - Kills bacteria in food
  - Activates pepsinogen → pepsin
  - Creates acidic environment for pepsin
  - Breaks down food mechanically (softens)
- **Production:** Parietal cells

**2. Pepsinogen** (inactive form)
- Activated by HCl → **Pepsin** (active enzyme)
- **Why inactive form?**
  - Protects stomach from self-digestion
  - Only activated when needed

**3. Pepsin** (active enzyme)
- **Function:** Protein → Peptones + Proteoses (large fragments)
- Works best at pH 2
- Does NOT completely digest proteins

**4. Mucus**
- **Critical Protection:**
  - Lines stomach wall
  - Protects from HCl and pepsin
  - Without mucus → stomach ulcers
  - Barrier between acid and stomach tissue

**Common Problem: Acidity**
- Excess HCl production
- Burning sensation
- Causes: stress, spicy food, irregular eating
- Mucus layer insufficient
- Can lead to ulcers

**Muscular Action:**
- Strong muscles in stomach wall
- Churn and mix food with gastric juice
- Forms semi-solid paste called **CHYME**
- Mixing takes 2-4 hours

**Pyloric Sphincter:**
- Ring of muscle at stomach exit
- Controls release of chyme into small intestine
- Releases small amounts at a time
- Prevents overloading small intestine

---

### 4.4 SMALL INTESTINE - Complete Digestion and Absorption

**Structure:**
- Longest part of alimentary canal
- Length: ~6-7 meters (20-23 feet) in living person
  - (Longer in cadaver due to muscle relaxation: ~7.5-8m)
- Diameter: ~2.5 cm
- Highly coiled to fit in abdominal cavity

**Three Parts:**
1. **Duodenum** (25 cm) - receives secretions from liver and pancreas
2. **Jejunum** (2.5 m) - main site of nutrient absorption
3. **Ileum** (3.5 m) - continues absorption

**Why Length Varies in Different Animals:**

**Herbivores (plant-eaters):**
- Longer small intestine
- Cellulose difficult to digest
- Needs more time and surface area
- Example: Cow, rabbit, goat

**Carnivores (meat-eaters):**
- Shorter small intestine
- Meat easier to digest
- Less time needed
- Example: Tiger, lion

---

### Secretions into Small Intestine

#### A. BILE (from Liver)

**Liver:**
- Largest gland in body (~1.5 kg)
- Located below diaphragm, right side

**Bile Storage:**
- Stored in gall bladder (small sac)
- Released when fatty food arrives

**Bile Composition:**
- Bile salts
- Bile pigments (bilirubin - from RBC breakdown)
- Cholesterol
- NO digestive enzymes

**Bile Functions:**

**1. Makes Medium Alkaline**
- Chyme from stomach is acidic (pH ~2)
- Pancreatic enzymes need alkaline medium (pH ~8)
- Bile neutralizes acid
- Creates proper pH for enzymes

**2. Emulsification of Fats**
- **Fat arrives as large globules**
- Bile salts break into tiny droplets (emulsification)
- Like soap breaking oil drops
- **Increases surface area for enzymes**
- **Critical:** Lipase can only act on surface
- More surface = faster digestion

**Why Emulsification Needed:**
- Fats and water don't mix
- Large fat globules have small surface area
- Enzyme (lipase) is water-soluble
- Can only work at fat-water interface
- Emulsification solves this problem

---

#### B. PANCREATIC JUICE (from Pancreas)

**Pancreas:**
- Mixed gland (endocrine + exocrine)
- Located below stomach
- Pancreatic duct joins bile duct
- Both empty into duodenum

**Pancreatic Juice Contains:**

**1. Trypsin** (protein digestion)
- **Function:** Proteins/Peptones → Peptides
- Secreted as inactive **trypsinogen**
- Activated by enterokinase (from intestine)

**2. Lipase** (fat digestion)
- **Function:** Emulsified fats → Fatty acids + Glycerol
- Needs bile for fat emulsification first

**3. Amylase** (carbohydrate digestion)
- **Function:** Remaining starch → Maltose
- Continues work of salivary amylase

**4. Sodium bicarbonate**
- Makes medium alkaline
- Optimum pH for pancreatic enzymes

---

#### C. INTESTINAL JUICE (from Intestinal Wall)

**Secreted by:** Intestinal glands in small intestine wall

**Contains enzymes:**

**1. Maltase**
- **Function:** Maltose → Glucose + Glucose

**2. Sucrase**
- **Function:** Sucrose → Glucose + Fructose

**3. Lactase**
- **Function:** Lactose → Glucose + Galactose

**4. Peptidases/Erepsin**
- **Function:** Peptides → Amino acids

**5. Lipase**
- **Function:** Remaining fats → Fatty acids + Glycerol

---

### Complete Digestion Summary Table

| Food Type | Mouth | Stomach | Small Intestine | Final Product |
|-----------|-------|---------|-----------------|---------------|
| **Carbohydrates** | Starch → Maltose (salivary amylase) | No change | Maltose → Glucose (maltase) | **Glucose** |
| **Proteins** | No change | Protein → Peptones (pepsin) | Peptones → Peptides → Amino acids (trypsin, peptidases) | **Amino acids** |
| **Fats** | No change | No change | Emulsification (bile), Fats → Fatty acids + Glycerol (lipase) | **Fatty acids + Glycerol** |

---

### Absorption in Small Intestine

**The Challenge:**
- Huge amount of digested food
- Must be absorbed efficiently
- Needs large surface area

**Solution: VILLI**

**Structure of Villi:**
- Finger-like projections on inner wall
- Millions of villi
- Each villus 0.5-1.5 mm long
- Further projections = microvilli
- Creates "brush border"

**Surface Area Increase:**
- Folds → Villi → Microvilli
- Total surface area ~200-250 m² (size of tennis court!)
- From tube of only 2.5 cm diameter

**Internal Structure of Each Villus:**
- Single layer of epithelial cells (easy absorption)
- **Blood capillaries** inside
  - Absorb: glucose, amino acids, vitamins, minerals
  - Transport to liver via hepatic portal vein
  
- **Lacteal** (lymphatic vessel) inside
  - Absorbs: fatty acids and glycerol
  - Reforms into fats inside lacteal
  - Transport via lymphatic system
  - Eventually enters bloodstream

**Why Fats Go to Lacteal:**
- Fats are large molecules
- Cannot enter blood capillaries directly
- Enter lymph first
- Lymph eventually drains into blood

**Absorption Timeline:**
- Most absorption in jejunum
- Continues in ileum
- Takes 3-5 hours

---

### 4.5 LARGE INTESTINE - Water Absorption and Waste Formation

**Structure:**
- ~1.5 meters long
- Wider than small intestine (~6 cm diameter)
- Three parts: Caecum, Colon, Rectum

**Caecum:**
- Pouch-like structure
- Junction of small and large intestine
- Contains **appendix** (vestigial organ)

**Colon:**
- Ascending, transverse, descending, sigmoid
- Main site of water absorption

**Functions:**

**1. Water Absorption**
- Remaining water absorbed back
- Undigested material becomes semi-solid
- ~90% water reabsorbed
- Forms **faeces**

**2. Bacterial Action**
- Contains helpful bacteria
- Produce vitamins (K, B12)
- Help digest some remaining materials
- Break down cellulose (partially)

**3. Temporary Storage**
- Faeces stored in rectum
- Until elimination

**4. Egestion**
- Removal through anus
- Controlled by anal sphincter
- Voluntary control (can be delayed)

**Composition of Faeces:**
- Undigested food (cellulose, fiber)
- Dead bacteria
- Dead cells from intestinal lining
- Bile pigments (gives brown color)
- Small amount of water

## 5. Respiration

### Definition and Purpose

**Respiration = Breakdown of glucose to release energy**

**NOT the same as breathing:**
- **Breathing** = physical process of inhaling/exhaling
- **Respiration** = chemical process in cells (cellular respiration)

**Purpose:**
- Release energy stored in glucose
- Energy captured in ATP molecules
- ATP = universal energy currency

**Why Organisms Need Energy:**
- Movement
- Growth
- Maintaining body temperature
- Active transport
- Synthesis of molecules
- All cellular work

---

### Types of Respiration

#### 1. AEROBIC RESPIRATION (With Oxygen)

**Location:** Mitochondria (powerhouse of cell)

**Overall Equation:**
```
C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Energy (38 ATP)
Glucose  Oxygen  Carbon  Water   Energy
                dioxide
```

**Three Stages:**

**Stage 1: Glycolysis** (in cytoplasm)
- Glucose (6C) → 2 Pyruvate (3C each)
- Produces: 2 ATP + 2 NADH
- Does NOT require oxygen
- Common to both aerobic and anaerobic

**Stage 2: Krebs Cycle** (in mitochondria)
- Pyruvate completely oxidized
- Produces: 2 ATP + CO₂ + H₂O
- Requires oxygen

**Stage 3: Electron Transport Chain** (in mitochondria)
- Most ATP generated here
- Produces: 34 ATP
- Requires oxygen

**Total Yield: ~38 ATP molecules per glucose**

**Products:**
- CO₂ (removed via lungs)
- H₂O (used by cells or excreted)
- Energy (stored in ATP)

---

#### 2. ANAEROBIC RESPIRATION (Without Oxygen)

**When does it occur:**
- When oxygen is absent/insufficient
- In organisms living in oxygen-poor environments
- In muscles during intense exercise

**Two Types:**

**A. In Yeast (Fermentation)**

**Equation:**
```
C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂ + Energy (2 ATP)
Glucose   Ethanol   Carbon   Energy
                    dioxide
```

**Process:**
1. Glycolysis: Glucose → 2 Pyruvate (2 ATP produced)
2. Pyruvate → Ethanol + CO₂

**Applications:**
- **Brewing** - alcohol production
- **Baking** - CO₂ makes bread rise

**NCERT Activity 5.5 - Demonstrating Fermentation:**

**Procedure:**
1. Mix fruit juice/sugar solution + yeast
2. Put in test tube with one-holed cork
3. Attach bent glass tube through cork
4. Other end in lime water
5. Observe

**Results:**
- Lime water turns milky (CO₂ produced)
- Sweet smell (ethanol produced)

**Conclusion:** Yeast produces CO₂ and ethanol (anaerobic respiration)

**B. In Muscle Cells (Lactic Acid Fermentation)**

**Equation:**
```
C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₃H₆O₃ + Energy (2 ATP)
Glucose   Lactic acid  Energy
```

**When:**
- During sudden/intense activity
- Oxygen supply insufficient
- Muscles need quick energy

**Process:**
1. Glycolysis: Glucose → 2 Pyruvate
2. Pyruvate → Lactic acid

**Effects:**
- Lactic acid builds up in muscles
- Causes muscle cramps/fatigue
- Painful sensation
- Temporary (lactic acid removed when oxygen available)

**After Exercise:**
- Deep breathing continues
- Extra oxygen breaks down lactic acid
- Lactic acid → CO₂ + H₂O
- This is why we pant after sprinting

---

### Comparison: Aerobic vs Anaerobic

| Feature | Aerobic | Anaerobic |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| Oxygen needed | Yes | No |
| Location | Mitochondria (+ cytoplasm for glycolysis) | Cytoplasm only |
| ATP yield | ~38 ATP | 2 ATP |
| Efficiency | High (complete oxidation) | Low (incomplete oxidation) |
| End products | CO₂ + H₂O | Ethanol + CO₂ (yeast) OR Lactic acid (muscles) |
| Organisms | Most plants, animals, many microbes | Yeast, some bacteria, muscle cells |
| Energy release | Complete (all energy extracted) | Incomplete (some energy remains in end products) |

**Why Aerobic is More Efficient:**
- Complete breakdown of glucose
- All chemical bonds broken
- Maximum energy extracted
- Anaerobic leaves energy in ethanol/lactic acid

---

### ATP - The Energy Currency

**What is ATP?**
- Adenosine Triphosphate
- Three phosphate groups attached to adenosine
- Universal energy molecule in all living cells

**Structure:**
- Adenine (nitrogenous base)
- Ribose (sugar)
- Three phosphate groups (high-energy bonds)

**How ATP Provides Energy:**

**Energy Release:**
```
ATP + H₂O → ADP + Pi + Energy (30.5 kJ/mol)
(Triphosphate)  (Diphosphate) (Phosphate)
```

**When terminal phosphate bond breaks:**
- Energy released
- Used to drive cellular reactions
- ADP + Pi formed

**Energy Storage:**
```
ADP + Pi + Energy → ATP
(from respiration)
```

**Why ATP, Not Direct Glucose?**
- ATP releases energy in small, usable amounts
- Glucose combustion releases too much energy at once
- Would damage cells
- ATP = controlled energy packets

**ATP Uses (Like a Battery):**
- Muscle contraction
- Active transport
- Protein synthesis
- Nerve impulse transmission
- Cell division
- Bioluminescence
- ANY energy-requiring process

**Key Concept:** Respiration produces ATP, ATP powers all life processes

## 6. Gas Exchange in Organisms

### Gas Exchange in Plants

**Gases Involved:**
- **CO₂** - needed for photosynthesis, produced in respiration
- **O₂** - produced in photosynthesis, needed for respiration

**Route of Gas Exchange:**

**Main Entry/Exit Point: STOMATA**
- Present on leaves (mainly lower surface)
- Also on green stems
- Open during day (photosynthesis)
- May close at night (water conservation)

**Other Routes:**
- **Lenticels** on woody stems
- Small openings in bark
- Allow gas exchange in non-green parts

- **Root hairs** 
- Oxygen from air spaces in soil
- Required for root cell respiration

**Internal Gas Transport:**

**Large Intercellular Spaces:**
- Between cells in leaf mesophyll
- Between cells in stems and roots
- All cells in contact with air
- Diffusion sufficient for gas movement

**Why Plants Don't Need Respiratory System:**
1. Low energy requirements (no movement, large proportion of dead cells)
2. Each part gets oxygen directly from nearby stomata/lenticels
3. Large surface area to volume ratio
4. Diffusion adequate for their needs

**Day vs Night Gas Exchange:**

**During Day:**
- Photosynthesis > Respiration (rate-wise)
- CO₂ from respiration used in photosynthesis
- Net O₂ release
- Net CO₂ uptake

**During Night:**
- No photosynthesis
- Only respiration occurs
- Net O₂ uptake
- Net CO₂ release

**Critical Detail Often Tested:**
- Plants respire 24/7 (day AND night)
- Photosynthesis only during day
- Day: photosynthesis masks respiration

### Gas Exchange in Animals

**Challenge for Animals:**
- Higher energy needs than plants
- Active movement requires more O₂
- Need specialized respiratory organs
- Must maximize gas exchange surface area

**Different Animals, Different Solutions:**

---

#### 1. AQUATIC ANIMALS - Gills

**Fish Respiratory System:**

**Gills:**
- Located behind head
- Covered by operculum (bony flap)
- Highly vascularized (many blood vessels)
- Large surface area due to gill filaments

**How Fish Breathe:**
1. Open mouth, water enters
2. Close mouth, water forced over gills
3. Dissolved O₂ from water → blood
4. CO₂ from blood → water
5. Water exits through operculum

**NCERT Activity 5.6 - Observing Fish:**

**Observation:**
- Fish opens/closes mouth rhythmically
- Operculum (gill cover) opens/closes

  - Mouth open → operculum closed

**Breathing Rate:**
- Fish: 40-80 times per minute
- Humans: 12-20 times per minute

**Why Fish Breathe Faster:**
- Dissolved O₂ in water is LOW (~5-8 ppm)
- Air has much more O₂ (~21%)
- Fish must process more water to get sufficient O₂

#### 2. TERRESTRIAL ANIMALS - Lungs

**Advantages of Living on Land:**
- More O₂ available in air
- Don't need to extract dissolved O₂

**Challenge:**
- Respiratory surface must be moist

- Need mechanism to move air in/out

### Human Respiratory System

**Components:**
- Nostrils
- Nasal cavity
- Pharynx (throat)
- Larynx (voice box)
- Trachea (windpipe)
- Bronchi (2 - one to each lung)
- Bronchioles (smaller tubes)
- Alveoli (air sacs)

**Pathway of Air:**
```
Nostrils → Nasal cavity → Pharynx → Larynx → Trachea → Bronchi → Bronchioles → Alveoli
```

---

#### NOSTRILS AND NASAL CAVITY

**Functions:**

**1. Filtration**
- Fine hairs filter dust, large particles
- First line of defense

**2. Warming**
- Nasal cavity has rich blood supply
- Warms cold air to body temperature
- Prevents damage to lungs

**3. Moistening**
- Mucus in nasal lining
- Adds moisture to dry air
- Prevents alveolar drying

**4. Detection**
- Olfactory receptors detect smells

---

#### PHARYNX AND LARYNX

**Pharynx (Throat):**
- Common passage for air and food
- Splits into:
  - Trachea (front) - for air
  - Oesophagus (back) - for food

**Larynx (Voice Box):**
- Contains vocal cords
- Produces sound
- Closed by epiglottis during swallowing

---

#### TRACHEA (Windpipe)

**Structure:**
- ~12 cm long tube
- C-shaped cartilage rings

**Why C-shaped Cartilage:**
- Keeps airway open always
- Prevents collapse
- C-shape allows oesophagus to expand during swallowing (back side)
- Flexible yet supportive

**Lining:**
- Mucus-secreting cells
- Cilia (hair-like structures)
- Cilia sweep mucus upward
- Traps and removes dust, microbes

**Effect of Smoking (Critical Health Info):**
- Smoke destroys cilia
- Mucus and pollutants accumulate
- Increased risk of:
  - Infections
  - Chronic cough
  - Lung cancer
  - COPD (Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease)

---

#### BRONCHI AND BRONCHIOLES

**Bronchi:**
- Trachea divides into two bronchi
- One enters each lung
- Right bronchus wider and shorter
- Left bronchus narrower and longer

**Bronchioles:**
- Bronchi divide into smaller tubes
- Progressive branching (like tree branches)
- End in clusters of alveoli
- Create "respiratory tree"

---

#### ALVEOLI - Site of Gas Exchange

**Structure:**
- Balloon-like thin-walled sacs
- Clustered like grapes
- Single-cell thick wall
- Surrounded by blood capillaries

**Numbers:**
- ~300-500 million alveoli in two lungs
- Total surface area ~70-80 m² (size of badminton court!)

**Why Such Large Surface Area?**
- Maximize gas exchange
- Meet high O₂ demands of body
- Efficient CO₂ removal

**Adaptations for Gas Exchange:**

**1. Thin Walls**
- One cell thick
- Reduces diffusion distance
- Quick gas exchange

**2. Moist Surface**
- Gases dissolve before diffusing
- Essential for diffusion

**3. Rich Blood Supply**
- Dense network of capillaries
- Continuous blood flow
- Maintains concentration gradient

**4. Large Surface Area**
- Millions of alveoli
- Maximize contact with blood

---

### Mechanism of Breathing

**Two Phases:**

#### INHALATION (Breathing In)

**What Happens:**
1. **Diaphragm contracts** → flattens, moves down
2. **Intercostal muscles contract** → ribs lift up and out
3. **Chest cavity expands** → volume increases
4. **Pressure inside lungs decreases** (below atmospheric)
5. **Air rushes into lungs** (high to low pressure)
6. **Alveoli inflate**

#### EXHALATION (Breathing Out)

**What Happens:**
1. **Diaphragm relaxes** → dome-shaped, moves up
2. **Intercostal muscles relax** → ribs move down and in
3. **Chest cavity reduces** → volume decreases
4. **Pressure inside lungs increases** (above atmospheric)
5. **Air pushed out of lungs**
6. **Alveoli deflate**

**Important Note:**
- Lungs are NEVER completely empty
- **Residual volume** always present (~1200 mL)
- Ensures continuous gas exchange
- Prevents alveolar collapse

**Breathing Rate:**
- Normal adult: 12-20 breaths/minute
- Children: 20-30 breaths/minute
- Increases during exercise

---

### Gas Exchange at Alveoli

**The Exchange Process:**

**Oxygen (O₂):**
- High concentration in alveolar air
- Low concentration in blood (deoxygenated blood from body)
- O₂ diffuses: Alveoli → Blood

**Carbon Dioxide (CO₂):**
- High concentration in blood (from body tissues)
- Low concentration in alveolar air
- CO₂ diffuses: Blood → Alveoli

**Diffusion driven by concentration gradient (no energy needed)**

---

### Transport of Gases in Blood

#### OXYGEN TRANSPORT

**Problem:** O₂ is poorly soluble in water/plasma

**Solution: HAEMOGLOBIN**

**Haemoglobin:**
- Red pigment in RBCs
- Contains iron (Fe²⁺)
- Each haemoglobin can bind 4 O₂ molecules

**In Lungs (High O₂):**
```
Haemoglobin + O₂ → Oxyhaemoglobin
(Hb)                  (HbO₂)
```
- Bright red color

**In Tissues (Low O₂):**
```
Oxyhaemoglobin → Haemoglobin + O₂
(HbO₂)           (Hb)
```
- O₂ released to cells
- Dark red color

**Affinity:**
- High affinity for O₂ in lungs
- Releases O₂ easily in tissues

**Why Haemoglobin is Essential:**
- 1 L blood can carry 200 mL O₂ (with Hb)
- Without Hb: only 3 mL O₂
- ~65 times more efficient!

**NCERT Activity 5.7 - Haemoglobin Levels:**

**Normal Ranges:**
- **Men:** 14-18 g/dL
- **Women:** 12-16 g/dL
- **Children:** 11-13 g/dL

**Why Difference?**
- Men: larger body size, more muscle mass
- Women: menstrual blood loss
- Similar in animals

**Low Haemoglobin = Anaemia:**
- Fatigue, weakness
- Shortness of breath
- Pale skin
- Caused by: iron deficiency, blood loss, diseases

---

#### CARBON DIOXIDE TRANSPORT

**CO₂ More Soluble than O₂**

**Three Forms:**

**1. Dissolved in Plasma (7%)**
- CO₂ dissolves directly
- Small amount

**2. As Bicarbonate Ions (70%)**
- Main transport form
```
CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃ ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻
      (Carbonic  (Bicarbonate)
       acid)
```
- Reversible reaction
- In tissues: forward (forms HCO₃⁻)
- In lungs: reverse (releases CO₂)

**3. Bound to Haemoglobin (23%)**
- Carbaminohaemoglobin
- Different binding site than O₂

## 8. Human Circulatory System

### Components

**Three Main Parts:**
1. **Blood** - transport medium
2. **Heart** - pumping organ
3. **Blood vessels** - transport channels

---

### 8.1 BLOOD

**Type:** Fluid connective tissue

**Composition:**

#### PLASMA (55% of blood volume)

**Liquid Component:**
- 90% water
- 10% dissolved substances

**Dissolved Substances:**
- **Proteins:** Albumin, globulin, fibrinogen
- **Nutrients:** Glucose, amino acids, fatty acids
- **Waste products:** Urea, uric acid, creatinine
- **Hormones**
- **Enzymes**
- **Gases:** Some CO₂, little O₂
- **Minerals:** Na⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺, Cl⁻

**Functions:**
- Transport nutrients, wastes, hormones
- Maintains blood pressure
- Temperature regulation
- pH buffering

---

#### RED BLOOD CELLS (RBCs) - Erythrocytes (42-45%)

**Structure:**
- Biconcave disc shape (dumbbell shape)
- No nucleus in mature RBCs (mammals)
- Increases space for haemoglobin
- Flexible - can squeeze through narrow capillaries

**Number:**
- Men: 5-5.5 million per mm³
- Women: 4.5-5 million per mm³

**Life Span:**
- ~120 days
- Destroyed in spleen
- Constantly produced in bone marrow

**Function:**
- Transport O₂ via haemoglobin
- Some CO₂ transport

**Color:**
- Red due to haemoglobin

---

#### WHITE BLOOD CELLS (WBCs) - Leukocytes (1%)

**Structure:**
- Irregular shape
- Contain nucleus
- Larger than RBCs
- Can change shape (amoeboid movement)

**Number:**
- 6,000-8,000 per mm³
- Increases during infection

**Types:**
1. **Neutrophils** - phagocytosis (engulf bacteria)
2. **Lymphocytes** - produce antibodies
3. **Monocytes** - phagocytosis
4. **Eosinophils** - allergic reactions, parasites
5. **Basophils** - inflammatory response

**Function:**
- Immunity and defense
- Fight infections
- Produce antibodies
- Remove dead cells

**Life Span:**
- Few days to few years (depends on type)

---

#### PLATELETS - Thrombocytes (<1%)

**Structure:**
- Cell fragments (not complete cells)
- No nucleus
- Irregular shape

**Number:**
- 150,000-400,000 per mm³

**Function:**
- Blood clotting
- Plug leaks in blood vessels
- Prevent blood loss

**Mechanism:**
1. Injury → blood vessel damaged
2. Platelets stick to damaged area
3. Release clotting factors
4. Fibrinogen → Fibrin (forms mesh)
5. RBCs trapped in mesh
6. Clot formed

**Low Platelet Count:**
- Excessive bleeding
- Bruising easily
- Difficulty clotting

### 8.2 THE HEART - The Pump

**Location:**
- Chest cavity
- Between two lungs
- Slightly left of center
- Protected by rib cage

**Size:**
- Fist-sized (~250-350 g)
- ~12 cm long

**Wall:**
- Made of cardiac muscle (myocardium)
- Involuntary muscle
- Never fatigues
- Contracts rhythmically throughout life

---

### Structure of Heart

**Four Chambers:**

**Upper Chambers: ATRIA (singular: atrium)**
- **Right Atrium** - receives deoxygenated blood from body
- **Left Atrium** - receives oxygenated blood from lungs
- Thin-walled (low pressure)
- Receiving chambers

**Lower Chambers: VENTRICLES**
- **Right Ventricle** - pumps blood to lungs
- **Left Ventricle** - pumps blood to body
- Thick-walled (high pressure needed)
- Pumping chambers

**Wall Thickness:**
- Left ventricle has THICKEST wall
- Must pump blood to entire body (high resistance)
- Right ventricle has thinner wall
- Only pumps to lungs (low resistance)

---

### Valves of Heart

**Purpose:** Prevent backflow of blood

**Types:**

**1. Atrioventricular (AV) Valves** (between atria and ventricles)
- **Tricuspid valve** - between right atrium and right ventricle (3 flaps)
- **Bicuspid/Mitral valve** - between left atrium and left ventricle (2 flaps)

**2. Semilunar Valves** (between ventricles and arteries)
- **Pulmonary valve** - between right ventricle and pulmonary artery
- **Aortic valve** - between left ventricle and aorta

**How Valves Work:**
- Open when pressure behind > pressure ahead
- Close when pressure ahead > pressure behind
- Prevent backward flow
- Produce heart sounds ("lub-dub")

**Separation of Chambers:**
- **Septum** divides left and right sides
- Prevents mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood
- Essential for efficient O₂ delivery

---

### Heart Function - Cardiac Cycle

**One cardiac cycle = one heartbeat (~0.8 seconds at rest)**

**Three Phases:**

**1. ATRIAL SYSTOLE (Atrial Contraction) - 0.1 sec**
- Atria contract
- AV valves open
- Blood pushed into ventricles
- Ventricles relaxed (diastole)
- Ventricles fill ~70%

**2. VENTRICULAR SYSTOLE (Ventricular Contraction) - 0.3 sec**
- Ventricles contract
- AV valves close ("lub" sound)
- Semilunar valves open
- Blood pumped out:
  - Right ventricle → Pulmonary artery → Lungs
  - Left ventricle → Aorta → Body

**3. JOINT DIASTOLE (Complete Relaxation) - 0.4 sec**
- All chambers relax
- Semilunar valves close ("dub" sound)
- Blood fills atria from veins
- Cycle repeats

**Heart Rate:**
- Normal adult: 60-100 beats per minute
- Children: 80-120 bpm
- Athletes: may be 40-60 bpm (more efficient heart)

**Cardiac Output:**
- Volume of blood pumped per minute
- ~5 liters/minute at rest
- Can increase to 25-30 L/min during exercise

---

### Double Circulation - Unique to Mammals and Birds

**Definition:** Blood passes through heart TWICE in one complete cycle

**Why Double Circulation?**
- Complete separation of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood
- Higher efficiency
- Maintains high blood pressure
- Meets high energy demands
- Essential for maintaining body temperature (warm-blooded animals)

**Two Circuits:**

#### CIRCUIT 1: PULMONARY CIRCULATION (Heart → Lungs → Heart)

**Pathway:**
```
Right Atrium → Right Ventricle → Pulmonary Artery → Lungs 
→ Pulmonary Veins → Left Atrium
```

**Function:**
- Oxygenation of blood
- Removal of CO₂
- Low pressure circulation

**Note:** Pulmonary artery carries DEOXYGENATED blood (exception!)

---

#### CIRCUIT 2: SYSTEMIC CIRCULATION (Heart → Body → Heart)

**Pathway:**
```
Left Atrium → Left Ventricle → Aorta → Body tissues 
→ Vena Cava → Right Atrium
```

**Function:**
- Delivers O₂ and nutrients to body
- Collects CO₂ and wastes
- High pressure circulation

**Note:** Pulmonary vein carries OXYGENATED blood (exception!)

---

### Complete Blood Flow Pathway

```
Body tissues (deoxygenated blood)
    ↓
Superior/Inferior Vena Cava
    ↓
RIGHT ATRIUM
    ↓ (tricuspid valve)
RIGHT VENTRICLE
    ↓ (pulmonary valve)
Pulmonary Artery
    ↓
LUNGS (gas exchange: CO₂ out, O₂ in)
    ↓
Pulmonary Veins
    ↓
LEFT ATRIUM
    ↓ (bicuspid/mitral valve)
LEFT VENTRICLE
    ↓ (aortic valve)
Aorta
    ↓
Body tissues (oxygenated blood)
```

**Mnemonic:** "Right receives Rubbish, Left launches Life-giving blood"

---

### Comparison with Other Animals

| Animal Group | Heart Chambers | Circulation | Blood Mixing |
|-------------|----------------|-------------|--------------|
| **Fish** | 2 (1 atrium, 1 ventricle) | Single | No |
| **Amphibians** | 3 (2 atria, 1 ventricle) | Double | Yes (some) |
| **Reptiles** | 3 (2 atria, 1 ventricle with partial septum) | Double | Yes (little) |
| **Crocodile** | 4 (2 atria, 2 ventricles) | Double | No |
| **Birds & Mammals** | 4 (2 atria, 2 ventricles) | Double | No |

**Why Differences?**

**Fish:**
- Single circulation
- Blood goes: Heart → Gills → Body → Heart
- Lower energy needs (cold-blooded, aquatic)

**Amphibians/Most Reptiles:**
- Some mixing tolerable
- Cold-blooded (don't maintain body temperature)
- Lower metabolic rate

**Birds/Mammals:**
- No mixing essential
- Warm-blooded (high metabolic rate)
- Need maximum O₂ delivery
- Maintain constant body temperature

### 8.3 BLOOD VESSELS - The Tubes

**Three Types:** Arteries, Veins, Capillaries

---

#### ARTERIES

**Definition:** Vessels carrying blood AWAY from heart

**Structure:**
- Thick, muscular, elastic walls
- Narrow lumen (central cavity)
- No valves (except at heart exit)

**Why Thick Walls?**
- Blood under HIGH pressure from heart
- Must withstand pressure without bursting
- Elastic tissue allows expansion during systole

**Main Arteries:**
- **Aorta** - largest artery, from left ventricle
- **Pulmonary artery** - from right ventricle to lungs
- Branch into smaller arteries (arterioles)

**Blood Pressure:**
- Highest in arteries
- Systolic: ~120 mm Hg (during ventricular contraction)
- Diastolic: ~80 mm Hg (during relaxation)
- Written as 120/80

**Pulse:**
- Rhythmic expansion of artery with each heartbeat
- Can be felt where artery is close to surface
- Common sites: wrist, neck, temple

---

#### VEINS

**Definition:** Vessels carrying blood TOWARD heart

**Structure:**
- Thin walls (less muscle and elastic tissue)
- Wide lumen
- **Valves present** at intervals

**Why Thin Walls?**
- Blood under LOW pressure
- Don't need thick walls
- Pressure already dissipated in capillaries

**Why Valves Needed?**
- Low pressure may not be enough to return blood to heart
- Valves prevent backflow
- Ensure one-way flow toward heart
- Especially important in legs (against gravity)

**How Blood Returns to Heart (against low pressure):**
1. **Muscle contractions** - squeeze veins, push blood
2. **Breathing movements** - create pressure changes
3. **Valves** - prevent backflow

**Main Veins:**
- **Vena Cava** (superior and inferior) - largest veins, enter right atrium
- **Pulmonary veins** - from lungs to left atrium
- Join from smaller veins (venules)

---

#### CAPILLARIES

**Definition:** Smallest blood vessels, site of exchange

**Structure:**
- ONE cell thick wall
- Extremely narrow (~8 micrometers)
- RBCs pass single file
- No valves

**Why So Thin?**
- Allow easy diffusion
- Reduce distance for exchange
- Nutrients, O₂, wastes can pass through

**Network:**
- Connect arterioles to venules
- Present in ALL tissues
- Form dense networks (capillary beds)

**Exchange Process:**
1. Blood enters from arteriole (high pressure)
2. Pressure forces plasma out through capillary walls
3. Forms tissue fluid (bathes cells)
4. Exchange: O₂, nutrients → cells; CO₂, wastes → blood
5. Most tissue fluid returns to capillaries
6. Some enters lymphatic vessels (becomes lymph)
7. Blood returns to venule

**Capillary Density:**
- Highest in metabolically active tissues
- High in: muscles, brain, liver, kidneys
- Low in: cartilage, epithelium

---

### Blood Pressure (Extended Concept)

**Definition:** Force exerted by blood on vessel walls

**Measurement:**
- Sphygmomanometer
- Expressed as systolic/diastolic
- Normal: 120/80 mm Hg

**Systolic Pressure (120):**
- Pressure during ventricular contraction
- When blood is ejected from heart
- Higher value

**Diastolic Pressure (80):**
- Pressure during ventricular relaxation
- When heart refills
- Lower value

**Factors Affecting Blood Pressure:**
- Heart rate
- Blood volume
- Vessel elasticity
- Vessel diameter
- Viscosity of blood

**Hypertension (High BP):**
- Sustained high blood pressure
- Can lead to:
  - Heart disease
  - Stroke
  - Kidney damage
  - Artery damage

**Hypotension (Low BP):**
- Low blood pressure
- Causes: dehydration, blood loss, heart problems
- Symptoms: dizziness, fainting, fatigue

---

### Lymphatic System - The Drainage System

**What is Lymph?**
- Clear, watery fluid
- Similar to plasma but:
  - Less protein
  - Contains WBCs (lymphocytes)
  - No RBCs

**How Lymph Forms:**
- Blood pressure forces some plasma out of capillaries
- Becomes tissue fluid (bathes cells)
- ~90% returns to blood capillaries
- ~10% enters lymphatic capillaries → becomes lymph

**Lymphatic System Components:**
- Lymphatic capillaries (blind-ended tubes)
- Lymphatic vessels (have valves)
- Lymph nodes (filter lymph, produce lymphocytes)
- Eventually drains into veins

**Functions:**

**1. Fluid Balance**
- Returns excess tissue fluid to bloodstream
- Prevents edema (swelling)

**2. Fat Absorption**
- Lacteals (lymphatic capillaries in intestine)
- Absorb fats and fat-soluble vitamins
- Transport to bloodstream

**3. Immunity**
- Lymph nodes filter lymph
- Remove bacteria, foreign particles
- Produce lymphocytes
- Part of immune system

## 9. Transportation in Plants

### Why Plants Need Transport System

**Challenges:**
- Tall trees (~100m high)
- Roots deep in soil
- Leaves high in air
- Long distances between organs

**What Needs Transport:**

**Upward (Roots → Leaves):**
- Water
- Minerals

**Downward (Leaves → Other parts):**
- Food (sugars)
- Can also be upward

---

### Two Separate Systems

#### XYLEM - Water and Mineral Transport

**Structure:**
- Composed of dead cells
- Long, hollow, tube-like
- Types: Vessels, Tracheids
- Thick, lignified walls
- Continuous column from root to leaf

**Direction:** Unidirectional (upward only)

**What Transported:**
- Water
- Dissolved minerals (N, P, K, etc.)

---

#### PHLOEM - Food Transport

**Structure:**
- Composed of living cells
- Types: Sieve tubes (with sieve plates), Companion cells
- Sieve plates have pores
- Companion cells provide energy

**Direction:** Bidirectional (up and down)

**What Transported:**
- Sucrose (main form of sugar)
- Amino acids
- Hormones
- Other organic compounds

---

### 9.1 Mechanism of Water Transport in Xylem

**Two Forces Work Together:**

#### 1. ROOT PRESSURE (Push from Below)

**Mechanism:**
1. Root cells actively absorb minerals from soil (using ATP)
2. Mineral concentration in root cells increases
3. Water enters root cells by osmosis
4. Creates pressure in xylem
5. Pushes water up

**Characteristics:**
- Active process (requires energy)
- Can push water up to ~1-2 meters
- NOT sufficient for tall trees
- More effective at night
- When there's no transpiration

**Evidence:**
- Guttation (water droplets on leaf edges in morning)
- Bleeding of cut stems

---

#### 2. TRANSPIRATION PULL (Pull from Above)

**Transpiration:** Loss of water vapor from aerial parts (mainly leaves through stomata)

**Mechanism:**
1. Water evaporates from leaf mesophyll cells
2. Creates suction/tension
3. Pulls water from xylem in leaf veins
4. Due to cohesion (water molecules stick together), entire column pulled up
5. More water pulled from roots
6. Continuous column maintained

**Cohesion-Tension Theory:**
- Water molecules have strong cohesion (stick together)
- Form continuous column in xylem
- Transpiration creates tension at top
- Entire column pulled up like a chain
- Adhesion to xylem walls prevents column breaking

**Characteristics:**
- Passive process (no ATP needed)
- Main force in tall trees
- Can pull water to 100m+ heights
- Occurs during day (stomata open)

**NCERT Activity 5.8 - Demonstrating Transpiration:**

**Setup:**
- Pot 1: Plant, soil covered with plastic
- Pot 2: Stick (same height), soil covered
- Both covered with plastic sheets
- Both in bright sunlight

**Result:**
- Pot 1: Water droplets on inside of plastic (transpiration)
- Pot 2: No water droplets

**Conclusion:** Plant loses water through transpiration

**Factors Affecting Transpiration:**
- Light (increases transpiration)
- Temperature (higher → more transpiration)
- Humidity (lower → more transpiration)
- Wind (increases transpiration)
- Stomatal opening

---

### 9.2 Mechanism of Food Transport in Phloem

**Process:** Translocation

**Mechanism: Pressure Flow Hypothesis**

**At Source (Leaves - where sugar is made):**
1. Glucose produced by photosynthesis
2. Converted to sucrose
3. Actively loaded into phloem sieve tubes (using ATP)
4. Increases solute concentration in phloem
5. Water enters phloem by osmosis
6. Creates high pressure

**At Sink (Roots, Fruits, Storage Organs - where sugar is used/stored):**
1. Sucrose actively unloaded from phloem (using ATP)
2. Used or stored
3. Decreases solute concentration
4. Water leaves phloem by osmosis
5. Creates low pressure

**Result:**
- Pressure gradient created
- High pressure at source → Low pressure at sink
- Mass flow of solution from source to sink
- Direction can reverse (e.g., spring: stored sugar in roots → growing buds)

**Key Differences from Xylem:**

| Feature | Xylem | Phloem |
|---------|-------|--------|
| Cells | Dead | Living |
| Direction | Unidirectional (up) | Bidirectional |
| Materials | Water, minerals | Organic food |
| Energy | No ATP (mostly passive) | Requires ATP |
| Mechanism | Transpiration pull | Pressure flow |

## 10. Excretion

### Definition and Need

**Excretion:** Removal of metabolic waste products from body

**Why Excretion Necessary:**
- Metabolic activities produce waste products
- Many are toxic/harmful if accumulated
- Must be removed to maintain homeostasis

**Main Metabolic Wastes:**

**1. Nitrogenous Wastes** (from protein/amino acid breakdown)
- **Ammonia** (NH₃) - very toxic, very soluble
- **Urea** (CO(NH₂)₂) - less toxic, less soluble
- **Uric acid** (C₅H₄N₄O₃) - least toxic, least soluble

**2. CO₂** (from respiration)
- Removed via lungs

**3. Excess water and salts**
- Can disturb osmotic balance

**4. Bile pigments** (from haemoglobin breakdown)
- Removed via liver

---

### Types of Nitrogenous Waste (Based on Organism)

| Organism Type | Waste | Reason |
|--------------|-------|---------|
| **Aquatic animals** (fish) | Ammonia | Plenty of water to dilute and remove |
| **Terrestrial animals** (mammals) | Urea | Less toxic, needs less water |
| **Birds, reptiles** | Uric acid | Least water needed, can be solid |

## 11. Excretion in Humans

### Human Excretory System Components

**Organs:**
1. **Kidneys** (2) - main excretory organs
2. **Ureters** (2) - transport urine to bladder
3. **Urinary Bladder** (1) - stores urine
4. **Urethra** (1) - releases urine out of body

---

### 11.1 KIDNEYS - The Filters

**Location:**
- Abdominal cavity
- One on each side of vertebral column
- Between last thoracic and third lumbar vertebra
- Right kidney slightly lower (due to liver)

**Structure:**
- Bean-shaped
- ~10-12 cm long
- ~5-6 cm wide
- ~3 cm thick
- Dark reddish-brown color

**External Features:**
- Convex outer surface
- Concave inner surface (hilum)
- Hilum: Entry/exit point for renal artery, renal vein, ureter

**Internal Structure:**

**Three Regions:**

**1. Cortex** (outer region)
- Darker color
- Contains Bowman's capsules
- Contains convoluted tubules

**2. Medulla** (inner region)
- Lighter color
- Contains loops of Henle
- Contains collecting ducts
- Forms renal pyramids

**3. Pelvis** (innermost)
- Funnel-shaped
- Collects urine
- Leads to ureter

---

### 11.2 NEPHRON - Functional Unit of Kidney

**Number:** ~1 million per kidney

**Structure - Two Parts:**

#### A. MALPIGHIAN BODY (Filtration Unit)

**Components:**

**1. Glomerulus**
- Cluster of blood capillaries
- Tuft of capillaries
- Receives blood from renal artery
- High pressure

**2. Bowman's Capsule**
- Cup-shaped structure
- Surrounds glomerulus
- Double-walled
- Collects filtrate

---

#### B. RENAL TUBULE (Reabsorption and Secretion Unit)

**Parts:**

**1. Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT)**
- Highly coiled
- In cortex
- Maximum reabsorption occurs here

**2. Loop of Henle**
- U-shaped
- Descending and ascending limbs
- Extends into medulla
- Water reabsorption

**3. Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT)**
- Coiled
- In cortex
- Selective reabsorption

**4. Collecting Duct**
- Many nephrons drain into one
- Passes through medulla
- Final concentration
- Opens into pelvis

---

### 11.3 Urine Formation - Three Steps

#### STEP 1: ULTRAFILTRATION (In Glomerulus)

**Process:**
- Blood enters glomerulus under HIGH pressure
- Pressure ~60 mm Hg
- Forces small molecules through capillary walls
- Into Bowman's capsule

**What is Filtered:**
- Water
- Glucose
- Amino acids
- Salts
- Urea
- Uric acid
- Creatinine

**What is NOT Filtered:**
- Blood cells (too large)
- Proteins (too large)
- Fats (too large)

**Filtrate = Glomerular filtrate**
- Similar to blood plasma minus proteins
- ~180 liters per day!

**Critical Detail:**
- Filtration is NON-SELECTIVE
- Both useful and waste substances filtered
- Useful substances must be reabsorbed

---

#### STEP 2: SELECTIVE REABSORPTION (In Renal Tubule)

**Occurs throughout tubule, mainly in PCT**

**What is Reabsorbed:**
- ~99% of water (178 L out of 180 L)
- **100% glucose** (all useful)
- **100% amino acids** (all useful)
- Most salts (as needed)
- Some urea (passive, unavoidable)

**What is NOT Reabsorbed:**
- Excess water
- Excess salts
- Urea (most remains)
- Uric acid
- Other wastes

**Mechanism:**
- Active transport (requires ATP)
- Against concentration gradient
- Blood capillaries surround tubule
- Reabsorbed substances enter blood

**In PCT:**
- Glucose, amino acids completely reabsorbed
- Most water and salts reabsorbed

**In Loop of Henle:**
- Water reabsorption
- Salt balance
- Creates concentration gradient

**In DCT:**
- Selective reabsorption based on body needs
- Hormone-regulated (ADH, aldosterone)

---

#### STEP 3: TUBULAR SECRETION (Throughout Tubule)

**Process:**
- Blood capillaries secrete substances INTO tubule
- Removes excess substances not filtered

**What is Secreted:**
- K⁺ ions
- H⁺ ions
- Creatinine
- Drugs, toxins

**Purpose:**
- Maintain pH
- Maintain ionic balance
- Remove non-filtered wastes

---

### Final Urine Composition

**Volume:** ~1-2 liters per day

**Composition:**
- Water (95%)
- Urea (2%)
- Uric acid, creatinine
- Excess salts
- Yellow color (urochrome pigment)

**Comparison:**

| Substance | Blood Plasma | Glomerular Filtrate | Urine |
|-----------|--------------|-------------------|-------|
| Water | 90-92% | 99% | 95% |
| Proteins | 7-9% | Trace/None | None |
| Glucose | 0.1% | 0.1% | None |
| Urea | 0.03% | 0.03% | 2% |
| Salts | 0.72% | 0.72% | Variable |

**Key Observation:** Urea concentration increases ~70 times (0.03% → 2%)

---

### 11.4 Regulation of Urine Volume

**Controlled by:** Antidiuretic Hormone (ADH) from pituitary gland

**Mechanism:**

**When Body Has Excess Water:**
- Blood becomes dilute
- Hypothalamus detects
- Less ADH released
- Less water reabsorbed in DCT and collecting duct
- More urine produced (dilute urine)

**When Body Needs Water:**
- Blood becomes concentrated
- Hypothalamus detects
- More ADH released
- More water reabsorbed
- Less urine produced (concentrated urine)

**Other Factors Affecting Urine Volume:**
- Water intake
- Sweating
- Temperature
- Emotional state
- Certain substances (alcohol, caffeine increase urine)

---

### 11.5 Other Excretory Structures

**Besides Kidneys:**

**1. Lungs**
- Remove CO₂ (from respiration)
- Remove water vapor

**2. Skin**
- Sweat glands
- Remove water, salts, small amounts of urea
- Also helps in temperature regulation

**3. Liver**
- Removes bile pigments (from old RBC breakdown)
- Detoxifies harmful substances
- Excreted via bile into intestine

---

### Kidney Failure and Dialysis

**Kidney Failure:**
- Reduced/stopped kidney function
- Causes:
  - Infections
  - Injury
  - Restricted blood flow
  - Diabetes complications
  - High blood pressure

**Consequences:**
- Urea accumulates in blood (uremia)
- Fluid imbalance
- Electrolyte imbalance
- Can be fatal

**Treatment: Artificial Kidney (Hemodialysis)**

**How It Works:**
1. Patient's blood removed from artery
2. Passed through dialysis machine
3. Blood flows through tubes with semi-permeable membrane
4. Tubes immersed in dialysis fluid
5. Dialysis fluid composition = blood minus wastes
6. Wastes diffuse from blood → fluid
7. Clean blood returned to patient via vein

**Differences from Real Kidney:**
- No reabsorption (only filtration)
- Takes 3-5 hours
- Needed 2-3 times per week
- Temporary solution

**Permanent Solution:**
- Kidney transplant
- Donor kidney implanted
- Can be from living related donor or deceased donor

## 12. Excretion in Plants

### Key Differences from Animals

**Plants:**
- No specialized excretory system
- Lower metabolic rate
- Less nitrogenous waste
- Different strategies

---

### Excretory Products in Plants

**1. Oxygen**
- Byproduct of photosynthesis
- Released through stomata
- Can be considered "waste"
- But used in respiration!

**2. Carbon Dioxide**
- Produced in respiration
- Released through stomata
- But used in photosynthesis!

**3. Excess Water**
- Removed via transpiration
- Through stomata
- Guttation at night/high humidity

**4. Organic Wastes**
- Tannins, resins, gums, latex
- Alkaloids, organic acids

---

### Strategies for Waste Removal

**1. Storage in Vacuoles**
- Waste products stored in cell vacuoles
- Away from cytoplasm
- In old xylem cells

**2. Storage in Leaves**
- Waste accumulated in old leaves
- Leaves shed seasonally
- Removes accumulated wastes

**3. Storage in Bark**
- Old xylem (heartwood)
- Bark formation
- Bark peels off, removing wastes

**4. Secretion as Resins and Gums**
- Stored in special cavities
- Examples:
  - Rubber (latex) from rubber tree
  - Resin from pine
  - Gum from acacia

**5. Excretion into Soil**
- Some wastes released into surrounding soil
- May inhibit growth of other plants (allelopathy)

---

### Why Plants Can Manage Without Excretory Organs

**Reasons:**

**1. Low Metabolic Rate**
- Don't move
- Less energy needed
- Less waste produced

**2. Use of Wastes**
- O₂ from photosynthesis → respiration
- CO₂ from respiration → photosynthesis
- Recycling within plant

**3. Large Dead Tissue**
- Xylem vessels dead
- Can store wastes
- No harm to living cells

**4. Slow Growth**
- Wastes accumulate slowly
- Time to deal with them

**5. Shedding Parts**
- Leaves fall
- Bark peels
- Takes wastes away

## 14. Common Misconceptions Clarified

### Misconception 1: Plants Don't Respire

**WRONG:** Plants only photosynthesize
**CORRECT:**
- Plants respire 24/7 (day and night)
- Photosynthesis only during day
- During day: Photosynthesis > Respiration (net O₂ release)
- At night: Only respiration (O₂ uptake, CO₂ release)

### Misconception 2: Arteries Always Carry Oxygenated Blood

**WRONG:** Arteries = oxygenated, Veins = deoxygenated
**CORRECT:**
- **Arteries** = carry blood AWAY from heart
- **Veins** = carry blood TO heart
- **Exceptions:**
  - Pulmonary artery: deoxygenated (heart → lungs)
  - Pulmonary vein: oxygenated (lungs → heart)

### Misconception 3: Digestion Only in Stomach

**WRONG:** All digestion happens in stomach
**CORRECT:**
- **Mouth:** Starch → Maltose
- **Stomach:** Proteins → Peptones
- **Small Intestine:** COMPLETE digestion (main site)

### Misconception 4: More Chambers = Better Heart

**WRONG:** 4 chambers always better than 2 or 3
**CORRECT:**
- Depends on organism's needs
- Fish (2 chambers) perfectly adapted for their lifestyle
- Mixing in 3-chamber hearts tolerable for amphibians/reptiles (cold-blooded)
- 4 chambers essential for high metabolic rate (warm-blooded)

### Misconception 5: Kidneys Only Remove Urea

**WRONG:** Kidneys just filter urea
**CORRECT:**
- Remove excess water
- Remove excess salts
- Maintain pH balance
- Remove uric acid, creatinine
- Regulate blood pressure (via water balance)
- Produce hormones (erythropoietin)

## 17. Exam Strategy and Common Questions

### 3-Mark Questions

**Q: Explain the process of nutrition in Amoeba.**
**Answer Structure:**
1. Food capture (pseudopodia)
2. Food vacuole formation
3. Digestion inside vacuole
4. Absorption into cytoplasm
5. Egestion of waste

**Q: What is the role of HCl in stomach?**
**Answer:**
1. Kills bacteria
2. Creates acidic medium for pepsin
3. Activates pepsinogen to pepsin
4. Helps in protein breakdown

**Q: Describe double circulation in humans.**
**Answer:**
1. Blood passes through heart twice
2. Pulmonary circulation: Right heart → Lungs → Left heart
3. Systemic circulation: Left heart → Body → Right heart
4. Prevents mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood
5. Ensures efficient O₂ delivery

---

### 5-Mark Questions

**Q: Describe the process of urine formation in humans.**
**Answer Structure:**
1. Introduction (nephron structure)
2. **Ultrafiltration** - in glomerulus, blood filtered under pressure
3. **Selective reabsorption** - useful substances reabsorbed (glucose, amino acids, water)
4. **Tubular secretion** - excess ions/wastes secreted
5. Final urine composition and volume

**Q: Explain the mechanism of breathing in humans.**
**Answer:**
1. Two phases: Inhalation and Exhalation
2. **Inhalation:** Diaphragm contracts → chest expands → pressure decreases → air in
3. **Exhalation:** Diaphragm relaxes → chest contracts → pressure increases → air out
4. Gas exchange at alveoli by diffusion
5. Role of haemoglobin in O₂ transport

## Summary: Core Principles

**1. Life Processes are Interconnected:**
```
Nutrition → Energy (via Respiration) → Distributed (via Transportation) 
→ Wastes produced → Removed (via Excretion)
```

**2. Structure-Function Relationship:**
- Villi (surface area) → absorption
- Alveoli (thin walls, surface area) → gas exchange
- Nephron (filtering, reabsorption) → selective excretion

**3. Specialized Organs in Multicellular Organisms:**
- Size increase → diffusion insufficient
- Specialized systems required
- Division of labor

**4. Energy is Central:**
- All processes require/produce energy
- ATP = universal currency
- Aerobic respiration most efficient

**5. Homeostasis:**
- All life processes maintain internal balance
- Excretion maintains chemical balance
- Respiration maintains energy balance
- Circulation distributes and collects
