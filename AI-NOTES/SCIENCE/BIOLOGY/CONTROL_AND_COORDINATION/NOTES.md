## 1. Overview
This chapter explains how living organisms control and coordinate responses to environmental changes through nervous impulses and hormones. You will learn about the structure and function of neurons, reflex arcs, brain regions, plant movement types (growth vs rapid response), and hormonal regulation in animals and plants. Learning objectives include understanding signal transmission pathways, comparing fast electrical vs slower chemical coordination, and identifying key glands and hormones.

## 2. Animals – Nervous System 🧠

### 2.1 Structure of Neuron
- Definition: **Neuron** = specialized cell that transmits electrical impulses.
- Main parts:
  - Dendrite = information **acquisition** tip
  - Cell body = impulse **processing** center
  - Axon = impulse **conduction** pathway
  - Axon terminal = impulse **chemical** conversion site

→ In-text Question: Identify (i) where information is acquired, (ii) path of impulse travel, (iii) where conversion occurs.

> [Flowchart]
> [Receptor] >[stimulus detection]> [Dendrite] >[impulse generation]> [Cell body] >[integration]> [Axon] >[impulse conduction]> [Axon terminal] >[neurotransmitter release]> [Next neuron/muscle]

*[Footnote]* Neurons cannot regenerate rapidly; damage may cause permanent loss of function.

### 2.2 Transmission of Nerve Impulse
- Electrical impulse moves along axon due to ion exchange (Na⁺/K⁺).
- At synapse:
  - Calcium influx triggers neurotransmitter release ✓
  - Diffusion across synaptic cleft → postsynaptic receptor binding

**Try-it Yourself**: Draw and label a synapse; shade regions of neurotransmitter release.


### 2.3 Reflex Actions
- Definition: **Reflex action** = rapid, involuntary response to stimulus without cortical involvement.
- Reflex arc flow:
  - Receptor > sensory neuron > spinal cord (relay neuron) > motor neuron > effector

→ In-text Question: What happens at each synapse in the reflex arc?

| Feature              | Reflex Action | Voluntary Action |
|----------------------|---------------|------------------|
| Speed                | Very fast     | Slower           |
| Control center       | Spinal cord   | Brain            |
| Conscious involvement| None          | Yes              |

*[Footnote]* Reflexes protect organisms by bypassing slower thought processes.

### 2.4 Human Brain and Its Regions
- **Fore-brain**: cerebrum, association areas (sight, hearing, hunger)
- **Mid-brain**: relay center; involuntary actions
- **Hind-brain**: cerebellum (balance, coordination), medulla (heart rate, breathing)

**Did You Know?** 🌟 Cerebellum contains over half of brain's neurons despite smaller volume.

### 2.5 Protection of Nervous Tissue
- Skull (brain) + vertebral column (spinal cord)
- Meninges + cerebrospinal fluid cushion

### 2.6 Muscle Action
- Definition: **Neuromuscular junction** = synapse between neuron and muscle fiber
- Impulse > acetylcholine release > muscle fiber contraction

**Mnemonic**: **FAST** = **F**iber **A**Ch **S**arcomere shorten → **T**raction

## 3. Coordination in Plants 🌱

### 3.1 Immediate Response to Stimulus
- No nervous tissue; uses electrical-chemical signals cell-to-cell.
- Movement by turgor change:
  - Water efflux/influx in pulvini cells

→ In-text Question: How do leaves of *Mimosa pudica* droop upon touch?

### 3.2 Movement Due to Growth (Tropisms)
- **Phototropism**:
  - Auxin > diffuses to shady side > cell elongation > shoot bends toward light
- **Geotropism**:
  - Roots(+geotropic) grow down; shoots(-geotropic) grow up
- **Hydrotropism**: root growth toward moisture
- **Chemotropism**: pollen tube growth toward ovule

*[Flowchart]* Shoot tip (light detection) >[auxin redistribution]> Shady side cells >[elongation]> Bending toward light

*[Footnote]* Auxin effects vary by concentration and tissue type.

## 4. Hormones in Animals

### 4.1 Endocrine Glands and Hormones
- **Pituitary**: growth hormone (child development)
- **Thyroid**: thyroxin (metabolism)
- **Adrenal**: adrenaline (fight-or-flight)
- **Pancreas**: insulin (blood sugar regulation)
- **Gonads**: testosterone, estrogen (puberty)

→ In-text Question: Why add iodine to salt?

### 4.2 Examples of Animal Hormones
> [Flowchart]
> Scary stimulus >[hypothalamus]> Adrenaline release >[bloodstream]> Heart rate ↑ & blood redistribution > Muscle readiness

### 4.3 Feedback Mechanisms
- Negative feedback: high blood glucose > insulin release ↑ > glucose uptake ↑ > insulin ↓

*[Footnote]* Hormonal effects are widespread but slower than nerve impulses.
