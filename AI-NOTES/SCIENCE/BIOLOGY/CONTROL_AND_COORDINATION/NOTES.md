# Control and Coordination - Class 10 Biology Notes (CBSE)

## Quick Definitions List

**Control and Coordination** - Systems that detect environmental changes and produce appropriate responses

**Neuron (Nerve cell)** - Specialized cell that transmits information via electrical impulses

**Dendrite** - Branched projection of neuron that receives signals/information

**Cell body (Soma)** - Contains nucleus; processes information received from dendrites

**Axon** - Long fiber that conducts electrical impulses away from cell body

**Synapse** - Gap/junction between two neurons where chemical transmission occurs

**Neurotransmitter** - Chemical messenger released at synapse to transmit signal to next neuron

**Receptor** - Specialized sensory nerve ending that detects environmental stimuli

**Effector** - Organ/tissue that produces response (muscles, glands)

**Reflex action** - Rapid, automatic, involuntary response to stimulus

**Reflex arc** - Pathway of neurons involved in reflex action (receptor → sensory → relay → motor → effector)

**Central Nervous System (CNS)** - Brain + spinal cord; main coordinating center

**Peripheral Nervous System (PNS)** - Cranial nerves + spinal nerves; connects CNS to body parts

**Voluntary action** - Conscious, controlled movement involving brain (writing, walking)

**Involuntary action** - Automatic action controlled by mid/hind brain (heartbeat, breathing)

**Cerebrum (Fore-brain)** - Main thinking part; sensory interpretation, voluntary actions

**Cerebellum** - Part of hind-brain controlling balance, posture, precision of voluntary movements

**Medulla** - Part of hind-brain controlling involuntary actions (breathing, heartbeat, blood pressure)

**Stimulus** - Change in environment that triggers response

**Response** - Action/reaction to stimulus

**Tropism** - Directional growth movement in plants in response to stimulus

**Phototropism** - Growth response to light (shoots toward, roots away)

**Geotropism (Gravitropism)** - Growth response to gravity (roots downward, shoots upward)

**Hydrotropism** - Growth response to water

**Chemotropism** - Growth response to chemicals (pollen tube toward ovule)

**Hormone** - Chemical messenger produced in one part, acts on another part

**Auxin** - Plant hormone causing cell elongation; responsible for phototropism

**Gibberellin** - Plant hormone promoting stem growth

**Cytokinin** - Plant hormone promoting cell division

**Abscisic acid** - Plant hormone inhibiting growth; causes wilting

**Endocrine gland** - Ductless gland that secretes hormones directly into blood

**Pituitary gland** - Master endocrine gland; secretes growth hormone

**Thyroid gland** - Secretes thyroxin; regulates metabolism

**Adrenal gland** - Secretes adrenaline; emergency hormone

**Pancreas** - Secretes insulin; regulates blood sugar

**Testosterone** - Male sex hormone from testes

**Oestrogen** - Female sex hormone from ovaries

**Feedback mechanism** - Self-regulating system where hormone level controls its own secretion

**Goitre** - Thyroid gland enlargement due to iodine deficiency

**Diabetes** - Disease where insulin deficiency causes high blood sugar

## 1. Why Control and Coordination?

### The Need for Coordinated Responses

**Key Concept:** Living organisms must respond appropriately to environmental changes

**Examples of controlled responses:**
- Whispering in class (not shouting) - context-appropriate behavior
- Touching hot object → immediate hand withdrawal
- Seeing mouse → cat runs toward it
- Plant growing toward sunlight

**Two Requirements for Coordination:**
1. **Detection** - recognizing environmental changes
2. **Response** - producing appropriate action

**Why "controlled" movement?**
- Random movement = inefficient/dangerous
- Each stimulus needs specific correct response
- Movement must match the triggering event

## 2. Nervous System in Animals

### 2.1 Structure of a Neuron

**Three Functional Parts:**

1. **Dendrite**
   - Tree-like branches
   - **Function:** Information ACQUISITION
   - Contains receptors for stimuli

2. **Cell Body (Cyton)**
   - Contains nucleus
   - **Function:** Information PROCESSING

3. **Axon**
   - Long fiber (can be several feet in humans)
   - **Function:** Information CONDUCTION
   - Covered by myelin sheath (fatty insulation) - speeds up transmission
   - Ends at axon terminal

**Key Structural Detail (Often Missed):**
- One neuron has ONE axon but MANY dendrites
- This allows receiving signals from multiple sources
- Axon can have multiple branches at terminal (sending to multiple targets)

### 2.2 How Nerve Impulse Travels

**Step-by-Step Mechanism:**

**Step 1: Reception**
- Receptor (specialized dendrite tip) detects stimulus
- Located in sense organs:
  - **Gustatory receptors** - taste (tongue)
  - **Olfactory receptors** - smell (nose)
  - **Photoreceptors** - light (eyes)
  - **Thermoreceptors** - temperature (skin)
  - **Mechanoreceptors** - touch/pressure (skin)

**Step 2: Signal Generation**
- Stimulus triggers chemical reaction at dendrite
- Chemical reaction creates **electrical impulse**
- Like dominos falling - once started, continues along neuron

**Step 3: Impulse Conduction**
- Electrical impulse travels: Dendrite → Cell body → Axon
- Speed: Up to 100 meters/second in myelinated neurons
- **Key Point:** Impulse travels in ONE DIRECTION ONLY

**Step 4: Synaptic Transmission**
- Electrical impulse reaches axon terminal
- **Cannot jump gap directly** (synapse = ~20-40 nanometer gap)
- Axon terminal releases **neurotransmitters** (chemicals)
- Examples: Acetylcholine, Dopamine, Serotonin

**Step 5: Next Neuron Activation**
- Neurotransmitters cross synapse (by diffusion)
- Bind to receptors on next neuron's dendrite
- Trigger NEW electrical impulse in that neuron
- Process repeats

**Critical Detail:** Why Chemical at Synapse?
- Allows signal amplification/modulation
- One-way transmission (prevents signal reversal)
- Allows integration of multiple signals
- Target specificity (different receptors for different neurotransmitters)

### 2.3 Reflex Actions - The Fast Response System

**Definition:** Automatic, rapid, involuntary response to stimulus without conscious thought

**Why Reflexes Exist:**
- Thinking takes time (many neurons, complex processing)
- Emergency situations need INSTANT response
- Example: Hand touching flame
  - If brain thinks first: "This is hot → I might get burned → I should move hand"
  - By that time = burned!
- Reflex bypasses thinking → saves time → protects body

**The Reflex Arc - 5 Components:**

```
STIMULUS → [1. Receptor] → [2. Sensory neuron] → [3. Relay neuron in spinal cord] 
→ [4. Motor neuron] → [5. Effector (muscle)] → RESPONSE
```

**Detailed Pathway:**

1. **Receptor** - detects stimulus (e.g., heat receptors in skin)
2. **Sensory neuron (Afferent)** - carries signal TO spinal cord
3. **Relay neuron (Interneuron)** - in spinal cord, connects sensory to motor
4. **Motor neuron (Efferent)** - carries signal FROM spinal cord to muscle
5. **Effector** - muscle contracts, produces response

**Critical Point:** Brain is NOT Involved in Reflex
- Signal goes to brain SIMULTANEOUSLY (awareness comes later)
- But response happens via spinal cord BEFORE brain processes info
- This is why you pull hand back BEFORE feeling pain consciously

**Example Reflex Arcs:**

**1. Knee-jerk reflex:**
- Tap below kneecap → stretch receptors → sensory neuron → spinal cord → motor neuron → thigh muscle contracts → leg kicks

**2. Pupil reflex:**
- Bright light → photoreceptors → sensory neuron → midbrain → motor neuron → iris muscles → pupil constricts

**Comparison Table:**

| Feature | Reflex Action | Voluntary Action |
|---------|---------------|------------------|
| Speed | Milliseconds | Slower (seconds) |
| Control center | Spinal cord/Medulla | Cerebrum (brain) |
| Consciousness | Unconscious | Conscious |
| Can be stopped? | NO | YES |
| Example | Hand withdrawal from flame | Writing, walking |
| Evolution | Ancient (even simple animals) | Recent (advanced animals) |

## 3. Human Brain Structure and Function

### Brain Organization - Three Major Regions

**Location:** Inside skull (cranium), protected by cerebrospinal fluid

**I. FORE-BRAIN (Cerebrum)**

**Main Functions:**
- **Thinking and reasoning**
- **Sensory interpretation**
- **Voluntary muscle control**
- **Memory and learning**

**Specialized Regions:**
- **Sensory areas** - receive information from receptors
  - Separate areas for: sight, hearing, smell, taste, touch
  - Each area processes ONLY its specific sense

- **Association areas** - interpret sensory information
  - Integrate data from multiple senses
  - Compare with stored memories
  - Make decisions

- **Motor areas** - send commands to voluntary muscles
  - Control movement of arms, legs, face, etc.

- **Other specialized centers:**
  - Hunger center - tells when to eat/stop eating
  - Thirst center
  - Temperature regulation center
  - Emotional centers

**Critical Detail:** Cerebral hemispheres are crossed
- Left hemisphere controls RIGHT side of body
- Right hemisphere controls LEFT side of body

**II. MID-BRAIN**

**Functions:**
- Relay station between fore-brain and hind-brain
- Controls some involuntary actions
- Coordinates eye movements
- Auditory and visual reflexes

**III. HIND-BRAIN**

**A. Cerebellum**

**Functions - The Balance Master:**
- **Precision of voluntary movements**
  - Makes movements smooth (not jerky)
  - Coordinates complex actions (riding bicycle, writing)
- **Posture maintenance** - keeps body upright
- **Balance and equilibrium** - prevents falling

**What happens if cerebellum damaged?**
- Cannot walk straight line
- Cannot pick up objects accurately
- Loss of balance
- Slurred speech

**B. Medulla**

**Functions - The Life Support System:**
- Controls involuntary actions (you don't think about these):
  - **Breathing rate and rhythm**
  - **Heart rate and blood pressure**
  - **Swallowing**
  - **Vomiting**
  - **Salivation**
  - **Coughing and sneezing**

**Critical:** Damage to medulla = often fatal (breathing/heartbeat stop)

### Function Summary Table

| Brain Part | Type of Control | Main Functions | Example Actions |
|------------|----------------|----------------|-----------------|
| Cerebrum (Fore-brain) | Voluntary | Thinking, senses, decisions | Writing, reading, recognizing faces |
| Mid-brain | Involuntary | Relay, reflexes | Eye movement coordination |
| Cerebellum (Hind-brain) | Voluntary precision | Balance, posture, coordination | Walking straight, catching ball |
| Medulla (Hind-brain) | Involuntary | Life functions | Breathing, heartbeat, blood pressure |

## 4. Protection of Nervous Tissue

### Why Protection is Needed

**Nervous tissue is:**
- Extremely delicate
- Cannot regenerate easily
- Controls all body functions
- Damage = permanent loss of function

### Three-Layer Protection System

**1. Bony Protection**

**Brain:**
- Enclosed in **cranium/skull** (hard bone box)
- Skull has 8 cranial bones fused together
- Small openings only for nerves and blood vessels

**Spinal Cord:**
- Protected by **vertebral column (backbone)**
- 33 vertebrae stacked on top of each other
- Each vertebra has hole - spinal cord passes through
- Intervertebral discs cushion between vertebrae

**2. Membrane Protection**

**Meninges** - three protective membranes:
- Dura mater (outermost, tough)
- Arachnoid mater (middle, web-like)
- Pia mater (innermost, delicate)

**3. Fluid Protection**

**Cerebrospinal Fluid (CSF):**
- Fills space between brain and skull
- Acts like "water cushion" or "shock absorber"
- Brain literally floats in fluid
- Absorbs impact from blows to head

**Additional Protection:** Blood-brain barrier
- Special membrane around brain blood vessels
- Filters what can enter brain from blood
- Blocks toxins and pathogens

## 5. How Muscles Produce Movement

### Muscle-Nerve Connection

**The Problem:** Nervous system makes decisions, but cannot move body parts directly

**The Solution:** Neuromuscular junction

**Neuromuscular Junction:**
- Synapse between motor neuron and muscle fiber
- Neurotransmitter: Acetylcholine (ACh)
- When ACh released → muscle fiber receives signal

### Mechanism of Muscle Contraction

**Step 1:** Nerve impulse arrives at muscle
**Step 2:** Neurotransmitter released
**Step 3:** Special proteins in muscle cells change shape and arrangement
**Step 4:** Protein filaments (actin and myosin) slide past each other
**Step 5:** Muscle fiber shortens (contracts)
**Step 6:** Shortening of many fibers → whole muscle contracts → movement

**Types of Muscles (from Class 9):**

| Type | Control | Location | Example |
|------|---------|----------|---------|
| Skeletal/Striated | Voluntary | Attached to bones | Arm, leg muscles |
| Smooth | Involuntary | Internal organs | Stomach, intestines |
| Cardiac | Involuntary | Heart only | Heart muscle |

## 6. Coordination in Plants

### Key Difference from Animals

**Plants have:**
- ❌ NO nervous system
- ❌ NO muscle tissue
- ✅ Still respond to stimuli
- ✅ Still show coordination

### Two Types of Plant Movements

**Type 1: INDEPENDENT of Growth (Fast movements)**
**Type 2: DEPENDENT on Growth (Slow movements - Tropisms)**

---

### 6.1 Rapid Movements (Not Involving Growth)

**Example 1: Touch-me-not Plant (Mimosa pudica/Chhui-mui)**

**Observation:** Touch leaf → immediately folds and droops

**Mechanism:**
- Electrical-chemical signal travels from point of touch
- Signal reaches **pulvinus** (swollen base of leaflet)
- Pulvinus cells lose water rapidly
- Water exits → cells shrink → leaf folds down
- **No growth involved** - purely water movement

**Why this happens:**
- Protection mechanism (appears dead to herbivores)
- Reduces water loss
- Discourages insects

**Example 2: Venus Flytrap**
- Traps insects when trigger hairs touched
- Rapid water movement causes trap closure

**Key Point:** Information transmission
- Plants use electrical signals (like animals)
- But NO specialized nervous tissue
- Signal moves cell-to-cell
- Slower than animal neurons

---

### 6.2 Directional Growth Movements (Tropisms)

**Definition:** Growth response to environmental stimulus in particular direction

**Mechanism of ALL Tropisms:**
- Unequal growth on two sides of plant part
- One side grows faster than other
- Causes bending toward/away from stimulus

**Types Based on Response:**
- **Positive tropism** - growth TOWARD stimulus
- **Negative tropism** - growth AWAY FROM stimulus

---

#### A. PHOTOTROPISM (Response to Light)

**Observations:**
- Shoots bend TOWARD light (positive phototropism)
- Roots bend AWAY from light (negative phototropism)

**Why Shoots Need Light:**
- For photosynthesis
- Maximum light exposure = maximum food production

**Molecular Mechanism (Very Important for Exams):**

**Step 1:** Light detected at shoot tip
**Step 2:** Hormone **AUXIN** produced at tip
**Step 3:** Auxin moves AWAY from light to shady side
**Step 4:** Auxin causes cells to elongate (grow longer)
**Step 5:** Shady side cells grow MORE than light side cells
**Step 6:** Unequal growth causes bending TOWARD light

```
     Light →
         
    [Shoot tip]
    
    Shady side          Light side
    More auxin          Less auxin
    More growth         Less growth
    
    Result: Bending toward light
```

**Key Concept:** Auxin promotes cell elongation
- High concentration on shady side → more growth → bending
- This is DIRECTIONAL growth

**Critical Detail Often Missed:**
- Only NEW growth bends
- Old/mature parts do NOT change direction
- If you turn plant around, old parts stay bent original way
- Only new growth responds to new light direction

---

#### B. GEOTROPISM/GRAVITROPISM (Response to Gravity)

**Observations:**
- Roots always grow DOWNWARD (positive geotropism)
- Shoots always grow UPWARD (negative geotropism)

**Why This Matters:**
- Roots need to reach soil/water
- Shoots need to reach air/light

**Mechanism:**
- Gravity detected by specialized cells
- Auxin accumulates on LOWER side (gravity pulls it down)
- **In roots:** High auxin INHIBITS growth → upper side grows more → bends down
- **In shoots:** High auxin STIMULATES growth → lower side grows more → bends up

**Note:** Same hormone (auxin), different effects in roots vs shoots!

---

#### C. HYDROTROPISM (Response to Water)

**Observation:** Roots grow toward moisture/water source

**Mechanism:**
- Roots detect water gradient
- Grow toward higher moisture
- Overrides geotropism (more important to find water)

**Example:** Roots may grow sideways or even upward toward water pipe

---

#### D. CHEMOTROPISM (Response to Chemicals)

**Main Example:** Pollen tube growth

**Process:**
- Pollen lands on stigma
- Pollen tube grows down through style
- Grows TOWARD ovule (where egg is)
- Ovule releases chemical attractants
- Pollen tube follows chemical gradient (positive chemotropism)

**Result:** Fertilization occurs

---

### 6.3 Plant Hormones (Phytohormones)

**Key Characteristics:**
- Produced in one part, act in another part
- Travel by diffusion (no blood vessels in plants)
- Very small quantities needed
- Control growth and development

#### Major Plant Hormones

**1. AUXIN**

**Functions:**
- Cell elongation (causes growth)
- Phototropism and geotropism
- Prevents leaf fall
- Promotes root formation

**Synthesis:** Shoot tip (apical meristem)

**2. GIBBERELLINS**

**Functions:**
- Stem elongation
- Seed germination
- Flowering
- Fruit development

**Effect:** Makes plants taller (dwarf plants have gibberellin deficiency)

**3. CYTOKININS**

**Functions:**
- Promote cell division (cyto = cell, kinin = division)
- Delay aging of leaves
- Promote opening of stomata

**Location:** High concentration in fruits and seeds (rapid cell division occurring)

**4. ABSCISIC ACID (ABA)**

**Functions:**
- INHIBITS growth (opposite of others)
- Closes stomata (prevents water loss)
- Promotes dormancy in seeds
- Causes wilting of leaves

**Called "stress hormone"** - increases during drought

## 7. Hormonal Coordination in Animals

### Why Animals Need Hormones

**Two Coordination Systems:**

1. **Nervous system:**
   - Fast (milliseconds)
   - Short-lived effects
   - Reaches specific targets (via neurons)
   - For immediate responses

2. **Endocrine system (hormones):**
   - Slower (seconds to hours)
   - Long-lasting effects
   - Reaches ALL body cells (via blood)
   - For widespread, prolonged responses

**Example Showing Need for Both:**

**Scary situation (seeing a snake):**

**Nervous response:**
- Eyes see snake → brain processes → muscles tense → jump back
- **Time:** Few milliseconds
- **Effect:** Immediate escape

**Hormonal response:**
- Brain signals adrenal gland → releases adrenaline → bloodstream
- **Effects:**
  - Heart rate increases
  - Breathing rate increases
  - Blood diverted to muscles
  - Glucose released for energy
  - Pupils dilate
  - Sweating increases
- **Time:** Takes seconds
- **Duration:** Effects last minutes
- **Purpose:** Sustains "fight or flight" state

---

### 7.1 Endocrine Glands and Their Hormones

**Endocrine Gland Characteristics:**
- Ductless glands
- Secrete directly into bloodstream
- Hormones travel throughout body
- Only affect cells with specific receptors (target cells)

---

#### 1. PITUITARY GLAND (Master Gland)

**Location:** Base of brain, attached to hypothalamus

**Why "Master Gland"?**
- Controls other endocrine glands
- Secretes many different hormones

**Main Hormone: GROWTH HORMONE (GH)**

**Functions:**
- Promotes growth of all organs
- Increases height
- Increases muscle mass
- Bone growth

**Disorders:**

**Deficiency in childhood:**
- **Dwarfism** - abnormally short height
- Proportionate body (normal ratios)
- Mental development normal

**Excess in childhood:**
- **Gigantism** - abnormally tall height
- Can reach 7-8 feet

**Excess in adulthood:**
- **Acromegaly** - enlarged hands, feet, jaw (bones already fused, so only extremities grow)

---

#### 2. THYROID GLAND

**Location:** Front of neck (feel the bump - Adam's apple)

**Hormone: THYROXINE**

**Functions:**
- Regulates metabolism (rate of energy production)
- Controls carbohydrate, protein, fat breakdown
- Essential for normal growth
- Brain development (especially in children)

**Raw Material Needed: IODINE**
- Thyroxine contains iodine atoms
- Without iodine, cannot produce thyroxine

**Disorders:**

**Iodine Deficiency:**
- **Goitre** - swelling of thyroid gland in neck
- Thyroid enlarges trying to produce more hormone
- Low metabolic rate
- Weight gain, tiredness

**Why Iodized Salt?**
- Most soils lack iodine
- Food grown in these soils = low iodine
- Adding iodine to salt prevents goitre
- Simple public health measure

**Excess Thyroxine:**
- High metabolic rate
- Weight loss
- Nervousness, rapid heartbeat

---

#### 3. ADRENAL GLANDS

**Location:** Top of each kidney (ad-renal = near kidney)

**Hormone: ADRENALINE**

**Other Names:** Epinephrine, "Emergency Hormone," "Fight or Flight Hormone"

**When Released:**
- Stress
- Fear
- Anger
- Emergency situations

**Effects:**

**On Heart:**
- Heart rate increases
- More blood pumped per minute
- More oxygen delivered

**On Blood Vessels:**
- Constriction in digestive organs and skin
- Dilation in skeletal muscles
- Redirects blood where most needed

**On Lungs:**
- Breathing rate increases
- Airways dilate
- More oxygen intake

**On Liver:**
- Releases stored glucose
- Instant energy available

**On Pupils:**
- Dilate (better vision)

**Overall Effect:** Body prepared for vigorous activity (fighting danger or running away)

**After Emergency Passes:**
- Adrenaline level drops
- Body returns to normal state

---

#### 4. PANCREAS (Mixed Gland)

**Unique:** Both exocrine AND endocrine

**Exocrine function:** Digestive enzymes into intestine (via duct)
**Endocrine function:** Hormones into blood (ductless)

**Hormone: INSULIN**

**Functions:**
- Lowers blood glucose level
- Promotes glucose uptake by cells
- Converts glucose to glycogen (storage form)
- Stored in liver and muscles

**How Insulin Works:**
- After eating → blood glucose rises
- Pancreas detects high glucose
- Releases insulin
- Insulin acts like "key" unlocking cells
- Glucose enters cells → blood glucose drops
- Pancreas stops releasing insulin

**Disorder: DIABETES MELLITUS**

**Cause:** Insufficient insulin production

**Symptoms:**
- High blood sugar (hyperglycemia)
- Glucose in urine
- Excessive thirst
- Frequent urination
- Fatigue
- Slow wound healing
- Blurred vision

**Treatment:**
- Insulin injections (cannot be taken as pill - digested by stomach)
- Dietary control (low sugar)
- Regular monitoring

**Why Serious:**
- High sugar damages blood vessels
- Can lead to blindness, kidney failure, nerve damage
- Must be managed lifelong

---

#### 5. TESTES (Males)

**Location:** Scrotum (outside body cavity)

**Hormone: TESTOSTERONE**

**Functions:**
- Development of male sex organs
- Secondary sexual characteristics at puberty:
  - Facial/body hair
  - Deep voice (larynx enlargement)
  - Muscle development
  - Sperm production

**Puberty Changes in Boys (10-14 years):**
- Rapid height growth
- Shoulders broaden
- Voice deepens
- Facial hair appears

---

#### 6. OVARIES (Females)

**Location:** Lower abdomen

**Hormones: OESTROGEN and PROGESTERONE**

**Oestrogen Functions:**
- Development of female sex organs
- Secondary sexual characteristics at puberty:
  - Breast development
  - Widening of hips
  - Menstrual cycle regulation
  - Fat distribution pattern

**Progesterone Functions:**
- Prepares uterus for pregnancy
- Maintains pregnancy
- Regulates menstrual cycle

**Puberty Changes in Girls (8-13 years):**
- Rapid height growth
- Hip widening
- Breast development
- Menstruation begins

### 7.2 Feedback Mechanisms

**Definition:** Self-regulating system where hormone level controls its own secretion

**Types:**

**1. Negative Feedback (Most Common)**

**Example: Blood Sugar Regulation**

```
High blood glucose 
    ↓
Pancreas detects
    ↓
Releases MORE insulin
    ↓
Glucose enters cells
    ↓
Blood glucose DROPS
    ↓
Pancreas detects
    ↓
Releases LESS insulin
```

**Result:** Blood glucose stays in normal range (70-100 mg/dL)

**Example: Thyroxine Regulation**

```
Low thyroxine
    ↓
Hypothalamus releases TRH
    ↓
Pituitary releases TSH
    ↓
Thyroid releases thyroxine
    ↓
High thyroxine
    ↓
Inhibits hypothalamus and pituitary
    ↓
Less TSH released
    ↓
Less thyroxine produced
```

**Why Feedback is Important:**
- Prevents excess hormone production
- Maintains homeostasis (stable internal environment)
- Automatic regulation (no conscious control needed)

**2. Positive Feedback (Rare)**
- Output enhances input
- Example: Oxytocin during childbirth

## 9. Common Misconceptions Clarified

### Misconception 1: Brain Controls All Actions

**WRONG:** Brain involved in every response
**CORRECT:** 
- Reflex actions bypass brain (spinal cord handles)
- Many involuntary actions controlled by medulla, not cerebrum
- Brain receives information about reflex AFTER it happens

### Misconception 2: Hormones Work Instantly

**WRONG:** Hormones act as fast as nerves
**CORRECT:**
- Hormones take seconds to minutes to show effects
- Must travel through bloodstream
- Effects are long-lasting, not immediate

### Misconception 3: Plants Don't Respond to Environment

**WRONG:** Plants are passive organisms
**CORRECT:**
- Plants actively respond to light, gravity, water, touch
- They just lack nervous system and muscles
- Use growth and turgor changes instead

### Misconception 4: All Movements Involve Muscles

**WRONG:** Movement = muscle contraction always
**CORRECT:**
- Plant movements = growth or water movement in cells
- Some single-celled organisms move without muscles

### Misconception 5: Auxin Only Causes Growth

**WRONG:** Auxin always promotes growth
**CORRECT:**
- In shoots: Promotes growth
- In roots: HIGH concentration inhibits growth
- Effect depends on concentration and tissue type

## 11. NCERT Exercise Solutions Guide

### Question Types and How to Answer

**Q: What is the difference between a reflex action and walking?**

**Answer Structure:**
- Define reflex action (involuntary, rapid, spinal cord)
- Define walking (voluntary, controlled, cerebrum)
- Make comparison table
- Give pathway for each

**Key Points:**
- Reflex = spinal cord, walking = cerebrum
- Reflex = unconscious, walking = conscious
- Reflex = emergency protection, walking = deliberate movement

**Q: What happens at the synapse between two neurons?**

**Answer:**
1. Electrical impulse reaches axon terminal
2. Triggers release of neurotransmitters from vesicles
3. Neurotransmitters diffuse across synaptic cleft
4. Bind to receptors on next neuron's dendrite
5. Generate new electrical impulse in next neuron

**Q: Which part of the brain maintains posture and equilibrium?**

**Answer:** Cerebellum
- Part of hind-brain
- Controls balance and coordination
- Maintains body posture
- Makes voluntary movements precise

**Q: How do we detect the smell of an agarbatti?**

**Answer:**
1. Chemical molecules from incense stick enter nose
2. Dissolve in mucus lining
3. Detected by olfactory receptors
4. Generate electrical impulse
5. Travel via olfactory nerve to fore-brain
6. Brain interprets as specific smell

**Q: What is the role of the brain in reflex action?**

**Answer:**
- Brain receives information about reflex simultaneously
- But does NOT control the reflex
- Reflex controlled by spinal cord
- Brain only makes us AWARE of what happened
- Awareness comes AFTER response (that's why you feel pain after pulling hand back)

## 12. Plant Hormone Questions - Common Exam Questions

**Q: What are plant hormones?**

**Answer:**
- Chemical substances produced by plants
- Synthesized in one part, act in another
- Control growth, development, and responses
- Examples: Auxin, gibberellins, cytokinins, abscisic acid

**Q: How is movement of sensitive plant different from shoot toward light?**

**Answer:**

| Feature | Sensitive Plant | Shoot Toward Light |
|---------|----------------|-------------------|
| Speed | Very fast (seconds) | Slow (hours/days) |
| Mechanism | Water movement (turgor change) | Growth (cell elongation) |
| Reversible | Yes (leaves re-open) | No (permanent growth) |
| Growth involved | No | Yes |
| Stimulus | Touch | Light |

**Q: Give example of plant hormone that promotes growth.**

**Answer:** 
- **Auxin** - promotes cell elongation, phototropism
- **Gibberellin** - promotes stem elongation
- **Cytokinin** - promotes cell division

**Q: How do auxins promote growth of tendril around support?**

**Answer:**
1. Tendril touches support
2. Auxin produced at tip
3. Auxin moves AWAY from contact side
4. Side away from support has MORE auxin
5. That side grows FASTER
6. Causes tendril to curve around support
7. Tendril wraps around support

**Q: Design experiment to demonstrate hydrotropism.**

**Experiment Design:**

**Materials:** 
- Pot with porous membrane on one side
- Seeds
- Water tray

**Procedure:**
1. Plant seeds in pot
2. Place pot so porous side faces water tray
3. Water is available only through porous side (not from top)
4. Observe root growth direction

**Result:**
- Roots grow toward porous side (toward water)
- Even if this means growing sideways or upward
- Demonstrates positive hydrotropism

**Control:** Pot with water equally available from all sides - roots grow downward (geotropism)

## 13. Animal Hormone Questions

**Q: How does chemical coordination take place in animals?**

**Answer:**
1. Endocrine glands produce hormones
2. Hormones secreted directly into bloodstream
3. Blood carries hormones throughout body
4. Hormones reach target organs/cells
5. Bind to specific receptors
6. Trigger specific responses
7. Feedback mechanisms regulate secretion

**Q: Why is use of iodised salt advisable?**

**Answer:**
- Iodine is essential for thyroid gland
- Required to produce thyroxine hormone
- Thyroxine regulates metabolism and growth
- Deficiency causes goitre (thyroid enlargement)
- Most natural foods lack sufficient iodine
- Iodised salt prevents iodine deficiency disorders
- Simple, cost-effective public health measure

**Q: How does body respond when adrenaline is secreted?**

**Answer - Immediate Effects:**
- Heart rate increases → more blood pumping
- Breathing rate increases → more oxygen intake
- Blood vessels in digestive system constrict
- Blood vessels in muscles dilate
- Blood diverted to skeletal muscles
- Liver releases glucose → instant energy
- Pupils dilate → better vision
- Alertness increases

**Overall:** Body prepared for "fight or flight"

**Q: Why are some diabetes patients given insulin injections?**

**Answer:**
- Their pancreas doesn't produce enough insulin
- Without insulin, glucose cannot enter cells
- Blood sugar remains dangerously high
- Insulin injections provide needed hormone
- Allows glucose to enter cells
- Brings blood sugar to normal levels
- Must be injected (oral insulin would be digested)

## 16. Quick Revision Points

### Neuron and Nervous System
- Dendrite (receive) → Cell body → Axon (conduct) → Terminal (transmit)
- Synapse uses chemicals, not electricity
- Reflex arc = 5 components, bypasses brain
- Cerebrum (think), Cerebellum (balance), Medulla (breathing)

### Plant Coordination
- Two movement types: rapid (water) and slow (growth)
- Tropisms = directional growth movements
- Auxin = phototropism, gibberellin = height, cytokinin = division, ABA = inhibit

### Animal Hormones
- Pituitary = growth hormone (master gland)
- Thyroid = thyroxine (needs iodine, metabolism)
- Adrenal = adrenaline (emergency)
- Pancreas = insulin (blood sugar)
- Testes = testosterone, Ovaries = estrogen/progesterone
- Feedback keeps hormone levels balanced

### Key Differences to Remember
- Voluntary (brain) ≠ Involuntary (medulla) ≠ Reflex (spinal cord)
- Nervous (fast, specific) ≠ Hormonal (slow, widespread)
- Plant rapid (water) ≠ Plant tropism (growth)

## Summary: The Big Picture

**Control and Coordination = Survival**

**Two Systems:**

1. **Nervous System (Animals only)**
   - Fast, specific, short-term
   - Uses electrical + chemical signals
   - For immediate responses

2. **Endocrine System (Animals and Plants)**
   - Slow, widespread, long-term
   - Uses only chemical signals (hormones)
   - For sustained responses and development

**Plants:**
- No nervous system
- Use hormones for growth control
- Electrical-chemical for rapid movements
- Tropisms for directional growth

**Animals:**
- Both nervous and endocrine
- Brain = decision center
- Spinal cord = reflex center
- Glands = hormone producers
- Feedback = balance maintenance

**Key Principle:** Right response at right time in right amount = survival and efficiency
