# Our Environment - Class 10 Biology Notes (CBSE)

## Quick Definitions List

**Environment** - All living organisms and physical surroundings interacting together

**Ecosystem** - All interacting organisms (biotic) + non-living constituents (abiotic) in an area

**Biotic components** - Living parts of ecosystem (plants, animals, microorganisms)

**Abiotic components** - Non-living physical factors (temperature, rainfall, wind, soil, minerals, sunlight)

**Natural ecosystem** - Ecosystem formed naturally without human intervention (forest, pond, lake)

**Artificial ecosystem** - Human-made ecosystem (garden, aquarium, crop field)

**Producers (Autotrophs)** - Organisms making their own food by photosynthesis (green plants, some bacteria)

**Consumers (Heterotrophs)** - Organisms depending on others for food

**Herbivores** - Plant-eating consumers (primary consumers)

**Carnivores** - Meat-eating consumers (secondary/tertiary consumers)

**Omnivores** - Organisms eating both plants and animals

**Parasites** - Organisms living on/in host and obtaining food from it

**Decomposers** - Microorganisms breaking down dead organic matter (bacteria, fungi)

**Food chain** - Series of organisms where each feeds on the previous one

**Food web** - Network of interconnected food chains

**Trophic level** - Position of organism in food chain (feeding level)

**Energy flow** - Transfer of energy from one trophic level to next

**10% Law** - Only ~10% of energy transferred to next trophic level

**Biological magnification** - Progressive accumulation of harmful chemicals at each trophic level

**Ozone (O₃)** - Molecule with three oxygen atoms; protects from UV radiation

**Ozone layer** - Layer in upper atmosphere containing ozone; shields Earth from UV rays

**Ozone depletion** - Reduction in ozone layer due to harmful chemicals

**CFCs (Chlorofluorocarbons)** - Synthetic chemicals causing ozone depletion (refrigerants, fire extinguishers)

**UV radiation (Ultraviolet)** - High-energy radiation from Sun; harmful to organisms

**Biodegradable substances** - Materials broken down by biological processes (food waste, paper, natural fibers)

**Non-biodegradable substances** - Materials NOT broken down by biological processes (plastics, metals, glass)

**Waste management** - Proper handling and disposal of waste materials

## 1. What is an Ecosystem?

### Definition and Components

**Ecosystem = Biotic components + Abiotic components + Their interactions**

**Key Concept:** All organisms interact with each other AND with physical environment to maintain balance

---

### BIOTIC COMPONENTS (Living)

**Classification by Nutrition:**

**1. PRODUCERS (Autotrophs)**

**Who:**
- All green plants
- Photosynthetic bacteria (cyanobacteria)
- Some protists (algae)

**What They Do:**
- Make organic compounds (glucose, starch) from inorganic substances
- Use sunlight energy + CO₂ + H₂O
- Chlorophyll essential

**Why Important:**
- **Foundation of ecosystem**
- Convert solar energy to chemical energy
- Only source of food for entire ecosystem
- Release O₂ as byproduct

**Examples:**
- Grass, trees, shrubs
- Phytoplankton (in water)
- Algae

---

**2. CONSUMERS (Heterotrophs)**

**Cannot make own food; depend on producers/other consumers**

**Types Based on Food Source:**

**A. Herbivores (Primary Consumers)**
- Eat only plants
- Directly depend on producers
- Examples: Cow, goat, rabbit, grasshopper, deer

**B. Carnivores (Secondary/Tertiary Consumers)**
- Eat other animals
- Secondary: eat herbivores (snake, frog, small fish)
- Tertiary: eat secondary consumers (hawk, lion, shark)

**C. Omnivores**
- Eat both plants and animals
- Examples: Human, bear, crow, cockroach

**D. Parasites**
- Live on/in host organism
- Obtain nutrition from host
- May harm host
- Examples: Tapeworm, lice, ticks, Cuscuta (plant parasite)

---

**3. DECOMPOSERS (Saprotrophs)**

**Who:**
- Bacteria
- Fungi (mushrooms, molds)

**What They Do:**
- Break down dead organisms
- Break down waste products
- Convert complex organic substances → simple inorganic substances
- Release nutrients back to soil

**Process:**
```
Dead plants/animals → Decomposers → Simple inorganic nutrients → Soil → Absorbed by plants
```

**Why Critically Important:**

**Without decomposers:**
- Dead bodies would pile up everywhere
- Nutrients locked in dead matter
- No replenishment of soil nutrients
- Plants would die (no nutrients)
- **Entire ecosystem would collapse**

**Critical Detail Often Missed:**
- Decomposers = nature's recyclers
- They DON'T just "clean up"
- They actually **release nutrients** that producers need
- Close the nutrient cycle

---

### ABIOTIC COMPONENTS (Non-living)

**Physical and Chemical Factors:**

**1. Temperature**
- Affects enzyme activity
- Determines species that can survive
- Influences metabolic rates

**2. Rainfall/Water**
- Essential for all life
- Determines type of ecosystem
- Desert vs rainforest

**3. Sunlight**
- Energy source for producers
- Affects photosynthesis rate
- Influences day/night cycles

**4. Wind**
- Seed dispersal
- Pollination
- Temperature regulation

**5. Soil**
- Nutrients for plants
- pH affects what can grow
- Texture affects water retention

**6. Minerals**
- N, P, K, etc. from soil
- Essential for plant growth
- Cycling through ecosystem

**7. Atmospheric Gases**
- O₂ for respiration
- CO₂ for photosynthesis
- Balance maintained

---

### Types of Ecosystems

**NATURAL ECOSYSTEMS:**

**Terrestrial:**
- Forest (tropical, temperate, boreal)
- Grassland
- Desert
- Tundra

**Aquatic:**
- Pond
- Lake
- River
- Ocean
- Wetland

**Characteristics:**
- Self-sustaining
- No human intervention needed
- Balanced naturally

---

**ARTIFICIAL ECOSYSTEMS (Human-Made):**

**Examples:**
- Garden
- Aquarium
- Crop field
- Park
- Artificial lake

**Characteristics:**
- Require human maintenance
- Not self-sustaining initially
- Need inputs (water, fertilizer, cleaning)
- Can become somewhat self-sustaining (aquarium)

### NCERT Activity 13.1: Designing an Aquarium

**Requirements for Aquarium:**

**1. Container**
- Large jar/tank
- Transparent (for light)
- Provides swimming space

**2. Water**
- Habitat for aquatic organisms
- Solvent for nutrients

**3. Oxygen**
- For respiration
- Provided by: oxygen pump (aerator) OR aquatic plants

**4. Food**
- Fish food (external input) OR producers in aquarium

**5. Aquatic Plants**
- Act as producers (photosynthesis)
- Release O₂
- Absorb CO₂
- Provide shelter

**6. Aquatic Animals**
- Fish (consumers)
- Snails (clean algae, scavengers)
- Maybe small herbivorous fish

**How It Becomes Self-Sustaining:**

**Without plants (non-sustainable):**
- Need external O₂ pump continuously
- Need to add fish food
- Need frequent water changes
- High maintenance

**With plants + compatible animals (self-sustaining):**
```
Plants photosynthesize → produce O₂ + food
Fish use O₂ → release CO₂
Plants use CO₂ → produce more O₂
Fish waste → nutrients for plants
Decomposers break down waste → nutrients
CYCLE CONTINUES
```

**Why Needs Periodic Cleaning:**
- Excess algae growth (blocks light)
- Accumulated waste exceeds decomposer capacity
- Dead organisms need removal
- Glass gets dirty
- Unlike ponds/lakes (larger, more balanced)

**Why Ponds/Lakes Don't Need Cleaning:**
- Much larger volume
- More diverse organisms
- Better balance of producers, consumers, decomposers
- Natural water flow
- Larger decomposer populations

## 2. Food Chains and Food Webs

### Food Chain Basics

**Definition:** Linear sequence of organisms where each feeds on the previous one

**Direction:** Energy and nutrients flow in ONE direction

**Format:** Producer → Primary Consumer → Secondary Consumer → Tertiary Consumer

---

### Examples of Food Chains

**1. Forest Ecosystem:**
```
Grass → Deer → Tiger
(Producer) → (Herbivore) → (Carnivore)
```

**2. Grassland Ecosystem:**
```
Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Hawk
```

**3. Pond Ecosystem:**
```
Algae → Small fish → Large fish
```

**4. Ocean:**
```
Phytoplankton → Zooplankton → Small fish → Large fish → Shark
```

**5. Terrestrial (Another Example):**
```
Plant → Caterpillar → Bird → Cat
```

---

### Trophic Levels

**Definition:** Position/step in food chain based on feeding relationship

**Levels:**

**First Trophic Level (T1):**
- **Producers** (autotrophs)
- Make their own food
- Capture solar energy
- Examples: Grass, algae, trees

**Second Trophic Level (T2):**
- **Primary Consumers** (herbivores)
- Eat producers
- Examples: Grasshopper, deer, rabbit, cow

**Third Trophic Level (T3):**
- **Secondary Consumers** (small carnivores)
- Eat primary consumers
- Examples: Frog, small fish, snake

**Fourth Trophic Level (T4):**
- **Tertiary Consumers** (large carnivores)
- Eat secondary consumers
- Examples: Hawk, lion, shark
- Often top predators

**Fifth Trophic Level (rare):**
- Quaternary consumers
- Very rare due to energy limitations

**Decomposers:**
- **NOT a trophic level** in linear chain
- Act at ALL levels simultaneously
- Break down dead matter from all levels

---

### NCERT Activity 13.2: Understanding Food Chain Interdependence

**Key Points from Activity:**

**Question:** Did you avoid putting predator fish?
**Answer:** Yes, must avoid or it will eat other fish

**What would happen otherwise:**
- Predator eats all other organisms
- Eventually dies (no food left)
- Ecosystem collapses

**Interdependence:**
- Each group depends on others
- **Producers** → provide food for all
- **Herbivores** → control plant growth, food for carnivores
- **Carnivores** → control herbivore population
- **Decomposers** → recycle nutrients for producers

**Writing Organisms in Order (3+ steps):**

Example:
```
Aquatic plant → Small fish → Large fish
```

**Is any group of primary importance?**

**Answer:** **ALL are equally important**

**Why:**
- **Without producers:** No food for anyone
- **Without herbivores:** Producers overgrow, carnivores starve
- **Without carnivores:** Herbivores overpopulate, destroy all plants
- **Without decomposers:** Nutrient cycle broken, soil depleted

**Critical Understanding:** Ecosystem balance requires ALL components

## 3. Energy Flow in Ecosystem

### Source of All Energy: THE SUN

**Energy Flow Pathway:**
```
Sun → Producers (photosynthesis) → Primary consumers → Secondary consumers → Tertiary consumers
```

**At each step:** Energy is used and lost (as heat, movement, growth, reproduction)

---

### THE 10% LAW (Energy Transfer)

**Fundamental Principle:** Only ~10% of energy transferred to next trophic level

**What Happens to Energy at Each Level:**

**Producers Capture Solar Energy:**
- Sunlight falls on Earth
- **Only 1% captured** by green plants (rest reflected/unused)
- Converted to chemical energy (glucose) via photosynthesis

**Energy Transfer: Producers → Primary Consumers:**

**When herbivore eats plant:**
- **10% → Growth and body mass** (available to next level)
- **90% lost as:**
  - **Heat** during metabolic processes (largest loss)
  - **Energy for movement, digestion**
  - **Undigested material** (in feces)
  - **Energy for reproduction**
  - **Cellular respiration**

**Energy Transfer: Primary → Secondary Consumers:**
- Same pattern repeats
- Only **~10%** of energy from herbivore transferred to carnivore
- **90% lost** again

**Energy Transfer: Secondary → Tertiary:**
- Again only **~10%** transferred
- Pattern continues

---

### Why Food Chains Are Short (3-4 Steps Usually)

**Mathematical Demonstration:**

**Assume:** Producers capture 10,000 units of energy

```
Producers (T1): 10,000 units
       ↓ (10% transfer)
Primary Consumers (T2): 1,000 units
       ↓ (10% transfer)
Secondary Consumers (T3): 100 units
       ↓ (10% transfer)
Tertiary Consumers (T4): 10 units
       ↓ (10% transfer)
Quaternary Consumers (T5): 1 unit (INSUFFICIENT!)
```

**After 4 trophic levels:**
- So little energy remains
- Not enough to support another level
- Population at 5th level would starve

**Why 90% Loss Occurs:**

**1. Metabolic Heat**
- Second Law of Thermodynamics
- Energy conversions never 100% efficient
- Always heat produced
- Heat cannot be reused by organisms

**2. Movement and Activity**
- Energy used for locomotion
- Breathing
- Circulation
- Maintaining body temperature (warm-blooded animals)

**3. Digestion Costs**
- Energy needed to digest food
- Enzyme production
- Peristalsis

**4. Undigested Material**
- Not all food absorbed
- Cellulose in plants (herbivores can't fully digest)
- Bones, hair (carnivores don't digest fully)

**5. Excretion**
- Some energy lost in waste products (urea, feces)

**6. Respiration**
- Cellular respiration produces heat
- ATP production not 100% efficient

---

### Characteristics of Energy Flow

**1. UNIDIRECTIONAL**
- Flows in ONE direction only: Sun → Producers → Consumers
- Energy does NOT cycle back
- Once used at one level, not available to previous level
- Unlike nutrients (which cycle)

**2. PROGRESSIVE DIMINUTION**
- Energy decreases at each successive level
- Largest amount at producer level
- Smallest at top carnivore level

**3. NON-CYCLIC**
- Energy flows through ecosystem
- Not recycled like nutrients
- Constant input from Sun needed

---

### Energy Flow Diagram Implications

**From diagram analysis:**

**1. Pyramid Shape**
- Most energy at base (producers)
- Least at top (tertiary consumers)
- Explains population pyramid

**2. Population Numbers**
- Greatest number of individuals at T1 (producers)
- Progressively fewer at each higher level
- Top predators always small in number
- Example: Many grass plants, few deer, very few tigers

**3. Biomass Pyramid**
- Total mass decreases at each level
- Producers have greatest biomass
- Top consumers have least biomass

**Exception:** Ocean (inverted pyramid)
- Phytoplankton reproduce very fast
- High turnover
- Less biomass at any instant but produces more over time

## 4. Food Webs - The Reality

### Why Food Webs, Not Just Chains?

**Reality Check:**
- Organisms don't eat just ONE type of food
- Each organism is food for multiple others
- Interconnected relationships

**Food Web = Many interconnected food chains**

**Example:**

```
        Grass ←──────┐
          ↓           │
    Grasshopper    Mouse
       ↓   ↓         ↓
    Frog   ←────── Snake ←── Hawk
       ↓              ↓
    Snake          Hawk
```

**Advantages of Food Web Over Simple Chain:**

**1. Stability**
- If one food source disappears, alternatives available
- Ecosystem more resilient

**2. Reality**
- Represents actual feeding relationships
- More complex and accurate

**3. Multiple Pathways**
- Energy can flow through different routes
- Reduces dependency on single source

**Example of Interconnections:**

**Grass eaten by:**
- Grasshopper
- Mouse
- Rabbit
- Deer

**Grasshopper eaten by:**
- Frog
- Bird
- Lizard

**Each organism part of multiple food chains**

## 5. Biological Magnification

### Definition and Mechanism

**Biological Magnification:** Progressive increase in concentration of harmful chemicals at successive trophic levels

**Also called:** Biomagnification, bioaccumulation

---

### How It Happens

**Step-by-Step Process:**

**Step 1: Chemical Entry**
- Pesticides/chemicals sprayed on crops
- Or released in water bodies

**Step 2: Soil/Water Contamination**
- Chemicals washed down to soil
- Or enter water bodies directly

**Step 3: Plant Uptake**
- Plants absorb from soil (along with water/minerals)
- Aquatic plants absorb from water
- Cannot distinguish between nutrients and toxins

**Step 4: Entry into Food Chain**
- Herbivores eat contaminated plants
- Chemicals accumulate in herbivore body

**Step 5: Progressive Accumulation**
- Herbivore eaten by carnivore
- Carnivore gets ALL the accumulated chemicals from herbivore
- Plus chemicals from many herbivores eaten
- Concentration MULTIPLIES

**Step 6: Maximum at Top**
- Top carnivores have highest concentration
- Humans often at top → highest exposure

---

### Why Concentration Increases

**Key Factors:**

**1. Non-Biodegradable**
- Chemicals like DDT, mercury, PCBs cannot be broken down
- Persist in environment
- Cannot be excreted efficiently by organisms

**2. Fat-Soluble**
- Many pesticides dissolve in fats
- Stored in fatty tissues
- Not eliminated in urine (water-soluble)
- Accumulate over lifetime

**3. Long-Lived Organisms at Top**
- Top predators live longer
- More time to accumulate chemicals
- Each meal adds more

**4. Feeding on Multiple Organisms**
- One carnivore eats MANY herbivores
- Each herbivore has accumulated chemicals
- Carnivore gets sum of all

---

### Mathematical Example

**Assume:** Water has 0.01 ppm (parts per million) of pesticide

```
Water: 0.01 ppm
     ↓
Algae (filter water): 0.1 ppm (10× increase)
     ↓
Small fish (eat many algae): 1 ppm (10× increase)
     ↓
Large fish (eat many small fish): 10 ppm (10× increase)
     ↓
Humans/Birds (eat many large fish): 100 ppm (10× increase)
```

**Result:** Concentration increased **10,000 times** from water to humans!

---

### Examples of Biomagnification

**1. DDT (Pesticide)**

**Historical Case:**
- Used extensively as pesticide
- Entered aquatic ecosystems
- Accumulated in fish
- Fish-eating birds affected
- **Eggshell thinning** in birds of prey (eagles, falcons)
- Eggs broke before hatching
- Population decline
- DDT now banned in many countries

**2. Mercury**

**Source:**
- Industrial waste
- Coal burning
- Mining

**Pathway:**
- Released into water → Converted to methylmercury (by bacteria)
- Absorbed by plankton → Fish → Larger fish → Humans

**Effects:**
- **Minamata disease** (Japan) - mercury poisoning
- Neurological damage
- Birth defects

**3. Lead**

**Sources:**
- Lead-based paints
- Industrial emissions
- Old pipes

**Effects:**
- Accumulates in bones
- Affects nervous system
- Especially dangerous for children

---

### Impact on Humans

**We are at top of food chain:**
- Maximum accumulation in our bodies

**Health Effects:**
- Cancer
- Reproductive problems
- Nervous system damage
- Liver and kidney damage
- Hormonal imbalances
- Immune system suppression

**Cannot be removed by:**
- Washing vegetables/fruits
- Cooking
- Peeling (pesticides absorbed inside)

## 6. Ozone Layer and Its Depletion

### What is Ozone?

**Chemical Formula:** O₃ (three oxygen atoms)

**Comparison with Regular Oxygen:**
- **O₂** - breathable oxygen, essential for life
- **O₃** - ozone, deadly poison at ground level

**Two Faces of Ozone:**

**Bad Ozone (Ground Level):**
- Component of smog
- Respiratory irritant
- Damages lungs
- Harms plants
- Air pollutant

**Good Ozone (Stratosphere):**
- 15-30 km above Earth's surface
- Forms protective layer
- Shields from UV radiation
- **Essential for life on Earth**

---

### Formation of Ozone Layer

**Natural Process:**

**Step 1: UV Splits Oxygen**
```
O₂ + UV radiation → O + O
(Molecular oxygen splits into atomic oxygen)
```

**Step 2: Atomic Oxygen Combines**
```
O + O₂ → O₃
(Atomic oxygen + molecular oxygen = ozone)
```

**Continuous Process:**
- Ozone also breaks down
- Dynamic equilibrium maintained
- Natural balance

---

### Why Ozone Layer is Critical

**Function:** Absorbs harmful UV-B and UV-C radiation from Sun

**Without Ozone Layer:**

**Effects on Humans:**
- **Skin cancer** (melanoma)
- **Eye cataracts** (blindness)
- **Weakened immune system**
- Sunburns
- Premature aging of skin

**Effects on Animals:**
- Same as humans
- Aquatic animals affected (radiation penetrates water)
- Damage to eyes and skin

**Effects on Plants:**
- Damage to photosynthesis
- Reduced crop yields
- Stunted growth
- DNA damage

**Effects on Ecosystems:**
- Phytoplankton killed (base of marine food chain)
- Marine ecosystems collapse
- Food chain disruption

**Effects on Materials:**
- Plastics degrade faster
- Paints fade
- Rubber deteriorates

---

### Ozone Depletion

**Discovery:**
- 1980s: Scientists noticed ozone levels dropping
- "Ozone hole" over Antarctica
- Seasonal thinning (August-October)

**Cause: CFCs (Chlorofluorocarbons)**

**What are CFCs?**
- Synthetic chemicals
- Contain: Carbon, Chlorine, Fluorine
- Very stable (don't break down easily)

**Uses of CFCs:**
- **Refrigerants** in refrigerators, ACs
- **Propellants** in aerosol sprays
- **Fire extinguishers**
- Foam production
- Cleaning electronic components

---

### How CFCs Destroy Ozone

**The Destructive Process:**

**Step 1: CFCs Released**
- Leaked from refrigerators/ACs
- Released from aerosols
- Enter atmosphere

**Step 2: CFCs Rise**
- Very stable, don't break down in troposphere
- Gradually rise to stratosphere
- Takes years to reach ozone layer

**Step 3: UV Breaks CFCs**
```
CFC + UV radiation → Cl (free chlorine atom) + Other fragments
```

**Step 4: Chlorine Attacks Ozone**
```
Cl + O₃ → ClO + O₂
(Chlorine destroys ozone, forms chlorine monoxide)
```

**Step 5: Chlorine Regenerates (CATALYTIC)**
```
ClO + O → Cl + O₂
(Chlorine freed again)
```

**Critical Point:**
- **ONE chlorine atom can destroy 100,000 ozone molecules** before removed
- Catalytic reaction (Cl regenerated)
- This is why CFCs so destructive

**Step 6: Cl Remains Active**
- Single CFC molecule causes damage for **50-100 years**
- Very long atmospheric lifetime

---

### Global Response

**Montreal Protocol (1987):**
- International agreement
- Freeze CFC production at 1986 levels
- Phaseout schedule

**Current Status:**
- CFCs banned in most countries
- CFC-free refrigerators mandatory worldwide
- HFCs (less harmful) used as replacement
- Ozone layer recovering (SLOWLY)

**Recovery Timeline:**
- Ozone hole stabilized
- Slowly decreasing
- Full recovery expected: **2060-2070**
- Long time because CFCs already in atmosphere persist

---

### Biodegradable vs Non-Biodegradable

#### BIODEGRADABLE SUBSTANCES

**Definition:** Broken down by biological processes (bacteria, fungi)

**Characteristics:**
- **Natural/organic** materials
- Contain bonds that enzymes can break
- Become part of nutrient cycle
- Time varies (days to years)

**Examples:**
- **Food waste** - few days/weeks
- **Paper** - 2-6 months
- **Cotton cloth** - 1-5 months
- **Wood** - 1-3 years (depending on size)
- **Wool** - 1-5 years
- **Leaves** - 1-2 months

**Why They Degrade:**
- Made of compounds enzymes can attack
- Bacteria/fungi produce specific enzymes
- Break C-C, C-O, C-N bonds
- Convert to simple molecules (CO₂, H₂O, nutrients)

---

#### NON-BIODEGRADABLE SUBSTANCES

**Definition:** NOT broken down by biological processes

**Characteristics:**
- **Synthetic/man-made** (usually)
- Bonds that enzymes cannot break
- Persist in environment
- Accumulate over time

**Examples:**
- **Plastics** - 500-1000+ years
- **Glass** - 1 million years (essentially forever)
- **Aluminum cans** - 200 years
- **Styrofoam** - 500+ years
- **Batteries** - 100+ years (toxic)
- **Tires** - 2000+ years

**Why They Don't Degrade:**

**1. Unique Bonds**
- Plastics have C-C bonds enzymes don't recognize
- Synthetic polymers
- No natural enzymes evolved to break them

**2. Stable Structure**
- Very resistant to decomposition
- Strong chemical bonds
- Require extreme conditions to break (high heat, pressure)

**3. No Nutritional Value**
- Bacteria/fungi cannot use as food
- No energy gained from breaking them down
- No evolutionary pressure to develop degrading enzymes

**Partial Exception:**
- Physical weathering (breaking into smaller pieces)
- But chemical structure unchanged
- **Microplastics** - small but still plastic

---

### Environmental Effects

#### Effects of BIODEGRADABLE Waste

**IF MANAGED PROPERLY:**
- **Positive:** Return nutrients to soil
- Enrich soil fertility
- Part of natural cycle
- Support decomposer populations

**IF NOT MANAGED:**
- **Negative effects:**

**1. Foul Smell**
- Decomposition produces gases (H₂S, NH₃, CH₄)
- Unpleasant odor
- Attracts pests

**2. Breeding Ground for Pests**
- Flies, mosquitoes, rats
- Disease vectors
- Health hazard

**3. Water Contamination**
- Leachate (liquid from decay)
- Pollutes groundwater
- Contains pathogens, toxins

**4. Methane Production**
- Anaerobic decomposition in landfills
- CH₄ = potent greenhouse gas
- Contributes to climate change

**5. Oxygen Depletion**
- In water bodies (if waste enters)
- Eutrophication
- Aquatic life dies

**Note:** Even biodegradable waste can be pollutant if excessive

#### Effects of NON-BIODEGRADABLE Waste

**Multiple Serious Problems:**

**1. Accumulation**
- Doesn't break down
- Piles up in landfills
- Landfills filling fast
- Running out of space

**2. Soil Contamination**
- Plastics in soil
- Prevent water infiltration
- Harmful chemicals leach
- Soil fertility reduced
- Plants cannot grow

**3. Water Pollution**
- Plastics in oceans, rivers, lakes
- **Great Pacific Garbage Patch** (size of Texas!)
- Chemicals leach into water
- Aquatic life affected

**4. Harm to Wildlife**

**Animals:**
- Eat plastic (mistaken for food)
- Intestinal blockage
- Starvation (stomach full of plastic)
- Entanglement (fishing nets, six-pack rings)
- Death

**Marine Life:**
- Turtles eat plastic bags (think it's jellyfish)
- Seabirds feed plastic to chicks
- Whales with stomachs full of plastic
- Microplastics in ALL marine life

**5. Toxic Chemical Release**
- Plastics contain additives
- Phthalates, BPA, heavy metals
- Leach into environment
- Hormone disruptors
- Enter food chain

**6. Air Pollution**
- Open burning of plastics
- Releases toxic gases:
  - Dioxins (highly toxic)
  - Furans
  - Heavy metals
  - CO, CO₂
- Causes respiratory diseases
- Cancer

**7. Clogged Drains**
- Plastic bags block drainage
- Flooding during rains
- Mosquito breeding
- Spread of diseases

**8. Aesthetic Pollution**
- Ugly litter everywhere
- Tourist spots ruined
- Reduces property values

### NCERT Activity 13.6: Research on Biodegradability

**Topics to Research:**

**1. How long substances last:**

| Material | Degradation Time |
|----------|-----------------|
| Paper | 2-6 months |
| Orange peel | 6 months |
| Cotton cloth | 1-5 months |
| Wool | 1-5 years |
| Wood | 10-15 years |
| Cigarette butt | 1-5 years |
| Aluminum can | 80-200 years |
| Plastic bottle | 450-1000 years |
| Glass bottle | 1 million years |
| Styrofoam | Never (500+ years minimum) |

**2. "Biodegradable" Plastics:**

**Types:**

**A. Oxo-biodegradable:**
- Break into tiny pieces (microplastics)
- **NOT truly biodegradable**
- Still plastic, just smaller
- **Worse** - enters food chain

**B. Bio-based plastics (PLA - Polylactic Acid):**
- Made from corn starch
- Can biodegrade
- **BUT:** Requires industrial composting
- Specific temperature/humidity needed
- Won't degrade in ocean or landfill
- **Still problematic**

**C. Compostable plastics:**
- Designed to break down in compost
- Needs commercial composting facility
- Most cities don't have such facilities
- **In reality:** Ends up in landfills, doesn't degrade

**Conclusion:**
- "Biodegradable plastic" often **greenwashing**
- Not a solution
- Reduce/reuse/recycle regular plastic better

| Aspect | Food Chain | Food Web |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| Definition | Linear sequence | Interconnected chains |
| Complexity | Simple | Complex |
| Reality | Oversimplified | More realistic |
| Stability | Less stable | More stable |
| Example | Grass→Rabbit→Fox | Multiple pathways |

| Feature | Biodegradable | Non-Biodegradable |
|---------|---------------|-------------------|
| Breakdown | Yes (biological) | No (or extremely slow) |
| Time | Days to years | Hundreds to thousands of years |

|---------|---------|-----------|

| Stability | High | Low (initially) |
| Diversity | Usually high | Usually low |
| Examples | Forest, pond | Aquarium, garden |

## 10. Common Misconceptions Clarified

### Misconception 1: Decomposers Are Not Important

**WRONG:** Decomposers just clean up
**CORRECT:**
- Critical for nutrient cycling
- Break down complex → simple
- Release nutrients to soil
- Without them: No nutrients → No plants → Ecosystem collapse
- As important as producers

### Misconception 2: More Trophic Levels = Better

**WRONG:** Long food chains are better
**CORRECT:**
- Energy limits chain length
- 10% rule restricts levels
- 3-4 levels typical
- Longer chains = more energy loss
- Not possible beyond 4-5 levels

### Misconception 3: All "Biodegradable" Products Are Good

**WRONG:** Biodegradable plastics solve pollution
**CORRECT:**
- Many need specific conditions to degrade
- Won't break down in ocean or landfill
- Often greenwashing
- Reduce/reuse still better than biodegradable disposables

### Misconception 4: Ozone Layer Hole Healed

**WRONG:** Ozone hole completely fixed now
**CORRECT:**
- Stabilized, but NOT healed
- Slowly recovering
- Will take until 2060-2070
- CFCs still in atmosphere (long lifetime)
- Still a concern

### Misconception 5: Recycling Solves Everything

**WRONG:** If we recycle, waste problem solved
**CORRECT:**
- Recycling uses energy and resources
- Not all materials recyclable
- Downcycling (lower quality)
- **REDUCE and REUSE are better**
- Recycling should be last resort

### Misconception 6: Burning Waste is Solution

**WRONG:** Burning garbage gets rid of it
**CORRECT:**
- Releases toxic gases (dioxins, furans)
- Air pollution
- Causes respiratory diseases and cancer
- Ash still needs disposal
- **Incineration with pollution control** - different from open burning

## 12. Exam Strategy

### 1-Mark Questions

**Q: Define ecosystem.**
**A:** System of all interacting living organisms and non-living components in an area.

**Q: What is 10% law?**
**A:** Only 10% of energy transferred from one trophic level to next.

**Q: Give two biodegradable substances.**
**A:** Food waste, paper

### 3-Mark Questions

**Q: What are trophic levels? Give example of food chain.**
**Answer:**
1. **Definition:** Steps/positions in food chain based on feeding relationship
2. **Example:** Grass (T1) → Grasshopper (T2) → Frog (T3) → Snake (T4)
3. **Explanation:**
   - T1: Producers (autotrophs)
   - T2: Primary consumers (herbivores)
   - T3: Secondary consumers (carnivores)
   - T4: Tertiary consumers (top carnivores)

**Q: Role of decomposers in ecosystem?**
**Answer:**
1. Break down dead organisms and waste
2. Convert complex organic substances → simple inorganic
3. Release nutrients back to soil
4. Nutrients absorbed by plants
5. Complete nutrient cycling
6. Without them: nutrients locked, ecosystem collapse

### 5-Mark Questions

**Q: What is biological magnification? Explain with example.**
**Answer:**
1. **Definition:** Progressive increase in concentration of harmful chemicals at successive trophic levels
2. **Why happens:**
   - Chemicals non-biodegradable
   - Cannot be excreted
   - Accumulate in body tissues
3. **Process:**
   - Pesticides sprayed → soil/water
   - Plants absorb → small concentration
   - Herbivores eat many plants → higher concentration
   - Carnivores eat many herbivores → very high concentration
   - Humans at top → maximum concentration
4. **Example:** DDT in aquatic ecosystem
   - Water: 0.01 ppm
   - Algae: 0.1 ppm
   - Small fish: 1 ppm
   - Large fish: 10 ppm
   - Birds/Humans: 100 ppm
5. **Effects:** Cancer, reproductive problems, nervous system damage

**Q: Explain ozone layer depletion and its effects.**
**Answer:**
1. **What is ozone:**
   - O₃ molecule
   - Layer in stratosphere
   - Shields Earth from UV radiation
2. **Formation:** O₂ + UV → O + O → O₃
3. **Depletion:**
   - CFCs (refrigerants, aerosols) released
   - Rise to stratosphere
   - UV breaks CFCs → Cl atoms
   - Cl + O₃ → ClO + O₂ (ozone destroyed)
   - One Cl destroys 100,000 O₃ molecules
4. **Effects:**
   - Skin cancer in humans
   - Eye cataracts
   - Weakened immune system
   - Damage to plants and animals
   - Harm to marine ecosystems (phytoplankton)
5. **Action taken:**
   - Montreal Protocol (1987)
   - CFC ban
   - CFC-free refrigerators
   - Ozone layer slowly recovering

## Summary: Core Principles

**1. Ecosystem = Biotic + Abiotic + Interactions**
- All components interdependent
- Balance maintained
- Disturbance affects all

**2. Energy Flow is Unidirectional**
- Sun → Producers → Consumers
- 10% rule limits chain length
- Energy lost as heat at each step

**3. Nutrients Cycle, Energy Flows**
- Nutrients recycled by decomposers
- Energy does NOT cycle
- Constant solar input needed

**4. Food Webs More Realistic Than Chains**
- Multiple feeding relationships
- Interconnected
- More stable

**5. Biological Magnification = Silent Killer**
- Non-biodegradable chemicals accumulate
- Highest concentration at top
- Humans most affected

**6. Ozone Layer = Earth's Shield**
- Protects from UV radiation
- CFCs destroying it
- International action helping recovery

**7. Waste Management Critical**
- Biodegradable waste: compost
- Non-biodegradable: reduce, reuse, recycle
- Segregation at source essential
- Individual actions matter

**8. Human Activities Impact Environment**
- Pesticide use → biomagnification
- CFC use → ozone depletion
- Plastic use → waste crisis
- **We are part of ecosystem, not separate**
