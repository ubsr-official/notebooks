# How Do Organisms Reproduce? - Class 10 Biology Notes (CBSE)

## Quick Definitions List

**Reproduction** - Process by which organisms produce new individuals of their own species

**DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid)** - Genetic material carrying hereditary information; blueprint for body design

**DNA copying** - Replication of DNA molecules during cell division

**Variation** - Differences in DNA sequences/traits between individuals of same species

**Asexual reproduction** - Reproduction involving single parent; no gamete fusion

**Sexual reproduction** - Reproduction involving two parents; gametes fuse

**Fission** - Division of single cell into two or more daughter cells

**Binary fission** - Cell splits into two equal daughter cells (Amoeba, bacteria)

**Multiple fission** - Cell splits into many daughter cells simultaneously (Plasmodium)

**Fragmentation** - Breaking of organism into fragments, each growing into new individual

**Regeneration** - Ability to regrow complete organism from body parts (Planaria, Hydra)

**Budding** - Formation of new individual as outgrowth from parent body

**Vegetative propagation** - Reproduction through vegetative plant parts (roots, stems, leaves)

**Spore formation** - Production of spores in sporangia for reproduction

**Sporangia** - Structures containing spores (singular: sporangium)

**Tissue culture** - Growing new plants from small tissue samples in artificial medium

**Gamete** - Specialized reproductive cell with half chromosome number

**Male gamete** - Sperm (small, motile)

**Female gamete** - Egg/Ovum (large, stores food)

**Meiosis** - Cell division producing four haploid gametes from one diploid cell

**Fertilization** - Fusion of male and female gametes to form zygote

**Zygote** - First cell formed after fertilization; diploid

**Pollination** - Transfer of pollen grains from anther to stigma

**Self-pollination** - Pollen transfer within same flower

**Cross-pollination** - Pollen transfer between different flowers

**Stamen** - Male reproductive part of flower; produces pollen

**Anther** - Part of stamen containing pollen grains

**Pistil (Carpel)** - Female reproductive part of flower

**Stigma** - Sticky top part of pistil receiving pollen

**Style** - Middle part of pistil connecting stigma to ovary

**Ovary** - Swollen base of pistil containing ovules

**Ovule** - Structure containing female gamete; develops into seed

**Embryo** - Developing organism from zygote before birth/germination

**Seed** - Fertilized ovule with protective coat

**Fruit** - Ripened ovary containing seeds

**Germination** - Process by which seed develops into seedling

**Puberty** - Period of sexual maturation during adolescence

**Testes (Testicles)** - Male reproductive organs producing sperm and testosterone (singular: testis)

**Scrotum** - Pouch outside body cavity containing testes

**Testosterone** - Male sex hormone; controls sperm formation and puberty

**Sperm (Spermatozoa)** - Male gametes; tiny cells with tail

**Vas deferens** - Tube carrying sperm from testis to urethra

**Urethra** - Common passage for sperm and urine in males

**Prostate gland** - Gland adding secretions to sperm

**Seminal vesicles** - Glands providing nutrition to sperm

**Semen** - Fluid containing sperm and gland secretions

**Ovaries** - Female reproductive organs producing eggs and hormones (singular: ovary)

**Egg (Ovum)** - Female gamete released monthly (plural: ova)

**Fallopian tube (Oviduct)** - Tube carrying egg from ovary to uterus

**Uterus (Womb)** - Elastic organ where embryo develops

**Cervix** - Lower narrow part of uterus opening into vagina

**Vagina** - Birth canal; receives sperm during intercourse

**Menstruation** - Monthly shedding of uterine lining when egg not fertilized

**Menstrual cycle** - Regular monthly cycle of egg release and uterine preparation (~28 days)

**Placenta** - Disc-shaped tissue connecting embryo to mother for nutrient/waste exchange

**Gestation** - Period of embryo development inside mother (~9 months in humans)

**Contraception** - Methods to prevent pregnancy

**STD (Sexually Transmitted Disease)** - Disease transmitted through sexual contact

## 1. Why Do Organisms Reproduce?

### The Paradox of Reproduction

**Key Question:** If reproduction isn't necessary for individual survival, why do organisms reproduce?

**Critical Understanding:**
- **Reproduction ≠ Essential for individual life** (unlike nutrition, respiration, excretion)
- Individual can live entire lifetime without reproducing
- Reproduction requires SIGNIFICANT energy investment
- Energy could be used for individual's own maintenance instead

**Why Still Reproduce?**

**Answer 1: Species Survival**
- Individual dies eventually (aging, disease, predation)
- Without reproduction → species extinction
- Reproduction ensures species continuation beyond individual lifespan

**Answer 2: We Notice Species Because They Reproduce**
- Single non-reproducing individual → likely unnoticed
- Large populations → bring species to our attention
- Numbers = visibility and ecological significance

**Answer 3: Ecological Niche Maintenance**
- Populations fill specific roles in ecosystems
- Reproduction maintains these roles across time
- Ensures ecosystem stability

### How Do We Know Two Organisms Are Same Species?

**Observable Similarity:**
- Look similar to each other
- Reproducing organisms create new individuals that resemble parents
- Body designs similar
- Behavior patterns similar

**Genetic Basis:**
- Similar because body designs similar
- Body designs similar because DNA blueprints similar
- Reproduction = copying DNA blueprints

## 2. Do Organisms Create Exact Copies?

### The DNA Copying Process

**Basic Event in Reproduction:** Creation of DNA copy

**Why DNA Copying?**
- Chromosomes in nucleus contain DNA
- DNA = information source for making proteins
- Different proteins → altered body designs
- Must copy DNA to pass information to offspring

**The Process:**

**Step 1: DNA Replication**
- Cell uses chemical reactions to build DNA copies
- Creates two identical DNA molecules
- Each new cell gets one copy

**Step 2: Cellular Apparatus Creation**
- DNA copy alone insufficient (no structure to maintain life)
- Must create entire cellular machinery
- Organelles, cytoplasm, cell membrane all duplicated

**Step 3: Cell Division**
- Two DNA copies separate
- Each takes its own cellular apparatus
- One cell becomes two cells

**Critical Detail Often Missed:**
- Cannot just push DNA out of cell
- DNA needs organized cellular structure to function
- This is why cell division accompanies DNA copying

---

### Are Copies Absolutely Identical?

**Answer: NO - And this is CRUCIAL**

**Why Not Identical?**

**1. Biochemical Reactions Are Not 100% Reliable**
- No biochemical process is absolutely accurate
- DNA copying involves millions of chemical reactions
- Small errors inevitable

**2. Variations in DNA Copies**
- Some variations drastic → DNA cannot work with cellular apparatus → cell dies
- Some variations minor → cell survives but slightly different
- Surviving cells = similar but subtly different

**3. Result: Variation**
- Inbuilt tendency for variation
- Not a flaw - it's a FEATURE
- Basis for evolution

---

### Importance of Variation

**For Individual:**
- Most variations: neutral or slightly harmful
- Rarely immediately beneficial to individual
- Individual with variation may be at disadvantage

**For Species (Population):**
- EXTREMELY beneficial
- Ensures survival over time
- Provides "insurance" against environmental changes

**The Ecological Context:**

**Populations and Niches:**
- Organisms occupy specific niches (defined places in ecosystem)
- Niche = role + habitat + resources used
- DNA copying consistency maintains traits suited to niche

**Environmental Changes:**
- Temperature fluctuations (global warming/cooling)
- Water level changes (floods, droughts)
- Catastrophic events (meteorite impacts, volcanic eruptions)
- New diseases, predators, competitors

**Variation as Survival Insurance:**

**Example: Heat-Tolerant Bacteria**

**Scenario:**
- Population of bacteria in temperate water
- Water temperature increases (global warming)

**Without Variation:**
- All bacteria identical
- All adapted to temperate conditions
- Temperature increase → ALL die → EXTINCTION

**With Variation:**
- Most bacteria die (adapted to old temperature)
- A few variants have heat-resistance genes
- These few survive
- Reproduce and repopulate
- New population heat-adapted

**Key Principle:** Variation beneficial to SPECIES (ensures survival), not necessarily to INDIVIDUAL (may be disadvantageous)

## 3. Modes of Reproduction in Single Organisms - ASEXUAL

### Characteristics of Asexual Reproduction

**Key Features:**
- **Single parent** only
- **No gamete formation**
- **No fertilization**
- Offspring **genetically identical** to parent (clones)
- Variation only through DNA copying errors
- **Faster** than sexual reproduction

**When Used:**
- Unicellular organisms
- Simple multicellular organisms
- Favorable, stable environments
- When rapid population increase needed

---

## 3.1 FISSION

**Definition:** Division of single cell into two or more daughter cells

**Who Uses It:** Bacteria, Protozoans (Amoeba, Paramoecium, Leishmania)

---

### A. BINARY FISSION (Division into 2)

**Process:**

**Step 1: DNA Replication**
- DNA duplicates
- Cell now has two identical DNA copies

**Step 2: Cell Elongation**
- Cell grows longer
- DNA copies move to opposite ends

**Step 3: Cytoplasm Division**
- Cell membrane pinches inward
- Cytoplasm divides

**Step 4: Separation**
- Two daughter cells separate
- Each with complete DNA and cellular apparatus

**Examples:**

**1. Amoeba (No Fixed Plane)**
- Shape irregular
- Can divide in ANY plane/direction
- No structural constraint on division plane

**2. Bacteria**
- Can divide in any plane
- Very rapid (20 minutes under ideal conditions)

**3. Leishmania (Fixed Plane)**
- Has whip-like structure (flagellum) at one end
- More organized body structure
- Divides in **definite orientation** relative to flagellum
- Maintains proper distribution of structures

**4. Paramoecium**
- Has fixed body shape
- Division perpendicular to length
- Oral groove must be properly distributed

**NCERT Activity 7.3:** Observe Amoeba slides with and without binary fission to see stages

---

### B. MULTIPLE FISSION (Division into Many)

**Definition:** Cell divides into MANY daughter cells simultaneously

**When Used:** 
- Unfavorable conditions
- Inside host (parasites)

**Process:**

**Step 1: DNA Replication (Multiple Times)**
- DNA replicates many times
- Multiple nuclei form in single cell

**Step 2: Cytoplasm Division**
- Cell membrane doesn't pinch during replication
- Waits until many nuclei present

**Step 3: Simultaneous Division**
- Cytoplasm divides around each nucleus
- ALL daughter cells form at once

**Step 4: Release**
- Daughter cells released together
- Each ready to infect/colonize

**Example: Plasmodium (Malaria Parasite)**

**Location:** Inside red blood cells of host

**Process:**
- Enters RBC
- Undergoes multiple fission
- Produces 10-20 daughter cells (merozoites)
- RBC bursts
- Merozoites released into blood
- Each infects new RBC
- Cycle repeats

**Why Multiple Fission Useful for Plasmodium:**
- Rapid population increase inside host
- Overcomes immune system
- Spreads infection quickly

---

### C. BUDDING IN YEAST

**Not true fission, but related process**

**Process:**
1. Small bud appears on parent cell
2. Nucleus divides
3. One nucleus moves into bud
4. Bud grows
5. Separates when mature
6. Becomes independent cell

**NCERT Activity 7.1:** Observe yeast culture under microscope to see budding

**Application:** Bread-making, alcohol production

---

## 3.2 FRAGMENTATION

**Definition:** Organism breaks into fragments; each grows into new individual

**Who Uses It:** Simple multicellular organisms (Spirogyra - algae)

**Process:**

**Step 1: Maturation**
- Filamentous organism grows
- Reaches certain size/age

**Step 2: Fragmentation**
- Breaks into smaller pieces
- Breaking can be spontaneous or due to water currents

**Step 3: Growth**
- Each fragment contains several cells
- Cells divide and differentiate
- Fragment grows into complete organism

**Why Simple Organisms Only?**

**Simple Organisms (like Spirogyra):**
- Similar cells throughout body
- Minimal specialization
- Each part can survive independently
- No complex organ systems

**Complex Organisms Cannot:**
- Highly specialized tissues (muscle, nerve, etc.)
- Organs at specific positions
- Cell-by-cell division impractical
- Fragment wouldn't have all necessary organs

**NCERT Activity 7.4:** Observe Spirogyra filaments under microscope

---

## 3.3 REGENERATION

**Definition:** Ability to regrow complete organism from body parts

**Who Uses It:** Simple animals (Hydra, Planaria)

**Process:**

**Step 1: Injury/Cutting**
- Organism cut into multiple pieces
- Not intentional reproduction strategy

**Step 2: Specialized Cells Activate**
- Each piece has some regenerative cells
- These cells can become any cell type (pluripotent)

**Step 3: Cell Proliferation**
- Regenerative cells divide rapidly
- Form mass of cells

**Step 4: Differentiation**
- Cells undergo changes
- Become different cell types (muscle, nerve, etc.)

**Step 5: Development**
- Changes occur in organized sequence
- Called "development"
- Forms complete organism with all parts

**Example: Planaria (Flatworm)**
- Can be cut into 10+ pieces
- Each piece regenerates into complete worm
- Each has head, tail, organs

**Example: Hydra**
- Cut into pieces
- Each regenerates complete Hydra
- Even small piece sufficient

**Critical Distinction:**

**Regeneration ≠ Reproduction**

**Why?**
- Organisms don't depend on being cut
- Not a regular reproductive strategy
- Just an ability possessed
- Actual reproduction through budding (Hydra)

**But regenerative cells ARE used for reproduction:**
- In budding process
- Specialized use of regenerative ability

---

## 3.4 BUDDING

**Definition:** New individual develops as outgrowth from parent body

**Who Uses It:** Hydra, Yeast

**Process in Hydra:**

**Step 1: Bud Initiation**
- Regenerative cells at specific site activated
- Usually on body wall

**Step 2: Repeated Cell Division**
- Cells divide repeatedly at that site
- Forms outgrowth (bud)

**Step 3: Bud Development**
- Bud grows larger
- Develops own structures (tentacles, mouth, etc.)
- Miniature Hydra forms

**Step 4: Maturation**
- Bud becomes fully developed
- All organs functional

**Step 5: Detachment**
- Mature bud detaches from parent
- Becomes independent individual

**Difference from Regeneration:**
- Budding = normal reproduction strategy
- Regeneration = repair mechanism
- Budding = uses same regenerative cells

## 3.5 VEGETATIVE PROPAGATION

**Definition:** Reproduction through vegetative plant parts (not specialized reproductive parts)

**Parts That Can Propagate:**
- **Roots** (sweet potato, dahlia)
- **Stems** (potato, ginger, sugarcane)
- **Leaves** (Bryophyllum)

---

### Natural Vegetative Propagation

**A. STEM MODIFICATIONS**

**1. Potato (Tuber)**
- Underground stem
- Has "eyes" (buds/nodes)
- Each eye can grow into new plant

**NCERT Activity 7.5: Potato Propagation**

**Procedure:**
1. Observe potato surface - see notches (eyes)
2. Cut potato into pieces:
   - Some with eyes
   - Some without eyes
3. Place on moist cotton
4. Note position of pieces with eyes
5. Observe over days

**Results:**
- Pieces WITH eyes → shoots and roots develop
- Pieces WITHOUT eyes → no growth

**Conclusion:** Eyes/buds necessary for vegetative propagation

**2. Ginger (Rhizome)**
- Horizontal underground stem
- Nodes and internodes present
- Buds at nodes grow into new plants

**3. Sugarcane**
- Cut stem pieces planted
- Nodes have buds
- Each node can produce new plant

**B. ROOT MODIFICATIONS**

**Examples:**
- Sweet potato
- Dahlia
- Tapioca

**Process:**
- Adventitious buds on roots
- Develop into new shoots
- Shoots grow into complete plants

**C. LEAF MODIFICATIONS**

**Example: Bryophyllum (Kalanchoe)**

**Structure:**
- Notches along leaf margin
- Buds present in notches

**Process:**
1. Buds develop in leaf notches
2. While attached: tiny plantlets form (with roots)
3. Leaf falls to ground OR buds fall off
4. Buds/plantlets contact soil
5. Roots establish
6. Grow into new plants

**D. RUNNERS AND STOLONS**

**Examples:** Grass, strawberry, mint

**Process:**
- Horizontal stems at/below ground
- Nodes touch soil
- Roots develop at nodes
- New plants arise

---

### Artificial Vegetative Propagation

**Human-Controlled Methods:**

**1. CUTTING**

**Method:**
- Cut portion of stem with node
- Place in water/soil
- Roots develop from node
- Plant in soil when established

**NCERT Activity 7.6: Money Plant Cuttings**

**Procedure:**
1. Cut money plant pieces:
   - Some with at least one leaf
   - Some between two leaves (no leaf)
2. Dip all in water
3. Observe over days

**Results:**
- Pieces WITH leaf → new leaves and roots
- Pieces WITHOUT leaf → no growth

**Conclusion:** Presence of leaf (and node) necessary

**Plants:** Rose, sugarcane, grapes, hibiscus

**2. LAYERING**

**Method:**
- Bend branch to ground (don't detach)
- Cover part with soil
- Roots develop at buried node
- Cut from parent when rooted
- Now independent plant

**Plants:** Jasmine, raspberry

**3. GRAFTING**

**Method:**
- Take cutting (scion) from desired plant
- Attach to rooted plant (stock)
- Tissues join and grow together
- Scion grows using stock's root system

**Advantage:** Combine best traits
- Stock: strong disease-resistant roots
- Scion: quality flowers/fruits

**Plants:** Rose, citrus fruits, mango

---

### Advantages of Vegetative Propagation

**1. Faster Growth**
- Plants produced faster than from seeds
- Skip seed germination stage
- Quickly reach maturity

**2. Earlier Fruiting/Flowering**
- Plants flower and fruit earlier
- Don't have to pass through juvenile phase
- Important for agriculture

**3. Propagation of Seedless Plants**
- Many varieties don't produce viable seeds
- Examples: Banana, orange, grapes, rose varieties
- Only way to propagate them

**4. Genetic Uniformity**
- All plants genetically identical to parent
- Predictable characteristics
- Quality maintained
- Farmers know what they'll get

**5. Preservation of Hybrid Varieties**
- Hybrids don't breed true from seeds
- Vegetative propagation maintains hybrid traits

---

### TISSUE CULTURE (Modern Technique)

**Definition:** Growing new plants from small tissue samples in laboratory

**Process:**

**Step 1: Explant Selection**
- Remove small tissue from growing tip (meristem)
- Called "explant"
- Few cells or small tissue piece

**Step 2: Sterilization**
- Explant sterilized to kill microorganisms
- Placed in sterile conditions

**Step 3: Culture on Nutrient Medium**
- Explant placed on artificial medium
- Contains: nutrients, sugars, vitamins, hormones
- Cells divide rapidly

**Step 4: Callus Formation**
- Undifferentiated mass of cells forms
- Called "callus"
- Continues growing

**Step 5: Differentiation**
- Callus transferred to different medium
- Contains hormones for growth and differentiation
- Shoots and roots develop
- Plantlets form

**Step 6: Hardening**
- Plantlets transferred to soil
- Acclimatize to normal conditions
- Grow into mature plants

**Advantages:**
- Thousands of plants from one parent
- Disease-free conditions
- Year-round production
- Rapid multiplication
- Used for: ornamental plants, endangered species
- Space efficient

## 3.6 SPORE FORMATION

**Definition:** Production of specialized reproductive cells (spores) in protective structures

**Who Uses It:** Fungi (bread mold - Rhizopus), ferns, mosses

**Structures Involved:**

**Hyphae:**
- Thread-like structures
- Vegetative parts (not reproductive)
- Grow on surface (like bread)
- Absorb nutrients

**Sporangia:**
- Blob-on-stick structures
- Reproductive parts
- Contain spores
- Located at tips of specialized hyphae

**Spores:**
- Cells that can develop into new organism
- Covered by thick protective walls
- Can survive harsh conditions

**Process:**

**Step 1: Sporangium Development**
- Specialized hypha grows upward
- Swells at tip
- Forms sporangium (spherical structure)

**Step 2: Spore Formation**
- Multiple nuclei form inside sporangium
- Cytoplasm divides around nuclei
- Hundreds of spores form

**Step 3: Spore Maturation**
- Each spore develops thick protective wall
- Resistant to:
  - Desiccation (drying)
  - Temperature extremes
  - Lack of nutrients

**Step 4: Spore Release**
- Sporangium wall breaks
- Spores released into air
- Dispersed by wind

**Step 5: Germination**
- Spore lands on suitable surface (moist, with food)
- Thick wall breaks
- Spore germinates
- Hyphae grow out
- Develops into new organism

**NCERT Activity 7.2: Observing Bread Mold**

**Procedure:**
1. Wet slice of bread
2. Keep in cool, moist, dark place
3. Observe with magnifying glass over week

**Observations:**
- Thread-like hyphae appear
- Black/brown dots appear (sporangia)
- Cottony growth spreads

**Why Spore Formation Advantageous:**

**1. Dispersal**
- Spores tiny and light
- Wind carries long distances
- Spreads to new areas

**2. Protection**
- Thick walls protect genetic material
- Survive unfavorable conditions
- Can remain dormant for years

**3. Large Numbers**
- One sporangium produces thousands of spores
- Increases chance of finding suitable environment

**4. No Need for Water**
- Unlike some other reproductive methods
- Can disperse through air

## 4. Sexual Reproduction

### Why Sexual Mode of Reproduction?

**The Central Question:** Why involve two organisms when asexual reproduction is faster and simpler?

**Answer: To INCREASE Variation**

---

### The Problem with Asexual Reproduction

**Limited Variation:**
- Only source: DNA copying errors
- These errors are RARE
- Variation accumulates SLOWLY
- Not enough for rapid adaptation

**Why Not Just Make Copying Less Accurate?**
- More errors → more variations, yes
- But MOST errors harmful/fatal
- Cell with bad DNA copy → dies
- Can't survive with very inaccurate copying

**The Solution: Combine DNA from Two Individuals**

**Advantage:**
- Each individual has accumulated variations from previous generations
- Two different individuals = different patterns of variations
- Combining them = NEW combinations
- Each combination NOVEL
- Creates much MORE variation than asexual reproduction

---

### The Double-DNA Problem

**Issue:**
- If each generation combines DNA from two parents
- Each new generation would have DOUBLE the DNA
- Generation 1: Normal amount
- Generation 2: 2× DNA
- Generation 3: 4× DNA
- Generation 4: 8× DNA
- This would "mess up" cellular control

**Solution: MEIOSIS**

**Special Cell Division:**
- Produces gametes (germ cells)
- Gametes have HALF the chromosome number
- HALF the DNA amount
- Located in specialized organs

**How It Works:**
```
Body cells: Diploid (2n) - full DNA set
     ↓
  Meiosis
     ↓
Gametes: Haploid (n) - half DNA set
```

**Fertilization Restores Normal:**
```
Sperm (n) + Egg (n) → Zygote (2n)
```
- Re-establishes normal chromosome number
- Re-establishes normal DNA content

**Critical Concept:**
- Sexual reproduction doesn't increase DNA indefinitely
- Reduction (meiosis) + Fusion (fertilization) = Balance

---

### Why Two Different Types of Gametes?

**Energy Requirement for Development:**
- Zygote must grow into complex organism
- Needs energy stores
- Needs nutrients

**Solution: Specialization of Gametes**

**In Simple Organisms:**
- Both gametes similar in size
- May even be identical
- Example: Some algae, fungi

**In Complex Organisms:**
- Gametes specialize into two types

**Female Gamete (Egg):**
- **LARGE** in size
- Contains **FOOD STORES**
- Provides nutrition for early development
- Usually **NON-MOTILE** (doesn't move)
- Energetically "expensive" to produce
- Produced in smaller numbers

**Male Gamete (Sperm):**
- **SMALL** in size
- Contains **LITTLE CYTOPLASM**
- Mainly genetic material
- Usually **MOTILE** (has tail/flagellum)
- Energetically "cheap" to produce
- Produced in LARGE numbers

**Why This Strategy?**
- One partner provides nutrients (female)
- Other partner provides genetic diversity (male)
- Increases chances of fertilization (many sperm, few eggs)
- Efficient division of reproductive investment

## 5. Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants

### Flower Structure - Review

**Four Whorls (from outside to inside):**

**1. Calyx** (outermost)
- Made of sepals
- Usually green
- Protective covering for flower bud
- May fall off after flowering

**2. Corolla**
- Made of petals
- Usually colorful
- Attracts pollinators (insects, birds)
- Protects reproductive parts

**3. Androecium** (male)
- Made of stamens
- Each stamen has:
  - **Filament** (stalk)
  - **Anther** (top part)
- Anther produces pollen grains

**4. Gynoecium** (female)
- Made of pistil(s)
- Each pistil has three parts:
  - **Stigma** (sticky top)
  - **Style** (middle tube)
  - **Ovary** (swollen base)
- Ovary contains ovules

**Types of Flowers:**

**Unisexual (Incomplete):**
- Contains EITHER stamens OR pistil
- Not both
- Examples: Papaya, watermelon, cucumber
- Requires two flowers for reproduction

**Bisexual (Complete/Perfect):**
- Contains BOTH stamens AND pistil
- In same flower
- Examples: Hibiscus, mustard, rose, pea
- Can self-pollinate

---

### Structure of Reproductive Parts

**STAMEN (Male):**

**Anther:**
- Contains pollen sacs
- Pollen sacs contain pollen mother cells
- Each pollen mother cell undergoes meiosis
- Produces 4 pollen grains

**Pollen Grain:**
- Yellow powder you see
- Sticks to hands if you touch
- Contains:
  - **Two cells** (vegetative cell + generative cell)
  - Thick protective wall (exine)
  - Resistant to harsh conditions

**PISTIL (Female):**

**Stigma:**
- Sticky surface
- Captures pollen grains
- May have feathery structures (wind-pollinated)
- Or sticky secretions (insect-pollinated)

**Style:**
- Connects stigma to ovary
- Pollen tube grows through it
- Variable length

**Ovary:**
- Contains one or more ovules
- Each ovule attached by stalk (funicle)
- Ovule has:
  - Nucellus (nutritive tissue)
  - **Embryo sac** (female gametophyte)
  - Integuments (protective layers)
  - Micropyle (small opening)

**Embryo Sac (inside ovule):**
- Contains egg cell
- Contains other cells (polar nuclei, synergids)
- Site of fertilization

---

### POLLINATION - Pollen Transfer

**Definition:** Transfer of pollen grains from anther to stigma

**Two Types:**

---

#### A. SELF-POLLINATION

**Definition:** Pollen transferred within same flower OR between flowers of same plant

**Requirements:**
- Bisexual flower (has both stamens and pistil)
- OR unisexual flowers on same plant

**Mechanism:**
- Anthers positioned above/near stigma
- Pollen falls directly on stigma
- OR transferred by contact

**Advantages:**
- No dependence on external agents
- Ensured in all conditions
- No wastage of pollen

**Disadvantages:**
- No variation (same genetic material)
- No cross-breeding
- Can lead to inbreeding depression

**Examples:** Pea, wheat

---

#### B. CROSS-POLLINATION

**Definition:** Pollen transferred from one flower to stigma of DIFFERENT flower (usually different plant)

**Agents:**

**1. Wind (Anemophily)**

**Adaptations:**
- Light, small pollen
- Produced in large quantities
- Dry and smooth
- Feathery or sticky stigma
- Flowers lack petals/small petals
- Examples: Grasses, corn, rice

**2. Water (Hydrophily)**
- Rare
- Pollen floats on water surface
- Examples: Some aquatic plants

**3. Insects (Entomophily)**

**Adaptations:**
- Brightly colored petals
- Fragrant
- Nectar production
- Sticky pollen
- Sticky stigma
- Examples: Rose, sunflower, mustard

**4. Birds (Ornithophily)**

**Adaptations:**
- Large, colorful flowers
- Abundant nectar
- No strong fragrance (birds have poor sense of smell)
- Examples: Hibiscus, bottlebrush

**5. Bats (Chiropterophily)**
- Nocturnal
- Strong odor
- Large pollen
- Examples: Baobab

**Advantages of Cross-Pollination:**
- Introduces variation
- Healthier offspring
- Better adaptation
- Avoids inbreeding

**Disadvantages:**
- Depends on external agents
- Wastage of pollen
- May not occur in all conditions

---

### POST-POLLINATION EVENTS

**Step 1: Pollen Grain Lands on Stigma**
- If compatible, stigma accepts pollen
- If incompatible, rejects (self-incompatibility mechanisms)

**Step 2: Pollen Grain Germinates**
- Absorbs water and nutrients from stigma
- Outer wall breaks
- Inner wall grows out

**Step 3: Pollen Tube Formation**
- Vegetative cell forms pollen tube
- Generative cell divides → two male gametes
- Tube grows down through style

**Step 4: Pollen Tube Entry into Ovary**
- Pollen tube enters ovary
- Grows toward ovule
- Enters through micropyle

**Step 5: Pollen Tube Enters Ovule**
- Tube penetrates embryo sac
- Tip bursts
- Releases two male gametes

---

### FERTILIZATION IN FLOWERING PLANTS

**Unique Feature: DOUBLE FERTILIZATION**

**Only occurs in flowering plants (angiosperms)**

**Two Fusions Occur:**

**Fusion 1: TRUE FERTILIZATION**
```
Male gamete (n) + Egg cell (n) → Zygote (2n)
```
- Forms zygote
- Will develop into embryo

**Fusion 2: Triple Fusion**
```
Male gamete (n) + Two polar nuclei (n + n) → Endosperm nucleus (3n)
```
- Forms triploid endosperm nucleus
- Will develop into endosperm (nutritive tissue)

**Why Called "Double"?**
- TWO male gametes involved
- TWO fusions occur
- Both essential for seed development

---

### POST-FERTILIZATION CHANGES

**Changes in Ovule:**

**1. Zygote Development**
- Zygote divides repeatedly (mitosis)
- Forms embryo
- Embryo has:
  - Radicle (future root)
  - Plumule (future shoot)
  - Cotyledons (seed leaves) - 1 or 2

**2. Endosperm Development**
- Endosperm nucleus divides
- Forms endosperm tissue
- Stores food for embryo

**3. Seed Coat Formation**
- Integuments of ovule become hard
- Form seed coat (testa - outer, tegmen - inner)
- Protects embryo

**4. Ovule → Seed**
- Ovule transforms into seed
- Seed = Baby plant in protective coat + food

**Changes in Ovary:**

**1. Ovary Enlargement**
- Ovary grows rapidly
- Becomes bigger and fleshy/dry

**2. Ripening**
- Develops color, flavor
- Accumulates sugars (in fleshy fruits)

**3. Ovary → Fruit**
- Ovary wall becomes fruit wall (pericarp)
- May be fleshy (mango) or dry (pea pod)

**Changes in Other Parts:**

**Usually shrivel and fall off:**
- Petals
- Sepals
- Stamens
- Style
- Stigma

**Sometimes persist:**
- Sepals in tomato, brinjal (seen at top)
- Style in some fruits

---

### SEED STRUCTURE

**Components:**

**1. Seed Coat**
- Outer protective covering
- Hard and tough
- Protects from:
  - Mechanical damage
  - Microorganisms
  - Water loss

**2. Embryo**
- Baby plant
- Parts:
  - **Radicle** - grows into root
  - **Plumule** - grows into shoot
  - **Cotyledons** - seed leaves, store food

**3. Endosperm**
- Nutritive tissue
- Provides food during germination
- Present in some seeds (maize, wheat)
- Absorbed by cotyledons in others (pea, bean)

**NCERT Activity 7.7: Bengal Gram Seed Dissection**

**Procedure:**
1. Soak chana seeds overnight
2. Drain water
3. Cover with wet cloth for one day
4. Cut open carefully
5. Observe parts

**Observations:**
- **Seed coat** - outer covering
- **Cotyledons** - two fleshy parts (food storage)
- **Embryo** - between cotyledons
  - Radicle (pointed toward micropyle)
  - Plumule (with tiny leaves)

---

### FRUIT TYPES

**True Fruit:**
- Develops from ovary only
- Examples: Mango, apple (mostly)

**False Fruit:**
- Develops from other parts (thalamus) in addition to ovary
- Examples: Apple (thalamus edible), cashew (thalamus swollen)

**Classification by Pericarp:**

**Fleshy Fruits:**
- Pericarp soft and juicy
- Types:
  - Berry (tomato, grape)
  - Drupe (mango, plum) - one seed
  - Pome (apple, pear)

**Dry Fruits:**
- Pericarp dry
- Types:
  - Dehiscent (split open) - pea, bean
  - Indehiscent (don't open) - wheat, rice

---

### GERMINATION - Seed to Seedling

**Definition:** Process by which embryo in seed develops into seedling

**Requirements:**
- **Water** - softens seed coat, activates enzymes
- **Oxygen** - for respiration (energy release)
- **Suitable temperature** - for enzyme activity
- **Sometimes light** - some seeds need light

**Process:**

**Step 1: Imbibition**
- Seed absorbs water
- Swells
- Seed coat softens/breaks

**Step 2: Enzyme Activation**
- Stored food (starch, proteins) broken down
- Provides energy and building materials

**Step 3: Radicle Emerges**
- First to come out
- Grows downward into soil
- Forms root system

**Step 4: Plumule Emerges**
- Comes out after radicle
- Grows upward
- Forms shoot system

**Step 5: Cotyledons**
- Epigeal: Cotyledons come above ground (bean)
- Hypogeal: Cotyledons remain below ground (pea, maize)

**Step 6: Establishment**
- True leaves develop
- Photosynthesis begins
- Seedling independent

---

### Advantages of Seed Formation

**1. Protection**
- Seed coat protects embryo
- Can survive harsh conditions

**2. Dispersal**
- Seeds can be transported far
- Wind, water, animals
- Colonize new areas

**3. Food Storage**
- Endosperm/cotyledons provide food
- Helps germination in poor soil

**4. Dormancy**
- Seeds can remain dormant
- Germinate when conditions favorable
- Survival strategy

**5. Variation**
- Seeds from sexual reproduction
- Contain genetic variation

## 7. Male Reproductive System

### Components and Functions

**Main Functions:**
1. Produce male gametes (sperm)
2. Produce male hormone (testosterone)
3. Deliver sperm to female system

---

### TESTES (Testicles)

**Location:**
- Outside abdominal cavity
- In pouch called **scrotum**
- Hangs below penis

**Why Outside Body?**
- Sperm formation requires temperature LOWER than body temperature
- Body temperature: 37°C
- Scrotal temperature: 34-35°C (2-3° lower)
- Higher temperature → abnormal/no sperm production

**Structure:**
- Oval-shaped organs
- Two testes (right and left)
- ~4-5 cm long each
- Connected to body by spermatic cord

**Microscopic Structure:**
- Contains seminiferous tubules (coiled tubes)
- Sperm produced in these tubules
- Interstitial cells between tubules (produce testosterone)

**Functions:**

**1. Sperm Production (Spermatogenesis)**
- Begins at puberty
- Continues throughout life
- Millions produced daily
- Process takes ~74 days

**2. Hormone Production**
- **Testosterone** secreted by interstitial cells
- Functions of testosterone:
  - Regulates sperm formation
  - Causes puberty changes (facial hair, voice, muscle)
  - Maintains male characteristics
  - Influences behavior/libido

---

### SPERM STRUCTURE

**Tiny cells - cannot be seen with naked eye**

**Three Parts:**

**1. Head**
- Contains **nucleus** with haploid DNA (23 chromosomes)
- Covered by **acrosome** (cap-like structure)
- Acrosome contains enzymes
- Enzymes help penetrate egg

**2. Middle Piece**
- Contains many **mitochondria**
- Mitochondria provide energy
- Energy needed for tail movement

**3. Tail (Flagellum)**
- Long whip-like structure
- Enables movement
- Swims toward egg
- Speed: ~3 mm per minute

**Total Length:** ~60 micrometers (0.06 mm)

---

### VAS DEFERENS (Sperm Duct)

**Structure:**
- Muscular tube
- ~30-35 cm long
- Two vas deferens (one from each testis)

**Path:**
- Originates from epididymis (sperm storage)
- Passes through inguinal canal
- Enters abdominal cavity
- Joins with duct from seminal vesicle
- Forms ejaculatory duct

**Function:**
- Transports mature sperm
- Muscular contractions propel sperm
- During ejaculation, rapid transport

---

### URETHRA

**Structure:**
- Single tube
- ~20 cm long in males
- Runs through penis

**Function:**
- **Dual purpose:**
  1. Passage for urine (from bladder)
  2. Passage for semen (from vas deferens)
- NOT simultaneous (sphincter control)

**Path:**
- Starts from urinary bladder
- Joined by ejaculatory ducts
- Passes through prostate gland
- Runs through penis
- Opens at tip of penis

---

### ACCESSORY GLANDS

**Function:** Add secretions to sperm → form SEMEN

**1. SEMINAL VESICLES**

**Location:**
- Behind bladder
- Pair of glands (two)

**Secretion:**
- Alkaline fluid
- ~60-70% of semen volume
- Contains:
  - **Fructose** (sugar) - energy for sperm
  - Prostaglandins - stimulate uterine contractions
  - Proteins
  - Vitamin C

**Function:**
- Provides nutrition to sperm
- Neutralizes acidic environment of male urethra
- Provides energy for swimming

**2. PROSTATE GLAND**

**Location:**
- Below bladder
- Surrounds upper urethra
- Single gland

**Secretion:**
- Milky, slightly acidic fluid
- ~20-30% of semen volume
- Contains:
  - Enzymes (helps liquefy semen)
  - Citric acid
  - Zinc
  - Prostate-specific antigen (PSA)

**Function:**
- Activates sperm
- Enhances sperm motility
- Neutralizes acidity of vagina (helps sperm survival)

**3. COWPER'S GLANDS (Bulbourethral Glands)**

**Location:**
- Below prostate
- Pair of small glands

**Secretion:**
- Clear, slippery fluid (pre-ejaculate)
- Small amount
- Alkaline

**Function:**
- Neutralizes traces of urine in urethra
- Lubricates urethra
- Released before ejaculation

---

### SEMEN

**Composition:**
- **Sperm:** ~2-5% of volume (200-500 million per ejaculation)
- **Seminal vesicle fluid:** 60-70%
- **Prostate fluid:** 20-30%
- **Cowper's gland fluid:** Small amount

**Volume:** ~2-5 mL per ejaculation

**pH:** Slightly alkaline (~7.2-8)

**Functions of Seminal Fluid:**
- Nourishes sperm
- Protects sperm from acidic environment
- Provides medium for swimming
- Enhances fertilization capacity

---

### PENIS

**Structure:**
- Cylindrical organ
- Contains erectile tissue
- Spongy tissues fill with blood → erection

**Parts:**
- Shaft (body)
- Glans (head) - sensitive
- Prepuce (foreskin) - covers glans

**Function:**
- Delivers semen into vagina
- Erection necessary for penetration
- Contains urethra

**Erection Mechanism:**
- Sexual stimulation
- Blood vessels dilate
- Erectile tissue fills with blood
- Penis becomes rigid and erect
- Enables intercourse

---

## 8. Female Reproductive System

### Components and Functions

**Main Functions:**
1. Produce female gametes (eggs)
2. Produce female hormones
3. Provide site for fertilization
4. Nurture developing embryo/fetus
5. Give birth

---

### OVARIES

**Location:**
- Lower abdomen
- One on each side of uterus
- Near lateral walls of pelvis

**Structure:**
- Almond-shaped organs
- ~3 cm long each
- Connected to uterus by ligaments

**At Birth:**
- Contain thousands (400,000) of immature eggs
- All eggs already present
- No new eggs produced after birth

**At Puberty:**
- Some eggs start maturing
- Usually ONE egg matures per month
- Process called ovulation

**Functions:**

**1. Egg Production**
- Eggs present from birth (immature)
- At puberty: maturation begins
- One egg released per month (alternating ovaries usually)
- ~400-500 eggs released in lifetime
- Stops at menopause (~45-55 years)

**2. Hormone Production**
- **Oestrogen (Estrogen):**
  - Main female hormone
  - Causes puberty changes in girls
  - Breast development
  - Female body shape
  - Regulates menstrual cycle
  - Thickens uterine lining
  
- **Progesterone:**
  - Prepares uterus for pregnancy
  - Maintains pregnancy
  - Inhibits further ovulation during pregnancy
  - Works with oestrogen in menstrual cycle

---

### EGG (OVUM) STRUCTURE

**Much larger than sperm**

**Components:**
- **Nucleus:** Contains haploid DNA (23 chromosomes)
- **Cytoplasm:** Large amount
- **Yolk:** Food reserves
- **Cell membrane:** Surrounded by zona pellucida (protective layer)
- **Corona radiata:** Layer of cells around zona pellucida

**Size:** ~0.1 mm diameter (barely visible to naked eye)

**Comparison:**
- Sperm: 0.06 mm long
- Egg: 0.1 mm diameter
- Egg is much larger (contains food stores)

---

### FALLOPIAN TUBES (Oviducts)

**Structure:**
- Pair of tubes (two - one from each ovary)
- ~10-12 cm long each
- Narrow (~0.5-1 cm diameter)
- One end near ovary (open)
- Other end opens into uterus

**Parts:**
- **Infundibulum:** Funnel-shaped end near ovary
  - Has finger-like projections (fimbriae)
  - Fimbriae sweep egg into tube
- **Ampulla:** Wider middle portion
  - **Site of fertilization**
- **Isthmus:** Narrow portion near uterus

**Internal Lining:**
- Ciliated epithelium
- Cilia beat in waves
- Move egg toward uterus

**Functions:**
1. **Capture egg** after ovulation
2. **Site of fertilization** - sperm meets egg here
3. **Transport** fertilized egg to uterus
4. **Provide nutrition** to egg/early embryo

---

### UTERUS (Womb)

**Structure:**
- Pear-shaped muscular organ
- ~7-8 cm long (in non-pregnant state)
- Highly elastic
- Can expand enormously during pregnancy

**Location:**
- Center of pelvis
- Between bladder (front) and rectum (back)
- Above vagina

**Wall Layers:**

**1. Perimetrium** (outer)
- Protective covering

**2. Myometrium** (middle)
- Thick muscular layer
- Contracts during labor
- Powerful contractions expel baby

**3. Endometrium** (inner)
- Lining of uterus
- Rich in blood vessels
- Thickens each month
- Site of embryo implantation
- Shed during menstruation if no pregnancy

**Parts:**
- **Fundus:** Upper rounded part
- **Body:** Main central part
- **Cervix:** Lower narrow neck
  - Opens into vagina
  - Produces mucus (consistency changes during cycle)

**Functions:**
1. **Receives fertilized egg**
2. **Site of implantation**
3. **Protects and nourishes developing fetus**
4. **Contracts during labor** to expel baby

---

### CERVIX

**Structure:**
- Lower part of uterus
- Narrow tube (~2-3 cm long)
- Opens into vagina
- Internal os (opens into uterus)
- External os (opens into vagina)

**Functions:**
1. **Produces mucus:**
   - Thick at most times (blocks sperm/bacteria)
   - Thin during ovulation (allows sperm passage)
2. **Barrier between uterus and vagina**
3. **Dilates during labor** (up to 10 cm)

---

### VAGINA

**Structure:**
- Muscular tube
- ~7-10 cm long
- Elastic walls

**Location:**
- Extends from cervix to outside body
- Opens to exterior (vaginal opening)

**Functions:**
1. **Receives penis** during sexual intercourse
2. **Passage for sperm** to reach uterus
3. **Birth canal** - baby passes through
4. **Menstrual flow** exits through vagina

## 9. Fertilization and Pregnancy in Humans

### The Journey of Sperm

**Step 1: Ejaculation**
- Semen deposited in vagina
- Near cervix
- Contains 200-500 million sperm

**Step 2: Through Cervix**
- Sperm swim through cervical mucus
- Mucus must be thin (ovulation time)
- Only few thousand reach uterus
- Most sperm die in vagina (acidic environment)

**Step 3: Through Uterus**
- Sperm swim upward
- Uterine contractions help
- Takes ~30 minutes to hours

**Step 4: Into Fallopian Tubes**
- Sperm enter both tubes
- Swim toward upper portion
- Only few hundred reach egg
- Chemical signals from egg guide sperm

**Sperm Survival:**
- Can live 3-5 days inside female body
- Wait for egg if ovulation hasn't occurred yet

---

### The Journey of Egg

**Step 1: Ovulation**
- Mature follicle ruptures
- Egg released from ovary surface
- Happens mid-cycle (~day 14 of 28-day cycle)

**Step 2: Capture by Fimbriae**
- Fimbriae sweep egg into fallopian tube
- Egg doesn't move on its own (no tail)

**Step 3: Movement Through Tube**
- Cilia beat in waves
- Push egg toward uterus
- Egg moves slowly
- Takes ~3-4 days to reach uterus

**Egg Survival:**
- Lives only ~12-24 hours after ovulation
- Must be fertilized within this time
- Otherwise degenerates

---

### FERTILIZATION

**Location:** Ampulla of fallopian tube (upper third)

**Process:**

**Step 1: Sperm Encounter Egg**
- Hundreds of sperm reach egg
- Surround zona pellucida

**Step 2: Acrosome Reaction**
- Sperm release enzymes from acrosome
- Enzymes digest zona pellucida
- Create path for sperm

**Step 3: Sperm Penetration**
- ONE sperm penetrates egg membrane
- Sperm head enters egg cytoplasm
- Tail left outside

**Step 4: Cortical Reaction**
- Egg immediately changes membrane
- Prevents other sperm from entering
- Ensures only one sperm fertilizes

**Step 5: Nuclear Fusion**
- Sperm nucleus (n) + Egg nucleus (n) → Zygote nucleus (2n)
- Restores diploid number
- Genetic material combined

**Step 6: Zygote Formation**
- First cell of new organism
- Contains DNA from both parents
- Begins dividing immediately

**Time from intercourse to fertilization:** ~24-48 hours

---

### POST-FERTILIZATION: Development

**Cleavage (Day 1-4):**
- Zygote divides repeatedly (mitosis)
- 2 cells → 4 → 8 → 16 → 32...
- Called "cleavage divisions"
- Cells become smaller with each division
- Forms solid ball of cells called **morula**

**Blastocyst Formation (Day 5-6):**
- Morula develops cavity
- Becomes **blastocyst**
- Outer layer: trophoblast (will form placenta)
- Inner cell mass: embryoblast (will form embryo)

**Journey to Uterus:**
- Continues moving down fallopian tube
- Takes ~3-4 days
- Reaches uterus on day 4-5
- Floats in uterus for 1-2 days

---

### IMPLANTATION (Day 6-10)

**Process:**
- Blastocyst attaches to endometrium
- Trophoblast invades uterine lining
- Burrows into endometrium
- Completely embedded by day 10

**Endometrium:**
- Must be thick and receptive
- Rich blood supply
- Prepared by progesterone

**Once Implanted:**
- Connection with mother's blood established
- Pregnancy officially begins
- Hormones prevent menstruation

---

### PLACENTA FORMATION AND FUNCTION

**Structure:**
- Disc-shaped organ
- ~20 cm diameter when fully formed
- ~2-3 cm thick
- Weighs ~500 grams at term

**Formation:**
- Develops from trophoblast + endometrium
- Both embryonic and maternal tissues
- Fully formed by ~12 weeks

**Two Sides:**

**1. Embryonic Side (Fetal Surface):**
- Faces fetus
- Covered by amnion (smooth)
- Has **villi** - finger-like projections
- Villi contain fetal blood vessels
- Umbilical cord attaches here

**2. Maternal Side:**
- Faces uterine wall
- Rough, irregular surface
- Has **blood spaces** (sinuses)
- Filled with mother's blood

**Key Point:** Fetal and maternal blood DO NOT MIX
- Separated by thin membrane
- Exchange occurs by diffusion

---

**Functions of Placenta:**

**1. Nutrition Transfer:**
- Glucose, amino acids, vitamins → fetus
- Via diffusion from mother's blood
- Large surface area of villi (efficient exchange)

**2. Oxygen Supply:**
- O₂ from mother's blood → fetus
- CO₂ from fetus → mother's blood
- Acts as "lung" for fetus

**3. Waste Removal:**
- Urea, uric acid from fetus → mother's blood
- Mother's kidneys excrete fetal wastes
- Acts as "kidney" for fetus

**4. Hormone Production:**
- **Human Chorionic Gonadotropin (hCG)**
  - Detected in pregnancy tests
  - Maintains corpus luteum
- **Oestrogen** and **Progesterone**
  - Maintain pregnancy
  - Prevent menstruation
  - Prepare breasts for lactation

**5. Barrier Function:**
- Protects fetus from:
  - Some bacteria (but NOT all)
  - Some toxins
- Many substances CAN cross:
  - Viruses (HIV, rubella)
  - Alcohol, drugs, nicotine
  - Some medications
- **This is why pregnant women must be careful about what they consume**

**6. Antibody Transfer:**
- Mother's antibodies (IgG) cross placenta
- Provide passive immunity to fetus
- Protects newborn for first few months

---

### UMBILICAL CORD

**Structure:**
- Rope-like structure
- ~50-60 cm long
- ~1-2 cm diameter
- Connects fetus to placenta

**Contents:**
- **Two umbilical arteries** (carry deoxygenated blood from fetus to placenta)
- **One umbilical vein** (carries oxygenated blood from placenta to fetus)
- **Wharton's jelly** (protective jelly-like substance)

**Note the reversal:**
- Umbilical ARTERIES carry deoxygenated blood (opposite to usual)
- Umbilical VEIN carries oxygenated blood (opposite to usual)

---

### GESTATION (Pregnancy Duration)

**Duration:** ~280 days (40 weeks or 9 months from last menstrual period)

**Trimester Division:**

**First Trimester (Week 1-12):**
- Major organ formation
- Most critical period
- Embryo becomes fetus (week 8)
- Morning sickness common
- High miscarriage risk

**Second Trimester (Week 13-26):**
- Rapid growth
- Mother feels movements (quickening)
- Sex can be determined (ultrasound)
- Most comfortable period

**Third Trimester (Week 27-40):**
- Final growth and maturation
- Fetus gains weight
- Lungs mature
- Moves into birth position (head down)
- Preparation for birth

---

### BIRTH (Parturition)

**Initiating Factors:**
- Fetal hormones signal maturity
- Uterus becomes sensitive to oxytocin
- Exact trigger not fully understood

**Process:**

**Stage 1: Dilation (8-12 hours for first baby)**
- Cervix dilates (opens)
- From 0 to 10 cm
- Uterine contractions begin
- Amniotic sac may rupture ("water breaking")

**Stage 2: Expulsion (30 minutes to 2 hours)**
- Baby pushed through cervix and vagina
- Mother pushes with contractions
- Baby's head emerges first (usually)
- Rest of body follows

**Stage 3: Placental (5-30 minutes)**
- Placenta detaches from uterus
- Expelled as "afterbirth"
- Uterus contracts to stop bleeding

**Post-Birth:**
- Umbilical cord cut and clamped
- Forms belly button (navel)
- Baby takes first breath
- Lungs inflate

---

## 10. Menstruation and Menstrual Cycle

### What is Menstruation?

**Definition:** Monthly shedding of uterine lining (endometrium) when pregnancy doesn't occur

**First Occurrence:**
- Menarche (first menstruation): 11-13 years (average)
- Can be 9-16 years (normal range)
- Signals reproductive maturity

**Cessation:**
- Menopause: ~45-55 years
- Periods stop permanently
- End of reproductive years

**Duration of Flow:**
- 2-8 days (usually 3-5 days)
- Varies between individuals
- Average blood loss: 30-40 mL

---

### The Menstrual Cycle

**Definition:** Regular changes in ovary and uterus preparing for pregnancy

**Average Length:** 28 days (can be 21-35 days - normal)

**Day 1 = First day of menstruation**

---

### FOUR PHASES OF MENSTRUAL CYCLE

#### PHASE 1: MENSTRUAL PHASE (Day 1-5)

**What Happens:**
- Uterine lining (endometrium) breaks down
- Shed as menstrual blood and tissue
- Exits through vagina
- Uterine lining at minimum thickness

**Hormone Levels:**
- Oestrogen: LOW
- Progesterone: LOW

**Reason:**
- Previous cycle's egg not fertilized
- Corpus luteum (temporary structure in ovary) degenerates
- Stops producing progesterone
- Without progesterone, lining cannot be maintained

---

#### PHASE 2: FOLLICULAR PHASE (Day 1-13)

**Overlaps with menstrual phase at beginning**

**In Ovary:**
- FSH (Follicle Stimulating Hormone) from pituitary
- Stimulates follicle development
- 10-20 follicles start maturing
- Usually ONE becomes dominant
- Others degenerate

**In Uterus:**
- Endometrium begins to rebuild
- Becomes thicker
- Blood vessels increase
- Glands develop

**Hormone Levels:**
- Oestrogen: RISING (from developing follicle)
- Oestrogen causes uterine lining to thicken

---

#### PHASE 3: OVULATION (Day 14 approximately)

**Trigger:**
- High oestrogen triggers LH surge
- LH (Luteinizing Hormone) from pituitary
- Massive increase in LH

**What Happens:**
- Mature follicle ruptures
- Egg released from ovary
- Egg swept into fallopian tube
- Typically occurs mid-cycle

**Timing:**
- Usually day 14 of 28-day cycle
- But varies (cycle length varies)
- Most fertile time

**Signs:**
- Some women feel mild pain (mittelschmerz)
- Basal body temperature increases slightly
- Cervical mucus becomes thin and stretchy

---

#### PHASE 4: LUTEAL PHASE (Day 15-28)

**In Ovary:**
- Ruptured follicle becomes **corpus luteum**
- Corpus luteum = temporary endocrine structure
- Produces hormones

**In Uterus:**
- Endometrium continues thickening
- Becomes spongier
- Blood supply increases
- Glands enlarge
- Ready to receive embryo

**Hormone Levels:**
- Progesterone: HIGH (from corpus luteum)
- Oestrogen: MODERATE
- These prepare uterus for implantation

**Two Possible Outcomes:**

**A. If Fertilization Occurs:**
- Embryo implants in endometrium (day 6-10 after fertilization)
- Embryo secretes hCG (human Chorionic Gonadotropin)
- hCG maintains corpus luteum
- Corpus luteum continues producing progesterone
- Endometrium maintained
- NO menstruation
- **Pregnancy begins**

**B. If No Fertilization:**
- Corpus luteum degenerates (after ~12-14 days)
- Progesterone and oestrogen levels DROP
- Without hormones, endometrium cannot be maintained
- Blood vessels constrict
- Lining breaks down
- **Menstruation begins** (Day 1 of next cycle)

---

### Hormonal Control Summary

**Pituitary Gland Hormones:**
- **FSH** (Follicle Stimulating Hormone)
  - Stimulates follicle development
  - High in follicular phase
- **LH** (Luteinizing Hormone)
  - Triggers ovulation (LH surge)
  - Maintains corpus luteum

**Ovarian Hormones:**
- **Oestrogen**
  - From developing follicle
  - Thickens endometrium
  - Triggers LH surge (when high)
- **Progesterone**
  - From corpus luteum
  - Maintains endometrium
  - Prepares uterus for pregnancy
  - Inhibits FSH and LH (prevents new follicle development)

---

### Common Menstrual Issues

**1. Irregular Cycles:**
- Common in first 2-3 years after menarche
- Due to hormonal imbalance
- Usually normalizes

**2. Dysmenorrhea (Painful periods):**
- Cramps in lower abdomen
- Due to prostaglandins (cause contractions)
- Can be relieved by pain medication, heat

**3. Heavy Bleeding (Menorrhagia):**
- Blood loss >80 mL
- May cause anemia
- Medical attention needed

**4. Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS):**
- Symptoms before menstruation
- Mood changes, bloating, breast tenderness
- Due to hormonal changes

## 12. Sexually Transmitted Diseases (STDs)

**Definition:** Diseases transmitted through sexual contact

**Why Sexual Transmission?**
- Sexual act = intimate body contact
- Exchange of body fluids
- Pathogens easily transmitted

---

### Common STDs

**BACTERIAL:**

**1. Gonorrhea**
- Caused by: Neisseria gonorrhoeae bacteria
- Symptoms:
  - Painful urination
  - Discharge from genitals
  - Can cause infertility if untreated
- Treatment: Antibiotics
- Curable

**2. Syphilis**
- Caused by: Treponema pallidum bacteria
- Stages:
  - Primary: Painless sore (chancre)
  - Secondary: Rash, fever
  - Tertiary: Organ damage (heart, brain)
- Treatment: Antibiotics (penicillin)
- Curable in early stages

**VIRAL:**

**1. HIV/AIDS**
- Caused by: Human Immunodeficiency Virus
- Attacks immune system
- AIDS = Advanced stage
- Symptoms: Weakened immunity, opportunistic infections
- Transmission: Sexual contact, blood, mother-to-child
- Treatment: Antiretroviral therapy (ART) - controls but doesn't cure
- **NOT curable** - lifelong infection

**2. Genital Warts**
- Caused by: Human Papillomavirus (HPV)
- Symptoms: Warts on genitals
- Some HPV types cause cervical cancer
- Treatment: Removal of warts
- Virus may persist
- Vaccine available (prevents certain types)

**3. Genital Herpes**
- Caused by: Herpes Simplex Virus (HSV-2)
- Symptoms: Painful blisters on genitals
- Recurrent outbreaks
- Treatment: Antiviral medications (control, not cure)
- Lifelong infection

---

### Prevention of STDs

**1. Abstinence**
- 100% effective
- No sexual contact = no sexual transmission

**2. Mutual Monogamy**
- Both partners faithful
- Both tested and disease-free
- Reduces risk significantly

**3. Barrier Methods**
- **Condoms** (male and female)
- Physical barrier prevents contact with infected fluids
- **NOT 100% effective** but significantly reduces risk
- Must be used correctly and consistently

**4. Vaccination**
- HPV vaccine (prevents cervical cancer, genital warts)
- Hepatitis B vaccine

**5. Regular Testing**
- Know your status
- Early detection and treatment
- Prevents complications
- Prevents transmission to partners

**Important:** Condoms DON'T prevent ALL STDs completely
- Some spread through skin contact (herpes, HPV)
- But still provide significant protection

## 13. Contraception

**Definition:** Methods to prevent pregnancy

**Why Needed:**
- **Unwanted pregnancy**
  - Unpl anned/mistimed
  - Major demands on body and mind
  - Life-changing decision
  - Should be conscious choice

- **Population control**
  - Rapid population growth
  - Strain on resources
  - But: inequality often bigger issue than population size

- **Birth spacing**
  - Time between pregnancies
  - Mother's health recovery
  - Care for existing children

---

### Methods of Contraception

#### CATEGORY 1: BARRIER METHODS

**Mechanism:** Physical barrier prevents sperm from reaching egg

---

**A. CONDOMS (Male)**

**Description:**
- Thin rubber/latex sheath
- Worn over erect penis
- Collects semen

**Effectiveness:** ~85-98% (depends on correct use)

**Advantages:**
- No side effects
- Protects against STDs
- No prescription needed
- Inexpensive

**Disadvantages:**
- Must be used during intercourse
- Can tear (rare if used correctly)
- Some people allergic to latex

**How to Use:**
- Put on erect penis BEFORE any contact
- Leave space at tip
- Use only once
- Remove carefully after ejaculation

---

**B. CONDOMS (Female)**

**Description:**
- Polyurethane pouch
- Inserted into vagina before intercourse
- Lines vagina

**Effectiveness:** ~79-95%

**Advantages:**
- Woman can insert herself (control)
- Can be inserted hours before intercourse
- Protects against STDs

**Disadvantages:**
- More expensive
- May be visible
- Makes noise during intercourse

---

**C. DIAPHRAGM**

**Description:**
- Dome-shaped rubber cup
- Covers cervix
- Used with spermicide

**Effectiveness:** ~84-94%

**Advantages:**
- Reusable
- No hormones

**Disadvantages:**
- Needs fitting by doctor
- Must be inserted before intercourse
- Must stay in for 6-8 hours after

---

#### CATEGORY 2: CHEMICAL METHODS

**Mechanism:** Hormones prevent ovulation or implantation

---

**A. ORAL CONTRACEPTIVE PILLS**

**Description:**
- Contain hormones (oestrogen and/or progesterone)
- Taken daily

**Mechanism:**
- Prevent ovulation (no egg released)
- Thicken cervical mucus (blocks sperm)
- Thin uterine lining (prevents implantation)

**Effectiveness:** >99% (if taken correctly)

**Advantages:**
- Highly effective
- Regular menstrual cycles
- Reduced menstrual cramps
- Lower risk of ovarian/endometrial cancer

**Disadvantages:**
- Must remember daily
- **Side effects:**
  - Nausea
  - Weight gain
  - Mood changes
  - Headaches
  - Slightly increased blood clot risk
- No STD protection
- Requires prescription

**Types:**
- Combined pills (oestrogen + progesterone)
- Progestin-only pills

---

**B. EMERGENCY CONTRACEPTION ("Morning After Pill")**

**Description:**
- High-dose hormone pill
- Taken after unprotected intercourse

**Timing:**
- Within 72 hours (3 days) - most effective
- Up to 120 hours (5 days) - less effective

**Mechanism:**
- Prevents/delays ovulation
- May prevent fertilization
- **Does NOT cause abortion** (ineffective if already pregnant)

**Effectiveness:** 75-89%

**Note:** Emergency use only, NOT regular method

---

**C. INJECTABLE CONTRACEPTIVES**

**Description:**
- Hormone injection (progesterone)
- Given every 3 months

**Effectiveness:** >99%

**Advantages:**
- Long-lasting
- Don't have to remember daily

**Disadvantages:**
- Irregular bleeding
- Weight gain
- Delayed return of fertility

---

#### CATEGORY 3: INTRAUTERINE DEVICES (IUDs)

**Mechanism:** Device inserted in uterus prevents pregnancy

---

**A. COPPER-T (Copper IUD)**

**Description:**
- T-shaped plastic device
- Wrapped with copper wire
- Inserted into uterus by doctor

**Mechanism:**
- Copper toxic to sperm
- Prevents fertilization
- Prevents implantation

**Duration:** 5-10 years

**Effectiveness:** >99%

**Advantages:**
- Long-lasting
- No hormones
- Reversible (removed anytime)
- No daily action needed

**Disadvantages:**
- Insertion discomfort
- Heavier/painful periods (for some)
- Small risk of uterine perforation
- Small risk of pelvic infection
- **NO STD protection**

---

**B. HORMONAL IUD**

**Similar to Copper-T but releases progesterone**

**Advantages over Copper-T:**
- Lighter/absent periods
- Fewer cramps

---

#### CATEGORY 4: SURGICAL METHODS (Permanent)

**Mechanism:** Block gamete transport permanently

---

**A. VASECTOMY (Male Sterilization)**

**Procedure:**
- Cut/seal/tie vas deferens
- Blocks sperm pathway
- Semen still ejaculated (no sperm in it)

**Procedure:**
- Local anesthesia
- Small incision in scrotum
- 15-20 minutes
- Minor surgery

**Effectiveness:** >99%

**Advantages:**
- Permanent
- No effect on sexual function
- Hormone levels unchanged

**Disadvantages:**
- **Permanent** (reversal difficult/expensive/not always successful)
- Surgery risks (infection, bleeding)
- Takes 3 months for sperm clearance
- **No STD protection**

---

**B. TUBECTOMY (Female Sterilization)**

**Procedure:**
- Cut/seal/tie fallopian tubes
- Blocks egg pathway
- Prevents sperm-egg meeting

**Procedure:**
- General anesthesia
- Small abdominal incisions (laparoscopy)
- 30 minutes

**Effectiveness:** >99%

**Advantages:**
- Permanent
- No effect on menstruation
- Hormone levels unchanged

**Disadvantages:**
- **Permanent** (reversal very difficult)
- More invasive than vasectomy
- Surgery risks
- **No STD protection**

---

- Protect from all STDs (except condoms - partial protection)
- Replace need for informed decision-making
- Guarantee 100% protection (nothing is 100%)

**Abortion:**
- Surgical removal of pregnancy
- **NOT a contraceptive method**

## 16. Common Misconceptions Clarified

### Misconception 1: Asexual Reproduction Produces Identical Offspring

**WRONG:** All asexually produced offspring are 100% identical
**CORRECT:**
- Similar but NOT absolutely identical
- DNA copying errors create small variations
- These variations accumulate over generations
- Identical twins (from same zygote) are more identical than asexual offspring

### Misconception 2: Regeneration is Reproduction

**WRONG:** Regeneration = reproductive strategy
**CORRECT:**
- Regeneration = repair ability
- Organisms don't depend on being cut
- Actual reproduction through other methods (budding in Hydra)
- Regeneration uses same regenerative cells

### Misconception 3: Sexual Reproduction Always Better

**WRONG:** Sexual reproduction superior in all situations
**CORRECT:**
- Each method has advantages
- **Asexual advantages:**
  - Faster
  - No need to find mate
  - Good in stable environments
- **Sexual advantages:**
  - More variation
  - Better for changing environments
- Choice depends on organism and environment

### Misconception 4: Seeds and Fruits Same Thing

**WRONG:** Seeds and fruits are the same
**CORRECT:**
- **Seed** = fertilized ovule (contains embryo)
- **Fruit** = ripened ovary (contains seeds)
- Fruit protects and disperses seeds
- One fruit can have many seeds

### Misconception 5: Menstruation = Body "Cleansing"

**WRONG:** Period is body getting rid of "impurities"
**CORRECT:**
- Shedding of uterine lining prepared for pregnancy
- Not impurities or waste
- Unused lining (embryo didn't implant)
- Natural, healthy process

### Misconception 6: Pills Cause Infertility

**WRONG:** Birth control pills cause permanent infertility
**CORRECT:**
- Pills prevent ovulation temporarily
- Effects reversible
- Fertility returns after stopping (may take few months)
- No long-term effect on fertility

### Misconception 7: Copper-T Prevents STDs

**WRONG:** IUD protects from sexually transmitted diseases
**CORRECT:**
- Copper-T/IUD only prevents pregnancy
- NO protection against STDs
- Only condoms provide STD protection
- Can use condom + another method together

### Misconception 8: Withdrawal Method Effective

**WRONG:** Pulling out before ejaculation is effective contraception
**CORRECT:**
- Very unreliable (~78% effectiveness at best)
- Pre-ejaculate can contain sperm
- Requires perfect timing and control
- NOT recommended as contraception

## 19. Exam Strategy

### 3-Mark Questions

**Q: Explain advantages of vegetative propagation.**
**Answer:**
1. Faster than seed propagation
2. Earlier flowering and fruiting
3. Can propagate seedless plants (banana, orange)
4. Maintains genetic uniformity (all characteristics preserved)
5. Can preserve hybrid varieties

**Q: What is the role of seminal vesicles and prostate?**
**Answer:**
1. Add secretions to sperm
2. Form semen
3. Provide nutrition (fructose) for sperm
4. Neutralize acidic environment (prostate secretion)
5. Enhance sperm motility

**Q: How does embryo get nourishment?**
**Answer:**
1. Through placenta
2. Placenta has villi on embryo side
3. Blood spaces on mother's side
4. Large surface area for exchange
5. Glucose and oxygen pass to embryo
6. Wastes pass to mother's blood

---

### 5-Mark Questions

**Q: Describe sexual reproduction in flowering plants.**
**Answer:**
1. **Pollination:** Pollen transfer from anther to stigma
2. **Pollen germination:** Pollen tube grows through style
3. **Fertilization:** Male gamete fuses with egg in ovule
4. **Double fertilization:** Second male gamete fuses with polar nuclei (forms endosperm)
5. **Seed formation:** Ovule develops seed coat, embryo forms
6. **Fruit formation:** Ovary ripens into fruit
7. **Dispersal:** Seeds dispersed, germinate under favorable conditions

**Q: Explain menstrual cycle with hormonal control.**
**Answer:**
1. **Menstrual phase (Day 1-5):** Uterine lining sheds; low hormones
2. **Follicular phase (Day 1-13):** FSH stimulates follicle; oestrogen rises; lining rebuilds
3. **Ovulation (Day 14):** LH surge; egg released
4. **Luteal phase (Day 15-28):** Corpus luteum forms; progesterone high; lining maintains
5. **If no fertilization:** Corpus luteum degenerates; hormone levels drop; cycle repeats
6. **If fertilization:** hCG maintains corpus luteum; no menstruation; pregnancy continues

## Summary: Core Principles

**1. Reproduction ensures species survival:**
- Not necessary for individual
- Essential for species continuation

**2. DNA copying is fundamental:**
- Creates similar offspring
- Errors create variation
- Variation enables evolution

**3. Two modes - different advantages:**
- **Asexual:** Fast, efficient, stable environments
- **Sexual:** Variation, adaptation, changing environments

**4. Sexual reproduction involves specialization:**
- Meiosis reduces chromosome number
- Gametes (sperm, egg) specialized
- Fertilization restores chromosome number

**5. Flowers have specialized parts:**
- Stamen (male) produces pollen
- Pistil (female) contains ovules
- Pollination → Fertilization → Seed/Fruit

**6. Human reproduction is complex:**
- Hormonal control (testosterone, oestrogen, progesterone)
- Monthly cycles in females
- Internal fertilization and development
- Long gestation period

**7. Reproductive health crucial:**
- Maturity ≠ readiness
- STD awareness and prevention
- Contraception for family planning
- Informed, responsible decisions
