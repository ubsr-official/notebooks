## SECTION D: CASE-BASED QUESTIONS [8 Marks]

**15.** **Case Study: Reproductive Strategies and Climate Change**

Dr. Sarah is studying the impact of climate change on reproductive strategies of various species in a forest ecosystem. Her research focuses on three different organisms:

**Species X (Plant)**: Uses both sexual reproduction (seeds) and asexual reproduction (vegetative propagation)
**Species Y (Insect)**: Traditionally reproduces once per year, synchronized with specific temperature cues
**Species Z (Mammal)**: Has a long gestation period and provides extensive parental care

**Climate Change Effects Observed:**
- Temperature increases by 2°C over 20 years
- Seasonal patterns become unpredictable
- Increased frequency of extreme weather events
- Food sources become less reliable

**Research Findings:**
- Species X has increased vegetative propagation by 40%
- Species Y now reproduces twice per year but with smaller broods
- Species Z shows delayed reproduction and reduced fertility

**Questions:**

**(a)** Explain why Species X has increased its reliance on vegetative propagation in response to climate change. What are the advantages and potential risks of this strategy? (3 marks)

**(b)** Analyze the reproductive changes observed in Species Y. How might these changes affect the species' long-term survival, and what does this suggest about the relationship between environmental predictability and reproductive timing? (3 marks)

**(c)** Evaluate the reproductive strategy changes in Species Z. Predict how continued climate change might affect this species and suggest potential conservation interventions that could help maintain reproductive success. (2 marks)

**9.** Sexual reproduction creates genetic diversity; Helps eliminate harmful mutations through recombination; Genetic load theory explains accumulation of deleterious mutations in asexual populations; Sexual reproduction purges these mutations

**10.** Stable conditions favor asexual reproduction (efficiency); Stress conditions favor sexual reproduction (creates adaptive variation); Permanently unstable environment would favor obligate sexual reproduction; Population would evolve increased genetic diversity

**11.** External fertilization: high gamete production, minimal parental care, aquatic environments; Internal fertilization: lower gamete production, high parental investment, terrestrial adaptation; Examples: fish vs mammals; Environmental constraints drive evolution of strategies

**12.** Valid claim in low-density populations; Simultaneous hermaphroditism in sparse populations; Sequential hermaphroditism when size/age affects reproductive success; Examples: earthworms, clownfish; Population structure and sexual selection intensity influence evolution
