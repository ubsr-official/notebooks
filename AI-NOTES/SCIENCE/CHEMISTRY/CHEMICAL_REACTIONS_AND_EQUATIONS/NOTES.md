### 1. Introduction: Observing Changes in Daily Life
**Definition/Key Concept:**
A **chemical reaction** is a process where substances transform into new substances with new properties (e.g., milk turning sour, rusting iron).

**Quick Overview:**
Recognizing chemical reactions is crucial since they occur everywhere, from cooking to digestion and respiration.

- → Signs of chemical reaction: change in state, color, temperature, gas evolution ([Activity 1.1], [Figure 1.1]).
- Example: Burning magnesium ribbon → dazzling light + new white powder.

**Footnote:**
Everyday changes often signal underlying chemical reactions, a central focus for deeper chemical understanding.

### 2. What is a Chemical Reaction?
**Definition:**
A process transforming reactants into products via rearrangement of atoms, forming substances with new properties.

**Quick Overview:**
Recognizing reactions involves careful observation (e.g., fizzing, odor, heat exchange).

- → **Reactants:** starting materials.
- → **Products:** new substances formed.
- "Magnesium + Oxygen → Magnesium oxide" (word equation)

**Flowchart:**
```
Reactants → (Chemical Change) → Products
                |                   |
           (Observation)        (Outcome)
```

**Footnote:**
Grasping this distinction helps in systematically studying all chemical changes.

### 3. Writing Chemical Equations
#### 3.1 Word Equations
- *Expresses chemical changes using substance names.*
- Example: Magnesium + Oxygen → Magnesium oxide

#### 3.2 Chemical Equations
- *Uses chemical formulae for brevity:*
    - \( \text{Mg} + \text{O}_2 → \text{MgO} \ )
- **Skeletal equation:** unbalanced form.

#### 3.3 Balanced Chemical Equations
- Law of conservation: Atoms on both sides must be equal.
    - Example: \( \text{Zn} + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 → \text{ZnSO}_4 + \text{H}_2 \ )
- **Balancing method:** Adjust coefficients, not subscripts.

**Footnote:**
Balanced equations succinctly represent real chemical transformations respecting conservation laws.

#### 3.4 Writing State Symbols
- (s) – solid, (l) – liquid, (g) – gas, (aq) – aqueous
- Example: \( 3\text{Fe(s)} + 4\text{H}_2\text{O(g)} → \text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4(s) + 4\text{H}_2(g)\ )

**Academic Tools:**

- **Example Table: Properties of States**

| Symbol | State       |
|--------|-------------|
| (s)    | Solid       |
| (l)    | Liquid      |
| (g)    | Gas         |
| (aq)   | Aqueous     |

**Footnote:**
Equations become more informative and universally relatable when formulae and state symbols are used.

### 4. Types of Chemical Reactions

#### 4.1 Combination Reactions
**Definition:**
Two or more reactants combine to form a single product.

**Quick Overview:**
Many synthesis and formation processes are combination reactions.

**Main Points:**
- \( \text{CaO(s)} + \text{H}_2\text{O(l)} → \text{Ca(OH)}_2(aq) + \text{Heat} \ )
- Burning coal: \( \text{C(s)} + \text{O}_2(g) → \text{CO}_2(g) \ )
- **Exothermic:** Releases heat (e.g., respiration, composting)

**Footnote:**
Combination reactions build complexity, forming new substances from simpler ingredients.

---

#### 4.2 Decomposition Reactions
**Definition:**
A single compound breaks down into two or more simpler substances.

**Quick Overview:**
Opposite of combination reactions, often requiring heat, light, or electricity.

**Main Points:**
- *Thermal decomposition:*
  - \( \text{CaCO}_3(s) \xrightarrow{\text{Heat}} \text{CaO(s)} + \text{CO}_2(g) \ )
- *Photolytic decomposition:*
  - \( 2\text{AgCl(s)} \xrightarrow{\text{Sunlight}} 2\text{Ag(s)} + \text{Cl}_2(g) \ )
- *Electrolytic decomposition:*
  - \( 2\text{H}_2\text{O(l)} \xrightarrow{\text{Electricity}} 2\text{H}_2(g) + \text{O}_2(g) \ )

**Footnote:**
Decomposition is essential for recycling matter in nature and industry.

---

#### 4.3 Displacement Reactions
**Definition:**
One element displaces another from its compound.

**Quick Overview:**
These demonstrate reactivity hierarchies among elements.

**Main Points:**
- \( \text{Fe(s)} + \text{CuSO}_4(aq) → \text{FeSO}_4(aq) + \text{Cu(s)} \ )
  - Iron replaces copper in copper sulfate.

**Footnote:**
Displacement reactions are foundational in metallurgy and extraction.

---

#### 4.4 Double Displacement Reactions
**Definition:**
Exchange of ions between two reactants to form new products.

**Quick Overview:**
Precipitation reactions are classic double displacement examples.

**Main Points:**
- \( \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4(aq) + \text{BaCl}_2(aq) → \text{BaSO}_4(s) + 2\text{NaCl}(aq) \ )
  - White precipitate of \( \text{BaSO}_4 \ )

**Table: Single vs. Double Displacement**

| Type                 | Ion Exchange      | Formation of Precipitate |
|----------------------|------------------|-------------------------|
| Displacement         | No               | Sometimes               |
| Double Displacement  | Yes              | Often                   |

**Footnote:**
Recognizing double displacement is crucial in identifying ionic exchanges in solution chemistry.

---

#### 4.5 Oxidation and Reduction (Redox)
**Definition:**
*Oxidation*: gain of oxygen or loss of hydrogen | *Reduction*: loss of oxygen or gain of hydrogen

**Quick Overview:**
Redox reactions drive extraction, corrosion, and biological processes.

**Main Points:**
- \( 2\text{Cu(s)} + \text{O}_2(g) \xrightarrow{\text{Heat}} 2\text{CuO(s)} \ )
- \( \text{CuO(s)} + \text{H}_2(g) \xrightarrow{\text{Heat}} \text{Cu(s)} + \text{H}_2\text{O(l)} \ )

**Footnote:**
Understanding redox underpins both chemical industries and living systems.

### 5. Effects of Oxidation in Everyday Life
#### 5.1 Corrosion
**Definition:**
Slow deterioration of metals due to exposure to moisture, air, or chemicals.

**Overview:**
Corrosion damages materials (e.g., rusting of iron, tarnishing of silver).

- Example: Iron rusts forming Fe2O3·xH2O

**Footnote:**
Combating corrosion is essential for preserving infrastructure.

#### 5.2 Rancidity
**Definition:**
Spoilage of fats/oils via oxidation, leading to bad taste/smell.

- Delayed by using antioxidants or nitrogen flushing (chip bags).

**Footnote:**
Rancidity illustrates the daily impact of chemical reactions on food preservation.
