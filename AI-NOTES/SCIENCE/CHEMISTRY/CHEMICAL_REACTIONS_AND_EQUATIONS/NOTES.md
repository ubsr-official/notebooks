# Chemical Reactions and Equations - Class 10 Chemistry Notes (CBSE)

## Quick Definitions List

**Chemical reaction** - Process where substances undergo chemical change; bonds break and form, creating new substances

**Reactants** - Starting substances that undergo change in a chemical reaction; written on left side of equation

**Products** - New substances formed as result of chemical reaction; written on right side of equation

**Word equation** - Chemical reaction written using names of substances; easy to understand but not precise

**Skeletal equation** - Chemical equation using formulae but not balanced; atoms not equal on both sides

**Balanced equation** - Equation where number of atoms of each element is same on both sides; follows mass conservation

**Chemical equation** - Symbolic representation of chemical reaction using formulae and symbols

**Law of conservation of mass** - Mass can neither be created nor destroyed in chemical reaction; total mass remains constant

**Coefficient** - Number placed before formula in equation to balance it; indicates number of molecules/formula units

**Physical state symbols** - (s) solid, (l) liquid, (g) gas, (aq) aqueous (dissolved in water)

**Combination reaction** - Two or more reactants combine to form single product; A + B → AB

**Decomposition reaction** - Single reactant breaks down into two or more products; AB → A + B

**Thermal decomposition** - Decomposition reaction that requires heat energy; heating breaks bonds

**Electrolysis** - Decomposition using electrical energy; electric current breaks compounds

**Photolysis** - Decomposition using light energy; light provides energy to break bonds

**Displacement reaction** - More reactive element displaces less reactive element from its compound; A + BC → AC + B

**Double displacement reaction** - Exchange of ions between two compounds; AB + CD → AD + CB

**Precipitation reaction** - Reaction that produces insoluble solid (precipitate); special type of double displacement

**Precipitate** - Insoluble solid formed during reaction; settles down or remains suspended

**Exothermic reaction** - Reaction that releases heat energy; products have less energy than reactants

**Endothermic reaction** - Reaction that absorbs heat energy; products have more energy than reactants

**Oxidation** - Gain of oxygen OR loss of hydrogen OR loss of electrons

**Reduction** - Loss of oxygen OR gain of hydrogen OR gain of electrons

**Redox reaction** - Reaction involving both oxidation and reduction simultaneously; electron transfer occurs

**Oxidizing agent** - Substance that causes oxidation while getting reduced itself; accepts electrons

**Reducing agent** - Substance that causes reduction while getting oxidized itself; donates electrons

**Corrosion** - Degradation of metal due to reaction with environment (moisture, acids, oxygen)

**Rusting** - Corrosion of iron; forms hydrated iron(III) oxide (reddish-brown)

**Rancidity** - Oxidation of fats and oils; causes bad smell and taste

**Antioxidant** - Substance that prevents oxidation; added to foods to prevent rancidity

! In ANY chemical reaction:
! - Atoms are NEVER created or destroyed
! - Atoms are only REARRANGED
! - Total mass BEFORE = Total mass AFTER
! - This is why we MUST balance equations
! Breaking this law = Breaking fundamental physics

## 1. What IS a Chemical Reaction? - The Deep Understanding

### Beyond the Definition

**NCERT tells you:** Chemical reaction = change in nature and identity of substances

**What NCERT doesn't fully explain:**

```diff
! At the ATOMIC level, chemical reactions involve:
! 1. Breaking of existing chemical bonds (requires energy)
! 2. Rearrangement of atoms
! 3. Formation of new chemical bonds (releases energy)
! 
! The bonds break and form, NOT the atoms themselves
! Atoms are indestructible in chemical reactions
! Only their ARRANGEMENT changes
```

**The energy perspective:**

```
Chemical reaction = Energy transformation process

Every reaction:
- Requires energy to BREAK old bonds (endothermic step)
- Releases energy when FORMING new bonds (exothermic step)

Overall reaction:
- If energy released > energy absorbed → Exothermic (net energy out)
- If energy absorbed > energy released → Endothermic (net energy in)
```

### How to Identify a Chemical Reaction - The Observable Signs

**From Activities 1.1, 1.2, 1.3:**

**Sign 1: Change in state**

```
Example: Ice melting → Physical change (still H₂O)
         Water → steam → Physical change (still H₂O)
       
But: CaCO₃(s) → CaO(s) + CO₂(g)
     Solid limestone → Solid lime + Gas
     This IS chemical (new substances formed)

The key: Same substance in different form = Physical
         New substance formed = Chemical
```

**Sign 2: Change in color**

```
Example: Blue CuSO₄ solution + Fe nail
         Blue color fades → brown coating on nail
       
Why color change indicates reaction:
- Different substances absorb different wavelengths of light
- New substance = Different electronic structure
- Different electronic structure = Different color
- Color change often means new substance formed
```

**Sign 3: Evolution of gas**

```
Visible as:
- Bubbles/effervescence
- Fizzing
- Frothing

Example: Zn + H₂SO₄ → ZnSO₄ + H₂↑
         Bubbles = H₂ gas escaping
         Gas wasn't there before
         Must be product of reaction
```

**Sign 4: Change in temperature**

```
Reaction mixture gets:
- Warmer → Exothermic (energy released)
- Cooler → Endothermic (energy absorbed)

Example from Activity 1.4:
CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + HEAT
Beaker becomes hot (exothermic)

Why temperature changes:
- Bond breaking/making involves energy transfer
- Energy appears as heat
- Measurable temperature change
```

**Sign 5: Formation of precipitate**

```
Precipitate = Insoluble solid forming in solution

Example: Na₂SO₄(aq) + BaCl₂(aq) → BaSO₄(s)↓ + 2NaCl(aq)
         Clear solutions → White solid appears

Why precipitate forms:
- Some salts insoluble in water
- When ions meet, form insoluble compound
- Solid separates from solution
- Clear evidence of new substance
```

### 2.2 Chemical Formulae - The Precision Tool

**Same reaction in formulae:**

```
Mg + O₂ → MgO

Advantages:
✓ Concise
✓ Universal (language-independent)
✓ Shows actual molecules involved
✓ Can include state symbols

But there's a problem...
```

**The problem - Unbalanced equation:**

```
Mg + O₂ → MgO

Left side (Reactants):
- Mg atoms: 1
- O atoms: 2

Right side (Products):
- Mg atoms: 1
- O atoms: 1

Atoms NOT equal! (2 O atoms → 1 O atom?)
This violates conservation of mass!
This is a SKELETAL equation (incorrect)
```

---

### 2.3 Balanced Equations - Respecting Nature's Laws

**What does "balanced" mean?**

```diff
! Balanced equation has:
! - SAME number of atoms of EACH element on both sides
! - This ensures mass conservation
! - Represents what ACTUALLY happens
! 
! Unbalanced equation is:
! - Mathematically wrong
! - Physically impossible
! - Violates fundamental law
! - Must NEVER be left unbalanced
```

**How atoms are conserved:**

```
Think of atoms as Lego blocks:

Before reaction: You have certain Lego blocks
During reaction: You rearrange them into new structures
After reaction: You still have SAME blocks, just different arrangement

You can't CREATE Lego blocks from nothing
You can't DESTROY Lego blocks
You can only REARRANGE them

Same with atoms in chemical reactions!
```

## 3. Balancing Chemical Equations - The Method and Logic

### The Law Behind Balancing

**Law of Conservation of Mass (Lavoisier, 1789):**

```
"In a chemical reaction, matter is neither created nor destroyed"

Mathematical form:
Mass of reactants = Mass of products

Since atoms have mass:
Number of atoms (reactants) = Number of atoms (products)

This is WHY we balance equations!
```

---

### The Hit-and-Trial Method - Step by Step

**Example: Fe + H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + H₂**

**Step 1: Never change formulae**

```diff
! CRITICAL RULE:
! - Draw mental boxes around each formula
! - NEVER change what's inside boxes
! - Can only add coefficients BEFORE boxes
! 
! ✓ Correct: 2H₂O (two water molecules)
! ✗ Wrong: H₂O₂ (completely different compound - hydrogen peroxide!)
! 
! Changing formula = Changing the reaction itself
! You'll get wrong products!
```

**Step 2: Count atoms**

```
Fe + H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + H₂

Element  |  Reactants (LHS)  |  Products (RHS)
---------|-------------------|------------------
Fe       |  1                |  3               ❌
H        |  2                |  2               ✓
O        |  1                |  4               ❌

Two elements unbalanced: Fe and O
```

**Step 3: Start with compound having most atoms**

```
Fe₃O₄ has most atoms (7 total)
Pick oxygen in Fe₃O₄ (4 atoms)

Strategy: Balance oxygen first
```

**Step 4: Balance oxygen**

```
Right side has 4 O atoms
Left side has 1 O atom

Solution: Put coefficient 4 before H₂O

Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + H₂

Now recount oxygen:
Left: 4 (in 4H₂O)
Right: 4 (in Fe₃O₄)
Oxygen balanced! ✓
```

**Step 5: Check hydrogen**

```
Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + H₂

Hydrogen count:
Left: 8 (4 × 2 in 4H₂O)
Right: 2 (in H₂)

Not balanced!
Need 8 H on right side
Solution: Coefficient 4 before H₂

Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂

Now hydrogen:
Left: 8
Right: 8 ✓
```

**Step 6: Balance iron**

```
Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂

Iron count:
Left: 1
Right: 3

Solution: Coefficient 3 before Fe

3Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂
```

**Step 7: Final verification**

```
3Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂

Element  |  Reactants  |  Products
---------|-------------|------------
Fe       |  3          |  3         ✓
H        |  8          |  8         ✓
O        |  4          |  4         ✓

BALANCED! ✓✓✓
```

---

### Why This Method Works - The Logic

**Understanding coefficients:**

```
4H₂O means:
- FOUR molecules of water
- Each has 2 H and 1 O
- Total: 8 H atoms and 4 O atoms

NOT:
- Some weird compound with 4 hydrogens and 1 oxygen
- Just 4 separate H₂O molecules

Coefficient = Number of molecules/formula units
It's like saying "4 apples" not "an apple with 4 stems"
```

**Why we start with complex compounds:**

```
Complex compound (like Fe₃O₄) has:
- Multiple atoms of multiple elements
- Balancing it affects several elements at once
- Do this FIRST to avoid repeated adjustments
- Then balance remaining simpler molecules

Simple first → Complex later = More trial and error needed
Complex first → Simple later = Faster convergence
```

**The cascade effect:**

```
Balancing one element affects others:

Balance O: Change H₂O coefficient
↓
This changes H count
↓
Must rebalance H
↓
This might affect other elements
↓
Check and adjust iteratively

It's a system of linked equations
Changes propagate through
Must verify ALL elements at end
```

---

### Common Balancing Mistakes

**Mistake 1: Changing formulae**

```diff
- Wrong: Fe + H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + H₂
-        To balance O: Fe + H₄O → Fe₃O₄ + H₂
- 
+ Correct: Fe + H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + H₂
+          To balance O: Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + H₂

! H₄O doesn't exist! Changed formula = Wrong compound
! Use coefficients, never change subscripts
```

**Mistake 2: Using fractions**

```
Technically allowed in chemistry, but:
- Avoid in Class 10 (marks may be deducted)
- Always convert to whole numbers
- Multiply entire equation by denominator

Example:
H₂ + ½O₂ → H₂O (has fraction)
Multiply by 2:
2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O (all whole numbers) ✓
```

**Mistake 3: Not verifying at end**

```diff
! ALWAYS count all atoms after balancing
! One mistake can throw everything off
! Verify EVERY element
! Don't assume it's correct

! Common trap: Balance 3 elements, forget to check 4th
! Always do final comprehensive check
```

**Mistake 4: Giving up too soon**

```
Balancing can take 3-4 attempts
This is NORMAL
Don't get frustrated
Systematic approach always works

If stuck:
1. Start over
2. Try different order
3. Balance polyatomic ions as units (SO₄²⁻, NO₃⁻)
4. Check arithmetic
```

## 4. Physical States and Reaction Conditions - Making Equations Informative

### Physical State Symbols

**The four states:**

```
(s) = Solid
(l) = Liquid  
(g) = Gas
(aq) = Aqueous (dissolved in water)

Example:
3Fe(s) + 4H₂O(g) → Fe₃O₄(s) + 4H₂(g)

This tells us:
- Iron is solid metal
- Water is steam (gas form)
- Iron oxide is solid
- Hydrogen is gas
```

**Why (aq) is important:**

```
NaCl(s) = Solid sodium chloride (table salt)
NaCl(aq) = Sodium chloride dissolved in water (salt solution)

These are DIFFERENT:
- Solid: Ions in crystal lattice, not mobile
- Aqueous: Ions free in solution, mobile, can conduct electricity

Reaction behavior changes:
NaCl(s) + AgNO₃(s) → No reaction (both solid)
NaCl(aq) + AgNO₃(aq) → AgCl(s)↓ + NaNO₃(aq) (Reaction occurs!)

(aq) indicates ions are FREE to react
```

**Special notation for water:**

```
H₂O(l) = Liquid water
H₂O(g) = Steam/water vapor

Example showing importance:
3Fe(s) + 4H₂O(l) → Different products (rust formation)
3Fe(s) + 4H₂O(g) → Fe₃O₄(s) + 4H₂(g) (As in equation)

State matters! Different products possible
```

```
Written above/below arrow:

      1000°C

Tells us:

```

**Pressure:**

```

```

Catalyst:

- Written above arrow

**Sunlight:**

- Photochemical reaction

## 5. Types of Chemical Reactions - Classification and Understanding

### 5.1 Combination Reactions - Coming Together

**Definition:** Two or more substances combine to form single product

**General form:**

```
A + B → AB
or
A + B + C → ABC
```

**Examples with explanations:**

**Example 1: CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + Heat**

```
Calcium oxide + Water → Calcium hydroxide
(Quick lime)            (Slaked lime)

What happens:
- CaO is ionic: Ca²⁺ and O²⁻
- Water molecules attack oxide ions
- Forms hydroxide ions
- Very exothermic (lot of heat)
- Used in whitewashing

Observation:
- Hissing sound
- Mixture becomes very hot
- White powder forms paste
- Can cause burns (handle carefully)
```

**Example 2: C + O₂ → CO₂**

```
Carbon + Oxygen → Carbon dioxide

Burning of coal/charcoal:
- Carbon atoms react with O₂
- Forms CO₂ gas
- Releases lot of energy (heat + light)
- This is combustion

Complete combustion → CO₂
Incomplete combustion → CO (dangerous!)
```

**Example 3: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O**

```
Hydrogen + Oxygen → Water

Explosive reaction:
- Needs spark to start
- Then very vigorous
- Lot of energy released
- Used in rocket fuel

This is why:
- Hydrogen balloons dangerous
- Hindenburg disaster (1937)
- Hydrogen highly flammable
```

---

**Characteristics of combination reactions:**

```diff
! Usually EXOTHERMIC
! Why? 
! - Breaking 2 bonds (in separate molecules)
! - Forming 3+ bonds (in combined molecule)
! - More bonds formed than broken
! - Net energy released
! 
! Exceptions exist, but most combinations are exothermic
```

**Real-world applications:**

```
1. Cement setting:
   CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ (first step)
   Then: Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃ (hardens)

2. Formation of ammonia (Haber process):
   N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃ (fertilizer production)

3. Photosynthesis:
   6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
   (Combination of CO₂ and H₂O)

4. Respiration (reverse of photosynthesis):
   C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Energy
```

### 5.2 Decomposition Reactions - Breaking Apart

**Definition:** Single substance breaks down into two or more simpler substances

**General form:**

```
AB → A + B
or
ABC → AB + C
or
ABC → A + B + C
```

**Why decomposition needs energy:**

```diff
! To break compound apart, must break chemical bonds
! Bond breaking ALWAYS requires energy (endothermic step)
! Energy can be supplied as:
! - Heat (thermal decomposition)
! - Light (photolysis)
! - Electricity (electrolysis)
! 
! Most decompositions are ENDOTHERMIC overall
! (energy absorbed > energy released)
```

---

**Type 1: Thermal Decomposition (Heat)**

**Example 1: FeSO₄ decomposition (Activity 1.5)**

```
         Heat
2FeSO₄ ------→ Fe₂O₃ + SO₂ + SO₃

Ferrous sulfate → Ferric oxide + Sulfur dioxide + Sulfur trioxide

Observations:
- Green crystals → Reddish brown solid
- Smell of burning sulfur (SO₂ has pungent smell)
- Color change proves new substance

Why color changes:
Fe²⁺ (ferrous) - pale green
Fe³⁺ (ferric) - reddish brown
Oxidation occurred: Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺
```

**The full story NCERT doesn't tell:**

```
FeSO₄.7H₂O crystals have water of crystallization

Step 1: Gentle heating
FeSO₄.7H₂O → FeSO₄ + 7H₂O↑ (water evaporates)
Green → Light green (becomes anhydrous)

Step 2: Strong heating
2FeSO₄ → Fe₂O₃ + SO₂ + SO₃
Light green → Reddish brown

It's actually TWO decompositions:
1. Dehydration (losing water)
2. Decomposition (breaking compound)
```

**Example 2: Pb(NO₃)₂ decomposition (Activity 1.6)**

```
          Heat
2Pb(NO₃)₂ ------→ 2PbO + 4NO₂ + O₂

Lead nitrate → Lead oxide + Nitrogen dioxide + Oxygen

Observations:
- White crystals → Yellow powder (PbO)
- Brown fumes (NO₂ - toxic!)
- Crackling sound

Why brown fumes:
NO₂ is brown gas
Toxic (don't inhale!)
This is why reaction needs teacher supervision
```

**Example 3: CaCO₃ decomposition**

```
         Heat
CaCO₃ -------→ CaO + CO₂

Limestone → Quicklime + Carbon dioxide

Industrial importance:
- Cement manufacturing
- Lime production
- Requires ~1000°C
- Large scale in lime kilns

Reversible:
CaO + CO₂ → CaCO₃ (combination)
This is how cement hardens!
```

**Type 2: Electrolysis (Electricity)**

**Water electrolysis (Activity 1.7):**

```
         Electricity
2H₂O(l) ------------→ 2H₂(g) + O₂(g)

What happens at electrodes:

At CATHODE (negative electrode):
2H₂O + 2e⁻ → H₂ + 2OH⁻
Reduction (gaining electrons)
Hydrogen gas collected

At ANODE (positive electrode):
2H₂O → O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻
Oxidation (losing electrons)
Oxygen gas collected

Volume ratio: H₂ : O₂ = 2 : 1
(Matches formula H₂O - twice as much hydrogen!)
```

**Why H₂SO₄ is added:**

```diff
! Pure water doesn't conduct electricity well
! Very few ions present
! Add dilute H₂SO₄:
! - Provides H⁺ and SO₄²⁻ ions
! - Increases conductivity
! - Electrolysis occurs faster
! 
! H₂SO₄ is NOT consumed (acts as electrolyte)
! Only water decomposes
```

**Tests for gases:**

```
Hydrogen (H₂):
- Bring burning splint near gas
- Burns with 'pop' sound
- 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O + Energy (mini explosion)
- Confirms H₂

Oxygen (O₂):
- Bring glowing splint (not flaming)
- Splint bursts into flames
- O₂ supports combustion
- Confirms O₂
```

**Type 3: Photolysis (Light)**

**Example 1: Silver chloride (Activity 1.8)**

```
          Sunlight
2AgCl(s) ----------→ 2Ag(s) + Cl₂(g)

White → Grey/Black

Observations:
- White AgCl darkens in sunlight
- Becomes grey (silver metal)
- Chlorine gas released (can't see, small amount)

Why this happens:
- Light photons provide energy
- Energy breaks Ag-Cl bonds
- Silver atoms aggregate (grey/black color)
- Used in old black & white photography
```

**The photography connection:**

```
Old photographic film:
- Coated with AgBr (silver bromide)
- Light from image hits film
- AgBr decomposes where light hits
- Forms metallic silver (dark areas)
- Creates negative image
- Dark where light, light where dark

Digital cameras replaced this!
```

**Example 2: Silver bromide**

```
          Sunlight
2AgBr(s) ----------→ 2Ag(s) + Br₂(g)

Same principle as AgCl
AgBr more sensitive to light
Better for photography
```

### 5.3 Displacement Reactions - The Takeover

**Definition:** More reactive element displaces less reactive element from its compound

**General form:**

```
A + BC → AC + B
(A more reactive than B)
```

**From Activity 1.9: Fe + CuSO₄**

```
Fe(s) + CuSO₄(aq) → FeSO₄(aq) + Cu(s)

Step-by-step what happens:

Initial state:
- Iron nail: Grey metallic Fe atoms
- Solution: Blue (Cu²⁺ ions dissolved)

As reaction proceeds:
- Fe atoms lose electrons: Fe → Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻
- Cu²⁺ ions gain electrons: Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu
- Fe goes into solution (Fe²⁺)
- Cu deposits on nail (metallic)

Final state:
- Iron nail: Brown coating (Cu metal)
- Solution: Pale green (Fe²⁺ ions)
- Blue color faded (less Cu²⁺)
```

**Why iron displaces copper:**

```diff
! Reactivity Series (partial):
! K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > H > Cu > Ag > Au
! (Most reactive)                              (Least reactive)
! 
! Rule: Metal higher in series displaces metal lower in series
! 
! Fe is ABOVE Cu in series
! Therefore: Fe can displace Cu
! But: Cu CANNOT displace Fe
```

**More examples:**

```
1. Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu
   (Zn more reactive than Cu)
   Blue solution → Colorless
   Brown Cu deposits

2. Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂
   (Zn more reactive than H)
   Bubbles of H₂ gas
   Zn dissolves

3. Cu + 2AgNO₃ → Cu(NO₃)₂ + 2Ag
   (Cu more reactive than Ag)
   Colorless → Blue solution
   Silver metal deposits

4. Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂
   (Mg very reactive)
   Vigorous reaction
   Rapid H₂ evolution
```

**What DOESN'T happen:**

```
Cu + ZnSO₄ → NO REACTION
(Cu less reactive than Zn)

Ag + CuSO₄ → NO REACTION
(Ag less reactive than Cu)

Au + HCl → NO REACTION
(Au less reactive than H, won't displace it)
```

---

**Applications:**

```
1. Metal extraction:
   Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂
   (Carbon displaces iron from oxide)
   
2. Thermite reaction:
   Fe₂O₃ + 2Al → 2Fe + Al₂O₃
   (Al very reactive, displaces Fe)
   (So exothermic, molten Fe produced)
   (Used to weld railway tracks)

3. Recovery of silver:
   2AgNO₃(aq) + Cu(s) → Cu(NO₃)₂(aq) + 2Ag(s)
   (Cu cheaper than Ag, used to recover Ag)
```

### 5.4 Double Displacement Reactions - The Exchange

**Definition:** Two compounds exchange ions/groups

**General form:**

```
AB + CD → AD + CB

(A and C exchange places)
```

**From Activity 1.10: Na₂SO₄ + BaCl₂**

```
Na₂SO₄(aq) + BaCl₂(aq) → BaSO₄(s)↓ + 2NaCl(aq)

In ionic form:
2Na⁺ + SO₄²⁻ + Ba²⁺ + 2Cl⁻ → BaSO₄(s) + 2Na⁺ + 2Cl⁻

What's exchanged:
- Ba²⁺ (was with Cl⁻) + SO₄²⁻ (was with Na⁺) → BaSO₄
- Na⁺ (was with SO₄²⁻) + Cl⁻ (was with Ba²⁺) → NaCl

Partners swapped!
```

**Why precipitate forms:**

```
Solubility rules:

BaCl₂ - Soluble (stays dissolved)
Na₂SO₄ - Soluble (stays dissolved)
NaCl - Soluble (stays dissolved)
BaSO₄ - INSOLUBLE (precipitates!)

When Ba²⁺ meets SO₄²⁻:
- Strong ionic attraction
- Very low solubility product (Ksp)
- Ions come together
- Form solid crystal
- Precipitates out

This is PRECIPITATION REACTION
(Special type of double displacement)
```

---

**More examples:**

**Example 1: Neutralization**

```
HCl(aq) + NaOH(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l)

Ionic view:
H⁺ + Cl⁻ + Na⁺ + OH⁻ → Na⁺ + Cl⁻ + H₂O

Exchange:
- H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O
- Na⁺ + Cl⁻ → NaCl

All neutralizations are double displacement!
```

**Example 2: From Activity 1.2**

```
Pb(NO₃)₂(aq) + 2KI(aq) → PbI₂(s)↓ + 2KNO₃(aq)

Observations:
- Colorless + Colorless → Yellow precipitate
- PbI₂ is bright yellow
- Insoluble in water
- Beautiful reaction to watch!

Exchange:
- Pb²⁺ was with NO₃⁻, now with I⁻
- K⁺ was with I⁻, now with NO₃⁻
```

**Example 3: Making insoluble salts**

```
AgNO₃(aq) + NaCl(aq) → AgCl(s)↓ + NaNO₃(aq)

White curdy precipitate:
- AgCl (silver chloride)
- Insoluble
- Used in photography
```

---

**Identifying the type:**

```diff
! How to know if double displacement:
! 
! 1. Two compounds reacting (not elements)
! 2. Two compounds forming (as products)
! 3. Ions/groups exchanged between them
! 
! Pattern: AB + CD → AD + CB
! 
! Special cases:
! - If precipitate forms → Precipitation reaction
! - If water forms → Neutralization
! - If gas evolves → Can still be double displacement!
```

**Example with gas evolution:**

```
Na₂CO₃(aq) + 2HCl(aq) → 2NaCl(aq) + H₂CO₃(aq)
H₂CO₃ → H₂O + CO₂(g)↑

Overall:
Na₂CO₃ + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H₂O + CO₂↑

This is double displacement!
(CO₃²⁻ and Cl⁻ exchanged)
(H₂CO₃ unstable, decomposes to CO₂)
```

### 5.5 Redox Reactions - Electron Transfer

**What oxidation and reduction REALLY are:**

**Three definitions (all correct, different perspectives):**

```
1. OXYGEN PERSPECTIVE:
   Oxidation = Gain of oxygen
   Reduction = Loss of oxygen

2. HYDROGEN PERSPECTIVE:
   Oxidation = Loss of hydrogen
   Reduction = Gain of hydrogen

3. ELECTRON PERSPECTIVE (most fundamental):
   Oxidation = Loss of electrons
   Reduction = Gain of electrons
```

---

**From Activity 1.11: Cu + O₂**

```
          Heat
2Cu(s) + O₂(g) ----→ 2CuO(s)

What happens:
- Shiny copper turns black
- Black coating = Copper(II) oxide
- Copper GAINED oxygen
- Therefore: Copper is OXIDIZED

At atomic level:
Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ (Oxidation - loses electrons)
O₂ + 4e⁻ → 2O²⁻ (Reduction - gains electrons)

Both happen simultaneously!
```

**The reverse reaction:**

```
         Heat
CuO + H₂ ----→ Cu + H₂O

What happens:
- Black CuO turns brown (metallic Cu)
- Water vapor forms
- Copper LOST oxygen (Reduction)
- Hydrogen GAINED oxygen (Oxidation)

At atomic level:
Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu (Reduction - gains electrons)
H₂ → 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ (Oxidation - loses electrons)

One reduced, one oxidized = REDOX
```

---

**Why they always occur together:**

```diff
! Fundamental principle:
! Electrons cannot be created or destroyed
! 
! If one substance LOSES electrons (oxidized)
! Another substance must GAIN those electrons (reduced)
! 
! Oxidation and reduction are TWO SIDES of same process
! Always occur TOGETHER
! Hence: REDOX (REDuction-OXidation)
```

---

**Identifying oxidation and reduction:**

**Example 1: ZnO + C → Zn + CO**

```
Step 1: Identify oxygen changes
- ZnO loses oxygen → Zn (REDUCTION)
- C gains oxygen → CO (OXIDATION)

Step 2: Name the substances
- ZnO is REDUCED (loses O)
- C is OXIDIZED (gains O)

Step 3: Identify agents
- C causes reduction of ZnO → C is REDUCING AGENT
- ZnO causes oxidation of C → ZnO is OXIDIZING AGENT

Remember:
Reducing agent gets oxidized (itself)
Oxidizing agent gets reduced (itself)
```

**Example 2: MnO₂ + 4HCl → MnCl₂ + 2H₂O + Cl₂**

```
Oxygen/Hydrogen perspective:

MnO₂ → MnCl₂:
- Mn lost oxygen (O₂ → nothing)
- Mn is REDUCED

HCl → Cl₂:
- H lost (HCl → Cl₂)
- Cl is OXIDIZED

Electron perspective:
Mn⁴⁺ + 2e⁻ → Mn²⁺ (Reduction)
2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻ (Oxidation)
```

**Example 3: 4Na + O₂ → 2Na₂O**

```
Oxygen perspective:
- Na gained oxygen → OXIDIZED
- O₂ caused oxidation → OXIDIZING AGENT

Electron perspective:
Na → Na⁺ + e⁻ (Oxidation - loss of electron)
O₂ + 4e⁻ → 2O²⁻ (Reduction - gain of electrons)
```

---

**The memory tricks:**

```
OIL RIG:
Oxidation Is Loss (of electrons)
Reduction Is Gain (of electrons)

LEO GER:
Loss of Electrons = Oxidation
Gain of Electrons = Reduction

Mnemonic for oxygen:
"Oxygen Joins = Oxidation"
"Oxygen Leaves = Reduction"
```

## 6. Energy Changes in Reactions

### 6.1 Exothermic Reactions - Energy Givers

**Definition:** Reactions that release heat energy

**Why heat is released:**

```
Bond breaking: Requires energy (endothermic)
Bond forming: Releases energy (exothermic)

In exothermic reaction:
Energy released in forming new bonds
>
Energy required to break old bonds

Net result: Energy released to surroundings
Appears as heat
Temperature increases
```

**From Activity 1.4:**

```
CaO(s) + H₂O(l) → Ca(OH)₂(aq) + Heat

Observations:
- Beaker becomes hot (can feel it)
- Hissing sound (vigorous reaction)
- Steam may rise (if enough heat)

Temperature rises because:
- Forming Ca-OH bonds releases energy
- More than energy needed to break Ca-O and H-H bonds
- Excess energy = heat
```

---

**More examples:**

**Example 1: Burning of natural gas**

```
CH₄(g) + 2O₂(g) → CO₂(g) + 2H₂O(g) + Heat

Used for:
- Cooking (gas stove)
- Heating homes
- Power generation

Why exothermic:
- Breaking: C-H, O=O bonds
- Forming: C=O, H-O bonds
- C=O and H-O bonds stronger
- Net energy released
- This is COMBUSTION
```

**Example 2: Respiration**

```
C₆H₁₂O₆(aq) + 6O₂(aq) → 6CO₂(aq) + 6H₂O(l) + Energy

This is why:
- Our body stays warm (37°C)
- We need food (fuel)
- We need oxygen (oxidizer)
- Like slow burning of glucose

Energy released:
- Captured in ATP molecules
- Used for all body processes
- Heat keeps body temperature stable
```

**Example 3: Thermite reaction**

```
Fe₂O₃ + 2Al → 2Fe + Al₂O₃ + Huge heat

So exothermic:
- Temperature reaches 2500°C
- Iron melts (m.p. 1538°C)
- Liquid iron flows
- Used to weld railway tracks
```

---

**Characteristics of exothermic reactions:**

```diff
! Observable signs:
! ✓ Temperature increases
! ✓ Heat felt on touching container
! ✓ May give light (flames)
! ✓ Mixture feels warm/hot
! 
! Examples:
! - All combustions
! - Most combinations
! - Neutralizations
! - Respiration
! - Many oxidations
```

---

### 6.2 Endothermic Reactions - Energy Takers

**Definition:** Reactions that absorb heat energy

**Why heat is absorbed:**

```
In endothermic reaction:
Energy required to break old bonds
>
Energy released in forming new bonds

Net result: Energy absorbed from surroundings
Taken as heat
Temperature decreases
```

**Example from text:**

```
Ba(OH)₂ + NH₄Cl + Heat → BaCl₂ + 2NH₃ + 2H₂O

Observations:
- Test tube becomes cold
- Can feel cooling on palm
- Sometimes ice forms on outside!

Why cold:
- Reaction absorbs heat from surroundings
- Surroundings include test tube and your hand
- Heat flows from warm (hand) to cold (reaction)
- You feel cooling
```

---

**More examples:**

**Example 1: Photosynthesis**

```
            Sunlight
6CO₂ + 6H₂O --------→ C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

Energy absorbed from:
- Sunlight (photons)
- Stored in glucose molecules
- Glucose has more energy than CO₂ + H₂O
- This stored energy we get when eating plants

Reverse of respiration!
```

**Example 2: Decompositions**

```
Most decompositions are endothermic:

        Heat
2FeSO₄ ----→ Fe₂O₃ + SO₂ + SO₃
(Need to keep heating)

         Heat
CaCO₃ -------→ CaO + CO₂
(Need high temperature ~1000°C)

Why endothermic:
- Breaking apart requires breaking bonds
- Bond breaking needs energy
- No compensating bond formation
- Net energy absorbed
```

**Example 3: Evaporation of water**

```
H₂O(l) + Heat → H₂O(g)

Not chemical change, but:
- Need energy to break H-bonds between molecules
- Energy absorbed
- This is why sweating cools us
- Evaporating sweat takes heat from skin
```

---

**Comparison:**

| Property                 | Exothermic                  | Endothermic                             |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| **Energy flow**    | Released to surroundings    | Absorbed from surroundings              |
| **Temperature**    | Increases                   | Decreases                               |
| **Feel**           | Warm/hot                    | Cold/cool                               |
| **Energy diagram** | Products lower energy       | Products higher energy                  |
| **Examples**       | Combustion, respiration     | Photosynthesis, decomposition           |
| **Common types**   | Combination, neutralization | Decomposition, some double displacement |

## 7. Effects of Oxidation - Real World Problems

### 7.1 Corrosion - The Slow Destroyer

**What is corrosion:**

```diff
! Corrosion = Slow oxidation of metals by environment
! 
! Environment includes:
! - Moisture (water vapor)
! - Oxygen (from air)
! - Acids (from pollution)
! - Salt (near sea)
! 
! Result: Metal surface degrades
! Forms metal oxides/hydroxides/carbonates
! Weakens structure
```

---

**Rusting of iron - The complete chemistry:**

**Simple equation:**

```
4Fe + 3O₂ + xH₂O → 2Fe₂O₃.xH₂O
(Iron) (Oxygen) (Water)   (Rust - hydrated iron oxide)
```

**What actually happens (step by step):**

```
Step 1: Formation of iron(II)
Fe → Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻ (at anodic areas)
O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻ (at cathodic areas)
Fe²⁺ + 2OH⁻ → Fe(OH)₂ (iron(II) hydroxide)

Step 2: Oxidation to iron(III)
4Fe(OH)₂ + O₂ + 2H₂O → 4Fe(OH)₃
(Iron(II) hydroxide → Iron(III) hydroxide)

Step 3: Dehydration to rust
2Fe(OH)₃ → Fe₂O₃.H₂O + 2H₂O
(Reddish-brown rust)
```

**Conditions needed for rusting:**

```diff
! BOTH needed:
! 1. Oxygen (O₂)
! 2. Water (H₂O)
! 
! Remove either → No rusting
! 
! Evidence:
! - Iron in dry air (no water) → No rust
! - Iron in boiled water (no O₂) → No rust
! - Iron in both present → Rusts quickly
```

**Factors affecting rate:**

```
Faster rusting with:
1. More moisture (humid climates)
2. Salt water (coastal areas)
   - NaCl increases conductivity
   - Speeds electron transfer
   - Accelerates corrosion

2. Acidic conditions (polluted areas)
   - Acid rain (H₂SO₄, HNO₃)
   - More H⁺ ions
   - Speeds oxidation

3. Higher temperature
   - Faster reaction rate
   - More kinetic energy
```

---

**Corrosion of other metals:**

**Copper:**

```
Green coating forms:
Basic copper carbonate: CuCO₃.Cu(OH)₂

Reaction:
2Cu + O₂ → 2CuO (black)
CuO + CO₂ + H₂O → CuCO₃.Cu(OH)₂ (green)

Examples:
- Statue of Liberty (was copper, now green)
- Old copper vessels
- Copper pipes
```

**Silver:**

```
Black coating forms:
Silver sulfide: Ag₂S

Reaction:
4Ag + 2H₂S + O₂ → 2Ag₂S + 2H₂O

Source of H₂S:
- Air pollution
- Eggs (contain sulfur)
- Onions, garlic

This is why silver jewelry turns black
```

**Aluminum:**

```
White oxide layer: Al₂O₃

Reaction:
4Al + 3O₂ → 2Al₂O₃

But this is PROTECTIVE:
- Thin, hard layer
- Prevents further corrosion
- This is why aluminum doesn't corrode like iron
- Self-protecting metal
```

---

**Prevention of corrosion:**

```
Method 1: Barriers (prevent O₂ and H₂O contact)
- Painting (car bodies, bridges)
- Oiling/greasing (machine parts)
- Galvanizing (coating with zinc)
- Electroplating (coating with Cr, Ni)
- Powder coating

Method 2: Alloying
- Stainless steel (Fe + Cr + Ni)
- Chromium forms protective oxide layer
- Corrosion resistant
- Expensive but long-lasting

Method 3: Sacrificial protection
- Attach more reactive metal (Zn, Mg)
- That metal corrodes instead
- Protects iron
- Used on ships, pipelines
```

---

**Economic impact:**

```
Corrosion costs:
- ~3-4% of GDP in developed countries
- Billions of dollars annually
- Replacement of:
  * Bridges
  * Buildings
  * Vehicles
  * Machinery
  * Pipelines

Understanding corrosion = Saving money and resources
```

### 7.2 Rancidity - Food Spoilage by Oxidation

**What is rancidity:**

```
Rancidity = Oxidation of fats and oils in food

Fats/oils contain:
- Unsaturated fatty acids
- Double bonds in carbon chains
- These bonds reactive with oxygen

Oxidation occurs:
R-CH=CH-R' + O₂ → R-CHO + R'-CHO
(Unsaturated fat)      (Aldehydes, ketones)

Products formed:
- Short-chain aldehydes
- Ketones  
- Carboxylic acids
- These have BAD smell and taste
```

**Signs of rancidity:**

```
Smell:
- Fresh oil: Mild, pleasant
- Rancid oil: Unpleasant, pungent, "off" smell

Taste:
- Fresh: Normal
- Rancid: Bitter, soapy, unpleasant

Appearance:
- May become darker
- May form sediment
```

---

**Prevention methods:**

**Method 1: Antioxidants**

```
Antioxidants = Substances that prevent oxidation

How they work:
- React with oxygen before fats do
- Sacrifice themselves
- Protect fats from oxidation

Common antioxidants:
- BHA (Butylated Hydroxy Anisole)
- BHT (Butylated Hydroxy Toluene)
- Vitamin E (natural antioxidant)
- Vitamin C (ascorbic acid)

Where added:
- Chips
- Cookies
- Fried foods
- Butter
- Vegetable oils
```

**Method 2: Air-tight packaging**

```
Prevent contact with oxygen:
- Vacuum packaging (removes air)
- Sealed packets
- Opaque containers (light also speeds oxidation)

Examples:
- Chip packets sealed
- Oil in closed bottles
- Butter in aluminum foil
```

**Method 3: Nitrogen flushing**

```
What chips manufacturers do:
1. Fill packet with chips
2. Remove air from packet
3. Flush with nitrogen gas (N₂)
4. Seal packet

Why nitrogen:
- Inert (doesn't react)
- Displaces oxygen
- No oxidation occurs
- Chips stay crispy and fresh

You can test:
- Open chip packet
- Feels "puffed" (nitrogen inside)
- Not air!
```

**Method 4: Refrigeration**

```
Cooling slows down:
- Chemical reactions (including oxidation)
- Microbial growth
- Enzyme activity

Rate of reaction decreases:
- Lower temperature
- Slower molecular movement
- Fewer collisions
- Oxidation slower

This is why:
- Butter in fridge lasts longer
- Oils stored in cool place
- Refrigeration preserves food
```

## 8. Common Misconceptions Clarified

### Misconception 1: Balancing by changing formulae

```diff
- Wrong: "Mg + O₂ → MgO, to balance O, change to MgO₂"
+ Correct: "Mg + O₂ → MgO, to balance, use 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO"
+ 
+ MgO₂ is a DIFFERENT compound (magnesium peroxide)
+ Changing formula = Changing reaction
+ Use coefficients, NEVER change subscripts
```

### Misconception 2: Products can be different from what's given

```diff
- Wrong: "If reaction doesn't balance, products must be wrong"
+ Correct: "Products are determined by chemistry, not balancing"
+ 
+ Cannot change products to make balancing easier
+ Must balance whatever products actually form
+ If can't balance, equation is fundamentally wrong
```

### Misconception 3: All decompositions need heat

```diff
- Wrong: "Decomposition = thermal decomposition always"
+ Correct: "Decomposition can use heat, light, or electricity"
+ 
+ Thermal: FeSO₄ with heat
+ Photolysis: AgCl with sunlight
+ Electrolysis: H₂O with electricity
+ 
+ Energy source varies by compound
```

### Misconception 4: Exothermic = always vigorous/fast

```diff
- Wrong: "If slow reaction, must be endothermic"
+ Correct: "Exothermic/endothermic is about energy, not speed"
+ 
+ Rusting is:
+ - Very slow (takes days/months)
+ - But exothermic! (releases energy)
+ 
+ Respiration:
+ - Happens continuously
+ - Slow, controlled
+ - Highly exothermic
+ 
+ Rate ≠ Energy change
```

### Misconception 5: Oxidation always needs oxygen

```diff
- Wrong: "No oxygen involved = Not oxidation"
+ Correct: "Oxidation can occur without oxygen present"
+ 
+ Modern definition: Loss of electrons = Oxidation
+ 
+ Example: Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu
+ No O₂ involved
+ But Zn oxidized (Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻)
+ 
+ Oxygen perspective is LIMITED
+ Electron perspective is COMPLETE
```

### Misconception 6: Same reactants always give same products

```diff
- Wrong: "Fe + O₂ always gives Fe₂O₃"
+ Correct: "Conditions determine products"
+ 
+ Fe + limited O₂ → FeO (iron(II) oxide)
+ Fe + excess O₂ → Fe₂O₃ (iron(III) oxide)
+ Fe + excess O₂ + high temp → Fe₃O₄ (mixed oxide)
+ 
+ Temperature, concentration, pressure all matter
```

### Misconception 7: Coefficients and subscripts are same

```diff
- Wrong: "2H₂O same as H₄O₂"
+ Correct: "Completely different meanings"
+ 
+ 2H₂O:
+ - TWO molecules of water
+ - Each H₂O separate
+ - Can exist
+ 
+ H₄O₂:
+ - One molecule with 4 H and 2 O
+ - Doesn't exist!
+ - Wrong formula
+ 
+ Coefficient = number of molecules
+ Subscript = atoms in ONE molecule
```

### Misconception 8: All single products = combination

```diff
- Wrong: "One product formed = combination reaction"
+ Correct: "Combination needs TWO OR MORE reactants"
+ 
+ 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂
+ - One reactant (H₂O₂)
+ - Two products (H₂O, O₂)
+ - This is DECOMPOSITION
+ 
+ H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl
+ - Two reactants
+ - One product (HCl, but coefficient 2)
+ - This is COMBINATION
+ 
+ Count TYPES of reactants, not molecules
```

### Misconception 9: Precipitate always white

```diff
- Wrong: "Precipitate = white solid"
+ Correct: "Precipitates can be many colors"
+ 
+ BaSO₄ - White
+ PbI₂ - Yellow
+ AgCl - White (turns grey in light)
+ CuS - Black
+ Fe(OH)₃ - Reddish brown
+ 
+ Color depends on compound formed
```

### Misconception 10: Reducing agent reduces itself

```diff
- Wrong: "Reducing agent gets reduced"
+ Correct: "Reducing agent gets OXIDIZED"
+ 
+ Reducing agent:
+ - Provides electrons to others (reduces them)
+ - Loses electrons itself (gets oxidized)
+ 
+ Oxidizing agent:
+ - Takes electrons from others (oxidizes them)
+ - Gains electrons itself (gets reduced)
+ 
+ Agent does TO others what's opposite to itself!
```

## 9. Exam Strategies and Tips

### Balancing Equations - Step-by-Step Checklist

```
✓ Step 1: Write skeletal equation correctly
✓ Step 2: Never change formulae (mental boxes)
✓ Step 3: Start with most complex compound
✓ Step 4: Balance element with most atoms first
✓ Step 5: Balance polyatomic ions as units (SO₄²⁻, NO₃⁻, OH⁻)
✓ Step 6: Balance remaining elements
✓ Step 7: Count EVERY atom on both sides
✓ Step 8: Check your arithmetic twice
✓ Step 9: Convert fractions to whole numbers (multiply)
✓ Step 10: Add physical states if asked
```

---

### Identifying Reaction Types - Decision Tree

```
Start here:
↓
How many reactants?

ONE reactant:
→ DECOMPOSITION
   (AB → A + B)
   Examples: 2FeSO₄ → Fe₂O₃ + SO₂ + SO₃

TWO OR MORE reactants:
↓
How many products?

ONE product:
→ COMBINATION
   (A + B → AB)
   Examples: CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂

TWO OR MORE products:
↓
Are reactants elements or compounds?

One element + one compound:
→ DISPLACEMENT
   (A + BC → AC + B)
   Examples: Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu

Both compounds:
→ DOUBLE DISPLACEMENT
   (AB + CD → AD + CB)
   Examples: AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl + NaNO₃
   
   Special cases:
   - Precipitate forms → Precipitation
   - Water forms → Neutralization
```

---

### Writing Balanced Equations from Word Equations

```
Given: "Zinc reacts with dilute sulfuric acid to produce zinc sulfate and hydrogen gas"

Step 1: Identify reactants
Zinc = Zn
Dilute sulfuric acid = H₂SO₄

Step 2: Identify products
Zinc sulfate = ZnSO₄
Hydrogen gas = H₂

Step 3: Write skeletal equation
Zn + H₂SO₄ → ZnSO₄ + H₂

Step 4: Check if balanced
Zn: 1 = 1 ✓
H: 2 = 2 ✓
S: 1 = 1 ✓
O: 4 = 4 ✓

Already balanced!

Step 5: Add states
Zn(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → ZnSO₄(aq) + H₂(g)
```

Example: CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O

## 10. Real-World Applications

### Industrial Processes

**Cement Manufacturing:**

```
Step 1: Limestone heating
CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ (Decomposition, ~1000°C)

Step 2: Mixing with other materials
CaO + SiO₂ + Al₂O₃ → Cement clinker

Step 3: Setting/hardening

Gives cement its strength

**Steel Manufacturing:**

```
Removing impurities from iron ore:

Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂ (Displacement)
Carbon (in CO) reduces iron oxide

Removing carbon from iron:
C + O₂ → CO₂ (Oxidation)
Pure iron → Steel (controlled carbon content)
```

**Ammonia Production (Haber Process):**

```
N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g) (Combination)

Conditions:
- High pressure (200 atm)
- Moderate temperature (450°C)
- Iron catalyst

Used for:
- Fertilizers (ammonium salts)
- Nitric acid production
- Explosives
```

### Environmental Chemistry

**Acid Rain Formation:**

```
Step 1: Pollution
S + O₂ → SO₂ (from coal burning)
N₂ + O₂ → 2NO (in car engines)

Step 2: Further oxidation
2SO₂ + O₂ → 2SO₃
2NO + O₂ → 2NO₂

Step 3: Acid formation

**Photosynthesis and Carbon Cycle:**

```

6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

Respiration (reverse - decomposition):
C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Energy

These balance each other globally
Disruption (excess CO₂) → Climate change
```

### Oxidation vs Reduction

| Aspect                              | Oxidation       | Reduction      |
| ----------------------------------- | --------------- | -------------- |

| **Electrons**                 | Loss            | Gain           |
| **Example**                   | Cu → CuO       | CuO → Cu      |
| **Agent that causes it**      | Oxidizing agent | Reducing agent |
| **What agent does to itself** | Gets reduced    | Gets oxidized  |

## 12. Practice Problems with Solutions

### Problem 1: Balancing

**Q: Balance: NH₃ + O₂ → NO + H₂O**

```
Solution:

Step 1: Count atoms
N: 1 = 1 ✓
H: 3 ≠ 2 ✗
O: 2 ≠ 3 ✗

Step 2: Balance H (odd number problematic)
4NH₃ + O₂ → NO + H₂O (try coefficient 4 for NH₃)

Now H: 12 on left, need 12 on right
4NH₃ + O₂ → NO + 6H₂O

Step 3: Balance N
N: 4 on left, need 4 on right
4NH₃ + O₂ → 4NO + 6H₂O

Step 4: Balance O
O: 2 on left, (4 + 6) = 10 on right
Need 10/2 = 5 O₂
4NH₃ + 5O₂ → 4NO + 6H₂O

Step 5: Verify
N: 4 = 4 ✓
H: 12 = 12 ✓
O: 10 = 10 ✓

BALANCED!
```

### Problem 2: Identifying Type

**Q: Classify: CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂**

```
Solution:

Reactants: 2 (CaCO₃ and HCl)
Products: 3 (CaCl₂, H₂O, CO₂)

Both reactants are compounds
Products show ion exchange:
- Ca²⁺ (was with CO₃²⁻) + Cl⁻ (was with H⁺) → CaCl₂
- H⁺ (was with Cl⁻) + CO₃²⁻ (was with Ca²⁺) → H₂CO₃ → H₂O + CO₂

Pattern: AB + CD → AD + CB + (decomposition product)

Answer: DOUBLE DISPLACEMENT REACTION
(Specifically: Acid-carbonate reaction)
```

### Problem 3: Oxidation-Reduction

**Q: In Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂, identify oxidized and reduced substances**

```
Solution:

Method 1: Oxygen tracking
Fe₂O₃ → Fe: Oxygen LOST → Reduction
CO → CO₂: Oxygen GAINED → Oxidation

Method 2: Oxidation states
Fe in Fe₂O₃: +3
Fe in Fe: 0
Change: +3 → 0 (gain of electrons) → REDUCTION

C in CO: +2
C in CO₂: +4
Change: +2 → +4 (loss of electrons) → OXIDATION

Answer:
- Fe₂O₃ is REDUCED (to Fe)
- CO is OXIDIZED (to CO₂)
- CO is REDUCING AGENT
- Fe₂O₃ is OXIDIZING AGENT
```
