# Set A: Chemical Reactions & Equations (40 Marks)

## Section A: Multiple Choice Questions (1 mark each) [8]
1. Which of the following indicates a chemical change?
    a) Melting of ice  
    b) Dissolving sugar in water  
    c) Formation of rust on iron  
    d) Boiling of water

2. In the reaction: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, the coefficient of O₂ is:
    a) 1  
    b) 2  
    c) 3  
    d) 4

3. A reaction that absorbs heat is called:
    a) Exothermic  
    b) Endothermic  
    c) Combination  
    d) Displacement

4. Which reaction type is represented by: Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu?
    a) Combination  
    b) Decomposition  
    c) Displacement  
    d) Double displacement

5. In Na₂SO₄ + BaCl₂ → BaSO₄ + 2NaCl, BaSO₄ is called:
    a) Catalyst  
    b) Precipitate  
    c) Reactant  
    d) Solvent

6. The balanced equation for combustion of methane CH₄ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O has coefficients:
    a) 1,2,1,2  
    b) 1,1,1,2  
    c) 1,2,1,1  
    d) 1,2,2,1

7. Loss of hydrogen is called:
    a) Oxidation  
    b) Reduction  
    c) Neutralization  
    d) Precipitation

8. Which is an endothermic decomposition?
    a) CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂  
    b) 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O  
    c) HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O  
    d) Zn + H₂SO₄ → ZnSO₄ + H₂

## Section B: Short Answer Questions (2 marks each) [10]
1. Write the word and balanced chemical equation for formation of water from hydrogen and oxygen.
2. Explain why balancing chemical equations is necessary, referring to conservation of mass.
3. Define oxidation and reduction with one example each.
4. Classify the reaction Cu(OH)₂ → CuO + H₂O and give its type.
5. Why is nitrogen used to flush potato chip packets? Identify reaction prevented.

## Section C: Long Answer Questions (5 marks each) [12]
1. Describe thermal decomposition of calcium carbonate. Write equation, explain steps and industrial relevance.
2. Explain double displacement and precipitation with reference to Na₂SO₄ + BaCl₂. Include ionic equation.

## Section D: Case-Based Question (10 marks) [10]
Case: A student performs electrolysis of water containing a little H₂SO₄ and collects gases.

Questions:
1. Explain why H₂SO₄ is added (2).
2. Write half-equations at each electrode (2).
3. Predict volume ratio of collected gases and justify (2).
4. If NaCl is used instead, what additional gas evolves? Write equation (2).
5. Suggest one safety precaution in this experiment (2).
