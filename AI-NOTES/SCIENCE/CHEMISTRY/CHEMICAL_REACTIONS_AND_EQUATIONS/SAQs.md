# Chemical Reactions and Equations - 50 Competency-Based SAQs (HOTS)

---

## Instructions for Students:
These questions are designed to test higher-order thinking skills (HOTS) and competency-based understanding. Answer each question thoughtfully, applying concepts rather than memorizing facts.

---

### Questions (1-50)

**1.** A student observes that when she mixes vinegar with baking soda, bubbling occurs and the mixture becomes cold. Analyze what types of chemical changes are happening and predict what would happen if she sealed the container.

**2.** During a camping trip, you notice that aluminum cookware develops a dull coating while iron tools rust. Compare and contrast these two processes in terms of their chemical nature and practical implications.

**3.** A factory uses limestone (CaCO₃) to produce quicklime (CaO). If they process 1000 kg of pure limestone, predict the theoretical yield of quicklime and explain why the actual yield might be different.

**4.** In black and white photography, silver halides decompose upon exposure to light. Design an experiment to investigate how different intensities of light affect the rate of this decomposition reaction.

**5.** A student claims that rusting of iron is a combination reaction because iron combines with oxygen. Critically evaluate this statement and provide a more accurate classification.

**6.** During respiration, glucose reacts with oxygen to produce energy. Explain why this process is essential for life and predict what would happen if the reverse reaction occurred spontaneously.

**7.** A metal refinery extracts copper from copper sulfate solution using iron strips. Justify the choice of iron over zinc or lead, considering both chemical and economic factors.

**8.** Food manufacturers flush potato chip packets with nitrogen gas. Analyze the scientific principle behind this practice and suggest alternative methods to achieve the same goal.

**9.** When calcium hydroxide solution is exposed to air, it gradually forms a hard coating. Explain this phenomenon and predict how environmental conditions might affect the process.

**10.** A student heats blue copper sulfate crystals and observes color changes. Design a series of observations that would help determine whether this is a reversible or irreversible change.

**11.** In electrolysis of water, hydrogen is collected at one electrode and oxygen at another. Explain why the volume ratio is always 2:1 and predict what would happen if salt were added to the water.

**12.** A chemical equation shows: Mg + HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂. Identify the errors in this equation and explain the principles needed to correct it.

**13.** Industrial production of ammonia involves combining nitrogen and hydrogen. Predict how changes in temperature and pressure would affect this combination reaction.

**14.** A student notices that silver jewelry tarnishes faster in coastal areas than in dry regions. Analyze the factors responsible and suggest preventive measures.

**15.** When lead nitrate is heated, it produces multiple products including a toxic brown gas. Evaluate the safety implications and suggest proper laboratory procedures.

**16.** Antacid tablets contain substances that neutralize stomach acid. Analyze what type of reaction occurs and predict the products formed when calcium carbonate reacts with hydrochloric acid.

**17.** A gardener notices that iron tools rust faster when left on soil compared to concrete surfaces. Investigate the factors contributing to this difference.

**18.** In thermal power plants, limestone is used to remove sulfur dioxide from flue gases. Explain the chemical process involved and evaluate its environmental impact.

**19.** A student observes that zinc displaces copper from copper sulfate but not from copper oxide. Analyze the reasons for this difference in reactivity patterns.

**20.** Food preservation techniques include adding antioxidants and vacuum packaging. Compare these methods in terms of the chemical principles they employ.

**21.** During photosynthesis, plants convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose. Classify this reaction type and explain how it relates to the reverse process of respiration.

**22.** A water treatment plant uses chlorine to purify water. Analyze this process as a redox reaction and evaluate potential health and environmental concerns.

**23.** When iron filings are mixed with sulfur and heated, a new compound forms. Predict the properties of this compound and explain why it differs from the original mixture.

**24.** Battery technology relies on redox reactions. Explain how oxidation and reduction occur simultaneously in a simple cell and predict factors affecting battery life.

**25.** A student finds that marble statues deteriorate faster in urban areas than rural locations. Investigate the chemical processes responsible and suggest conservation strategies.

**26.** Industrial production of cement involves heating limestone with clay. Analyze this process in terms of decomposition and combination reactions occurring simultaneously.

**27.** A chef notices that cut apples turn brown when exposed to air but remain fresh when stored in lemon juice. Explain the chemistry behind both observations.

**28.** In metallurgy, carbon is used to extract metals from their oxides. Design a general principle for predicting which metals can be extracted using this method.

**29.** A student heats magnesium ribbon in a crucible with a lid. Predict and explain the differences in observations compared to heating it in open air.

**30.** Catalytic converters in automobiles convert harmful gases into less toxic substances. Analyze these reactions and evaluate the role of the catalyst.

**31.** A laboratory accident involves mixing incompatible chemicals that produce a violent reaction. Design safety protocols based on understanding different reaction types.

**32.** Weathering of rocks involves both physical and chemical changes. Focus on chemical weathering and explain how it contributes to soil formation.

**33.** A student electrolyzes copper sulfate solution using copper electrodes. Predict the observations at each electrode and explain the underlying chemical processes.

**34.** Fire extinguishers use different chemicals for different types of fires. Analyze the chemical principles behind using CO₂ for electrical fires and foam for oil fires.

**35.** A jeweler uses nitric acid to test the purity of gold ornaments. Explain the chemical basis of this test and predict how different metals would react.

**36.** During wine fermentation, grape sugars convert to alcohol and carbon dioxide. Classify this process and explain factors that could affect the fermentation rate.

**37.** A student observes that hydrogen peroxide bubbles when added to a wound. Investigate the chemical reaction involved and explain its biological significance.

**38.** In space exploration, chemical reactions are used for propulsion. Analyze the requirements for rocket fuel reactions and explain why certain combinations are preferred.

**39.** A water softener removes hardness-causing ions through ion exchange. Explain this process as a type of chemical reaction and predict its limitations.

**40.** Airbags in vehicles deploy through rapid gas-generating reactions. Analyze the requirements for such reactions and evaluate safety considerations.

**41.** A student notices that iron nails rust faster when wrapped in wet cotton than when submerged in boiled water. Investigate the role of dissolved gases in corrosion.

**42.** Baking powder contains both an acid and a base. Explain how this combination produces the desired effect in baking and predict what happens when moisture is added.

**43.** A forensic scientist uses chemical tests to detect gunpowder residue. Explain how combustion reactions leave characteristic traces and evaluate the reliability of such tests.

**44.** Solar water disinfection relies on UV light to kill pathogens. Analyze this as a photochemical process and compare its effectiveness with chlorination.

**45.** A student electroplates a key with copper using copper sulfate solution. Design the experimental setup and predict factors that would affect the coating quality.

**46.** Acid rain formation involves multiple chemical reactions in the atmosphere. Trace the reaction pathways from pollutant emissions to environmental damage.

**47.** A biochemist studies enzyme-catalyzed reactions in living cells. Compare these biological processes with the chemical reactions studied in laboratory settings.

**48.** Nuclear power plants use controlled nuclear reactions. Contrast nuclear reactions with chemical reactions in terms of energy changes and reaction mechanisms.

**49.** A materials scientist develops corrosion-resistant alloys. Explain the chemical principles that guide such development and predict testing methods for effectiveness.

**50.** Climate change involves complex chemical cycles including the carbon cycle. Analyze how human activities affect these natural chemical processes and predict long-term consequences.

## Answer Key (Key Points Only)

**1.** • Double displacement (acid-base neutralization) • Gas evolution (CO₂) • Endothermic reaction • Sealed container: pressure buildup, potential explosion

**2.** • Aluminum: protective oxide layer formation • Iron: progressive oxidation (rusting) • Both involve oxidation but different protective mechanisms • Aluminum coating prevents further corrosion

**3.** • Thermal decomposition: CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ • Theoretical yield calculation using stoichiometry • Actual yield affected by: incomplete reaction, impurities, heat loss

**4.** • Photolytic decomposition • Controlled variables: light intensity, exposure time • Measurable outcomes: color change, silver formation • Rate increases with higher light intensity

**5.** • Rusting is oxidation (redox reaction) • Involves multiple steps and water/oxygen • Not simple combination due to complex mechanism • Requires moisture and oxygen simultaneously

**6.** • Cellular respiration provides ATP energy • Exothermic process essential for life • Reverse reaction (glucose formation) requires energy input • Occurs in photosynthesis with external energy

**7.** • Activity series: Zn > Fe > Cu • Iron displaces copper efficiently • Economic factors: cost, availability • Zinc too reactive (wasteful), lead toxic

**8.** • Prevents oxidation (rancidity) • Nitrogen is inert gas • Alternatives: vacuum packaging, antioxidants • Principle: exclude oxygen from food

**9.** • Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O • Forms protective calcium carbonate layer • Environmental factors: humidity, CO₂ concentration • Whitewashing application

**10.** • Dehydration of hydrated salt • Color change: blue → white • Reversibility test: add water • Indicates physical/chemical nature of change

**11.** • Water formula H₂O gives 2:1 H:O ratio • Electrolysis breaks molecular bonds • Salt addition increases conductivity • Volume ratio remains constant due to molecular composition

**12.** • Unbalanced equation • Correct: Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂ • Conservation of mass principle • Balancing coefficients, not subscripts

**13.** • N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ + heat • Higher pressure favors forward reaction • Lower temperature favors exothermic direction • Le Chatelier's principle application

**14.** • Salt air increases corrosion rate • Humidity provides water for oxidation • Prevention: protective coatings, proper storage • Coastal areas have higher salinity

**15.** • Thermal decomposition produces NO₂ (toxic) • Proper ventilation required • Eye protection necessary • Lead compounds are toxic

**16.** • Neutralization reaction • CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂ • Reduces stomach acidity • Gas production may cause bloating

**17.** • Soil retains moisture longer • Increased oxygen availability in soil • Concrete surface allows faster drying • Soil pH may affect corrosion rate

**18.** • CaCO₃ + SO₂ → CaSO₃ + CO₂ • Desulfurization process • Environmental benefit: reduces acid rain • Produces useful gypsum byproduct

**19.** • Displacement requires ionic compounds • CuSO₄ is ionic, CuO is covalent • Water needed for ion mobility • Different reaction mechanisms

**20.** • Antioxidants prevent oxidation chemically • Vacuum packaging removes oxygen physically • Both prevent rancidity • Different mechanisms, same goal

**21.** • Combination reaction (endothermic) • 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ • Reverse of respiration • Requires light energy input

**22.** • Cl₂ + H₂O → HClO + HCl • Oxidation of pathogens • Health concerns: byproduct formation • Environmental impact on aquatic life

**23.** • Formation of iron sulfide (FeS) • Different properties from mixture • Chemical combination vs. physical mixing • New compound has unique characteristics

**24.** • Oxidation at anode, reduction at cathode • Simultaneous electron transfer • Battery life affected by: temperature, current draw, electrode materials

**25.** • Acid rain causes marble dissolution • CaCO₃ + H₂SO₄ → CaSO₄ + H₂O + CO₂ • Urban pollution higher • Conservation: protective treatments, pollution control

**26.** • Limestone decomposes (CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂) • CaO combines with clay minerals • Multiple reactions in cement kiln • High temperature required

**27.** • Apple browning: oxidation of phenolic compounds • Lemon juice: antioxidant (vitamin C) • Acidic pH prevents oxidation • Natural preservation method

**28.** • Carbon reduces metal oxides • Applicable to metals less reactive than carbon • Position in activity series determines feasibility • Temperature requirements vary

**29.** • Limited oxygen supply in covered crucible • Less complete combustion • Product collection improved • Safety consideration for bright flame

**30.** • Convert CO to CO₂, NOₓ to N₂ • Redox reactions on catalyst surface • Catalyst provides alternative reaction pathway • Reduces activation energy

**31.** • Identify incompatible reaction types • Separate storage requirements • Emergency procedures for different reactions • Understanding reaction hazards

**32.** • Chemical weathering: oxidation, hydrolysis, carbonation • Rock mineral breakdown • Soil formation process • Long-term geological changes

**33.** • Anode: Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ • Cathode: Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu • Electroplating process • Mass changes at electrodes

**34.** • CO₂ displaces oxygen (combination fires) • Foam creates barrier (separation method) • Different fire types need different suppression • Chemical vs. physical suppression

**35.** • Nitric acid dissolves base metals • Gold remains unreacted • Acid resistance indicates purity • Qualitative analysis method

**36.** • Biochemical conversion (fermentation) • Enzyme-catalyzed process • Factors: temperature, pH, yeast strain • Anaerobic conditions required

**37.** • Catalase enzyme decomposes H₂O₂ • 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂ • Biological significance: wound cleaning • Natural enzyme activity

**38.** • High energy release per unit mass • Rapid gas production • Controllable reaction rate • Storage stability requirements

**39.** • Ion exchange resin process • Replace Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ with Na⁺ • Chemical equilibrium process • Resin regeneration needed

**40.** • Rapid gas generation required • High temperature stability • Safety: controlled decomposition • Timing precision critical

**41.** • Dissolved oxygen necessary for rusting • Boiled water has less dissolved O₂ • Wet cotton provides continuous oxygen supply • Gas availability affects corrosion rate

**42.** • Acid + base reaction produces CO₂ • Moisture activates the reaction • Gas production causes rising • Timing of reaction important

**43.** • Combustion leaves chemical residues • Specific compounds indicate gunpowder • Analytical chemistry application • Reliability depends on proper collection/analysis

**44.** • UV radiation breaks molecular bonds • Pathogen DNA/RNA damage • Chemical-free disinfection • Effectiveness varies with water clarity

**45.** • Electrolytic cell setup required • Current density affects coating quality • Time duration controls thickness • Solution concentration important

**46.** • SO₂/NOₓ emissions from industry • Atmospheric oxidation to acids • Acid precipitation formation • Environmental damage pathway

**47.** • Enzyme catalysis vs. chemical catalysis • Biological specificity higher • Similar energy principles • Different reaction conditions

**48.** • Nuclear: nucleus changes • Chemical: electron arrangement changes • Energy scales vastly different • Different conservation laws

**49.** • Alloying changes surface chemistry • Protective layer formation • Sacrificial metal principles • Accelerated testing methods

**50.** • Carbon cycle disruption by emissions • Atmospheric CO₂ increase • Ocean acidification • Long-term climate implications
