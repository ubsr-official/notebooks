# Outcomes of Democracy: CBSE Class 10 Political Science Chapter 5

## Introduction
This chapter examines what democracy delivers in practice compared to what we expect from it. We assess democracy's outcomes in terms of:
- Quality of government
- Economic growth and development
- Reduction of inequality and poverty
- Accommodation of social diversity
- Dignity and freedom of citizens

## How to Assess Democracy's Outcomes

Democracy is considered better than other forms of government because it:
- Promotes equality among citizens
- Enhances individual dignity
- Improves decision-making quality
- Provides methods to resolve conflicts
- Allows room to correct mistakes

While people generally support democracy in principle, many are dissatisfied with its practice. This creates a dilemma requiring critical examination of democracy's actual outcomes.

Democracy is fundamentally a form of government that creates conditions for achieving certain goals. Citizens must take advantage of these conditions to achieve those goals.

## Accountable, Responsive and Legitimate Government

### Expected Political Outcomes
The most basic outcome of democracy should be producing a government that is:
- Accountable to citizens
- Responsive to citizens' needs and expectations

### Efficiency and Effectiveness
- Non-democratic rulers can make quick decisions without consultation
- Democratic governments take more time due to deliberation and negotiation
- While slower, democratic decisions are often more acceptable and effective
- Democracy ensures transparency - citizens can examine decision-making processes

### Democratic Performance
- Democracies have been successful in establishing regular, free elections
- They have created conditions for open public debate
- Most democracies fall short in ensuring fair electoral opportunity for everyone
- Democratic governments often have poor records in sharing information with citizens
- They sometimes fail to address people's needs and are not free from corruption

### Legitimacy
Democratic government's greatest strength is its legitimacy - it is the people's own government. This explains the overwhelming support for democracy worldwide, even in countries with non-democratic regimes.

## Economic Growth and Development

- Between 1950-2000, dictatorships showed slightly higher rates of economic growth
- Economic development depends on multiple factors:
  - Country's population size
  - Global situation
  - Cooperation from other countries
  - Economic priorities

The difference in economic growth between less developed democratic and dictatorial countries is negligible. Democracy is not a guarantee of economic development, but it also doesn't significantly hinder it.

## Reduction of Inequality and Poverty

Despite being based on political equality, democracies often exhibit:
- Growing economic inequalities
- Ultra-rich individuals enjoying disproportionate wealth shares
- Increasing income disparities
- Impoverished populations struggling to meet basic needs

Democratically elected governments don't always prioritize addressing poverty despite the poor constituting a large proportion of voters. In some countries like Bangladesh, more than half the population lives in poverty.

## Accommodation of Social Diversity

Democracy's success lies in its ability to handle social differences, divisions and conflicts:
- Democracies develop procedures to conduct competition among different groups
- They allow expression of differences without threatening national unity
- Regular expression of popular opinion prevents accumulation of discontent
- Ability to handle social differences reduces the possibility of violence

For a democracy to successfully accommodate diversity, two conditions must be met:
- Democracy must not be simply rule by majority opinion; the majority needs to work with the minority
- Rule by majority should not become rule by majority community in terms of religion, race, or linguistic group

Countries that accommodate diversity and differences tend to remain democratic, while those failing to do so often lose democracy.

## Dignity and Freedom of Citizens

Democracy stands for the dignity and freedom of individuals:
- All individuals deserve equal respect and dignity
- The passion for respect and freedom is the basis of democracy
- Democracies throughout the world have recognized this expectation
- All democratic countries have formal constitutional safeguards for citizens' rights

While democracies haven't been as successful in ensuring economic equality, they have been successful in advancing the dignity and freedom of ordinary citizens.

For societies historically built on subordination and domination, recognizing all individuals as equal is challenging. However, once democratic principles are recognized, it becomes easier for disadvantaged groups to struggle against discrimination, as seen with women's rights and caste inequalities in India.

## Conclusion

Democracy has successfully established different practices and institutions:
- Regular, free and fair elections
- Open public debate
- Citizens' right to information
- Constitutional protections for citizens

Democracy remains superior to other forms of government because it promotes citizenship, dignity, and freedom while allowing for correction of mistakes and resolution of conflicts.

What is most distinctive about democracy is that its examination never gets over. As people get some benefits of democracy, they ask for more and want to make democracy even better. Public expression of dissatisfaction with democracy shows its success - it transforms people from subjects into citizens.

## Important Definitions

**Democracy**: A form of government where people have the right to choose their rulers and have control over them through regular, free and fair elections.

**Transparency**: The ability of citizens to examine the process of decision-making in government.

**Legitimacy**: The acceptance of authority of a government by its citizens because they believe it has the right to rule.

**Political Equality**: The principle that all citizens have equal weight in electing representatives.

**Accountable Government**: A government that is answerable to citizens for its decisions and actions.

**Responsive Government**: A government that addresses and responds to the needs and expectations of its citizens.

**Social Diversity**: The existence of different social groups in society based on religion, ethnicity, language, or other factors.

**Economic Inequality**: Unequal distribution of income and wealth among different sections of society.
