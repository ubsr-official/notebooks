## 📌 CORE CONCEPTS

### **Nationalism in India vs Europe**

| Aspect                  | Europe                               | India                                      |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| **Nature**        | Formation of nation-states           | **Anti-colonial struggle**           |
| **Driving Force** | Unity of people with shared identity | Unity against**colonial oppression** |
| **Timeline**      | Gradual (centuries)                  | Accelerated (few decades)                  |
| **Key Feature**   | Internal unification                 | **External liberation**              |

### **Critical Understanding:**

* In India: **Nationalism = Anti-colonialism**
* People discovered **unity** in the process of **struggle** against British
* **Shared oppression** = common bond
* BUT: Different groups experienced colonialism differently
* ∴ **Different notions of freedom**

### **Unity with Conflict:**

```
Different social groups
         ↓
Different experiences of colonialism
         ↓
Different ideas of Swaraj (freedom)
         ↓
Congress tries to forge unity (under Gandhi)
         ↓
Unity achieved BUT with internal conflicts
         ↓
Movement: High points → Disunity phases → High points
```

### **Central Theme:**

**"Nation with many voices wanting freedom"**

## 🌍 FIRST WORLD WAR & ITS IMPACT (1914-1918)

### **Economic Impact:**

```
World War I (1914-18)
         ↓
DEFENSE EXPENDITURE ↑↑↑
         ↓
Financed by:
  • War loans
  • Increased taxes
  • Customs duties ↑
  • Income tax introduced
         ↓
PRICES DOUBLED (1913-1918)
         ↓
Extreme hardship for common people
```

### **Social Impact:**

| Impact                                    | Effect                               |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| **Forced recruitment**              | Villages forced to supply soldiers   |
|                                           | Widespread anger in rural areas      |
| **Crop failures**(1918-19, 1920-21) | Acute food shortages                 |
| **Influenza epidemic**(1918-19)     | Combined with famine                 |
| **Deaths**                          | **12-13 million**(Census 1921) |

### **💡 Logic:**

∵ War created economic crisis + social suffering

∴ People hoped hardships would end after war

**BUT** that didn't happen

∴ Anger and resentment increased

∴ **Ripe conditions for mass movement**

## 🕉️ MAHATMA GANDHI & SATYAGRAHA

### **Background:**

* **January 1915** : Returned to India from South Africa
* Successfully fought **racist regime** in South Africa
* Developed  **novel method** : Satyagraha

### **Core Philosophy of Satyagraha:**

| Principle                        | Meaning                                                   |
| -------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Satya**= Truth           | Search for truth; if cause is true = no need for violence |
| **Agraha**= Holding firmly | Firmness in pursuit of truth                              |
| **Non-violence**(Ahimsa)   | Not passive weakness;**active soul-force**          |
| **No vengeance**           | Appeal to oppressor's conscience                          |
| **Persuasion**             | Make people (including oppressors) see truth              |
| **Dharma**                 | Non-violence = supreme dharma; can unite all Indians      |

### **Gandhi's View:**

> "Satyagraha is not physical force. A satyagrahi does not inflict pain on the adversary... In the use of satyagraha, there is no ill-will whatever."

### **Early Satyagraha Experiments:**

#### **1. Champaran Satyagraha (1917) - Bihar**

* **Issue** : Oppressive **plantation system**
* **Who** : Peasants forced to grow indigo
* **Result** : Success; inspired peasants

#### **2. Kheda Satyagraha (1917) - Gujarat**

* **Issue** : **Crop failure** + plague epidemic
* **Demand** : Revenue collection be **relaxed**
* **Who** : Peasants unable to pay revenue
* **Result** : Successful

#### **3. Ahmedabad Mill Workers' Satyagraha (1918)**

* **Issue** : Workers' rights
* **Who** : Cotton mill workers
* **Result** : Successful labor movement

### **💡 Pattern Recognition:**

All three = **Local issues** + **Specific groups** + **Non-violent struggle** = **Success**

∴ Gandhi confident to launch **nationwide** movement

## 🚫 ROWLATT ACT & JALLIANWALA BAGH (1919)

### **The Rowlatt Act (1919):**

**What it did:**

* Passed **hurriedly** through Imperial Legislative Council
* **United Indian opposition** = ignored
* Gave government  **enormous powers** :
  * Repress **political activities**
  * **Detention without trial** for **2 years**
  * No legal recourse

### **Gandhi's Response:**

```
Rowlatt Act (unjust law)
         ↓
Gandhi decides: Nationwide satyagraha
         ↓
Non-violent civil disobedience
         ↓
Hartal on 6 April 1919
```

### **Movement Spread:**

* **Rallies** in various cities
* **Strikes** in railway workshops
* **Shops closed down**
* Mass participation

### **British Reaction:**

```
Popular upsurge alarmed British
         ↓
Feared disruption of railways & telegraph
         ↓
CLAMPDOWN on nationalists
         ↓
Local leaders arrested in Amritsar
         ↓
Gandhi barred from entering Delhi
```

### **JALLIANWALA BAGH MASSACRE (13 April 1919)**

#### **What Happened:**

```
10 April: Police fire on peaceful procession
         ↓
Attacks on banks, post offices, railway stations
         ↓
Martial law imposed
         ↓
General Dyer takes command
         ↓
13 April: Large crowd in Jallianwalla Bagh
  • Some protesting new repressive measures
  • Some attending annual Baisakhi fair
  • Many villagers unaware of martial law
         ↓
Dyer enters, BLOCKS EXIT POINTS
         ↓
Opens FIRE on crowd
         ↓
HUNDREDS KILLED
```

#### **Dyer's Objective:**

> "To produce a  **moral effect** , to create in the minds of satyagrahis a feeling of  **terror and awe** ."

### **Aftermath:**

| Action                      | Response                                         |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| News spreads                | Crowds on streets in north Indian towns          |
|                             | Strikes, clashes with police                     |
|                             | Attacks on government buildings                  |
| **Brutal Repression** | Satyagrahis forced to rub noses on ground        |
|                             | Crawl on streets                                 |
|                             | Do salaam to all sahibs                          |
|                             | Flogging                                         |
|                             | Villages bombed (Gujranwala, Punjab)             |
| **Gandhi's Reaction** | **Calls off movement**(violence spreading) |

### **💡 Cause-Effect Analysis:**

```
Rowlatt Act (repressive)
         ↓
Nationwide satyagraha
         ↓
British panic
         ↓
Jallianwala Bagh Massacre
         ↓
Brutal repression across Punjab
         ↓
MASS ANGER + RADICALIZATION
         ↓
But Gandhi withdraws (violence)
         ↓
Realization: Need BROADER movement
```

## 🙅 NON-COOPERATION MOVEMENT (1921-1922)

### **Ideological Basis:**

**Hind Swaraj (1909) - Gandhi's Argument:**

```
British rule ESTABLISHED with Indian cooperation
         ↓
British rule SURVIVED because of Indian cooperation
         ↓
∴ If Indians REFUSE to cooperate
         ↓
British rule will COLLAPSE within a year
         ↓
Swaraj will come
```

### **The Programme (Stages):**

#### **Stage 1: Surrender & Boycott**

1. **Surrender** government-awarded titles
2. **Boycott** :

* Civil services
* Army
* Police
* Courts & legislative councils
* Schools & colleges
* **Foreign goods**

#### **Stage 2: Civil Disobedience**

* If government uses repression
* **Full civil disobedience campaign**

### **Internal Congress Debate (Sept-Dec 1920):**

| Supporters            | Opponents                                         |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Gandhi, Shaukat Ali   | Some Congress leaders                             |
| Want immediate action | Concerned about proposals                         |
|                       | Reluctant to boycott council elections (Nov 1920) |
|                       | Feared movement might lead to violence            |
|                       | Intense tussle within Congress                    |

 **Resolution** : Nagpur Congress (December 1920)

→ **Compromise worked out**

→ Non-Cooperation programme **adopted**

---

### **2.1 MOVEMENT IN TOWNS (Middle Class)**

#### **Participation:**

| Sector              | Action                                    | Impact                        |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **Education** | Students left government schools/colleges | Thousands participated        |
|                     | Headmasters & teachers resigned           |                               |
| **Legal**     | Lawyers gave up legal practices           |                               |
| **Political** | Council elections boycotted               | Except Madras (Justice Party) |

 **Note** : Justice Party (non-Brahmans) entered councils to gain power (usually Brahman domain)

#### **Economic Boycott:**

```
Foreign goods boycotted
         ↓
Liquor shops picketed
         ↓
Foreign cloth burnt in bonfires
         ↓
RESULTS:
  • Import of foreign cloth HALVED (1921-22)
  • Value: Rs 102 crore → Rs 57 crore
  • Merchants refused to trade in foreign goods
  • Production of Indian textiles ↑
```

#### **Why Movement Slowed in Cities:**

| Problem                                 | Effect                                           |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **Khadi expensive**               | Poor couldn't afford to buy                      |
|                                         | Couldn't boycott mill cloth long-term            |
| **Alternative institutions slow** | Students/teachers returned to government schools |
|                                         | Lawyers rejoined government courts               |
| **Movement needed alternatives**  | These were slow to develop                       |

### **💡 Logic:**

∵ Economic boycott required **economic capacity**

∴ **Poor people** couldn't sustain it

**+** Alternative institutions **not ready**

∴ Urban movement **gradually slowed**

### **2.2 REBELLION IN THE COUNTRYSIDE**

### **A. Awadh Peasant Movement**

#### **Leader: Baba Ramchandra**

* **Background** : Sanyasi; earlier indentured laborer in Fiji
* **Region** : Awadh (Uttar Pradesh)

#### **Issues:**

| Oppression                      | Details                                 |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| **Talukdars & landlords** | Demanded exorbitantly high rents        |
|                                 | Variety of other**cesses**(taxes) |
| **Begar**                 | Forced unpaid labor on landlords' farms |
| **No tenure security**    | Tenants regularly evicted               |
|                                 | No rights over leased land              |

#### **Demands:**

1. **Reduction of revenue**
2. **Abolition of begar**
3. **Social boycott** of oppressive landlords

#### **Methods:**

* **Nai-dhobi bandhs** organized by panchayats
* Deprived landlords of services (barbers, washermen)

#### **Organization:**

```
June 1920: Jawaharlal Nehru visits villages
         ↓
Talks to villagers, understands grievances
         ↓
October 1920: OUDH KISAN SABHA formed
         ↓
Leaders: Nehru, Baba Ramchandra, others
         ↓
Within ONE MONTH: 300+ branches in villages
```

#### **Integration with Non-Cooperation (1921):**

 **Congress Goal** : Integrate Awadh struggle into wider movement

**What Actually Happened** (Congress unhappy):

* Houses of talukdars & merchants **attacked**
* Bazaars **looted**
* Grain hoards **taken over**
* Local leaders told peasants:
  * **"Gandhi declared no taxes to be paid"**
  * **"Land to be redistributed among poor"**
* **Mahatma's name invoked** to sanction all actions

### **💡 Pattern:**

∵ Peasants had **own grievances** (land, rent, begar)

∴ Interpreted Swaraj as **economic liberation**

∴ Used Gandhi's name for **radical actions**

**BUT** Congress wanted **controlled, non-violent** movement

∴ **Conflict** between peasant aspirations & Congress leadership

### **B. Gudem Hills Tribal Movement (Andhra Pradesh)**

#### **Leader: Alluri Sitaram Raju**

**Background:**

* Claimed  **special powers** :
  * Astrological predictions
  * Healing
  * Survive bullet shots
* Rebels proclaimed him **"incarnation of God"**
* Captivated followers

#### **Issues:**

| Colonial Action                         | Tribal Impact                     |
| --------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| **Large forest areas closed**     | Couldn't graze cattle             |
|                                         | Couldn't collect fuelwood, fruits |
| **Livelihoods affected**          | Economic hardship                 |
| **Traditional rights denied**     | Cultural violation                |
| **Forced begar**for road building | Added oppression                  |

#### **Raju's Mixed Message:**

| Gandhian Elements                         | Non-Gandhian Elements                         |
| ----------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Talked of**Gandhi's greatness**     | Said India liberated only by**FORCE**   |
| Inspired by Non-Cooperation               | **Not non-violence**                    |
| Wore**khadi**                       | Carried on**guerrilla warfare**         |
| Asked people to**give up drinking** | **Attacked police stations**            |
|                                           | **Attempted to kill British officials** |

#### **Outcome:**

* **Militant guerrilla movement** (early 1920s)
* **1924** : Raju captured and **executed**
* Became **folk hero** over time

### **💡 Analysis:**

∵ Tribal people had **forest rights** grievances

∴ Interpreted Swaraj as **freedom from forest restrictions**

**+** Respected Gandhi **BUT**

∴ Believed **force necessary** (not non-violence)

∴ **Form of struggle Congress couldn't approve**

### **C. Plantation Workers (Assam)**

#### **Understanding of Swaraj:**

* **Freedom** = Right to **move freely**
* Get out of **confined plantation space**
* **Retain link** with home village

#### **Oppression:**

**Inland Emigration Act of 1859:**

* Workers **not permitted to leave** tea gardens without permission
* Permission **rarely given**
* **Trapped** in plantations

#### **Action:**

```
Heard of Non-Cooperation Movement
         ↓
Believed "GANDHI RAJ coming"
         ↓
"Everyone will get land in own villages"
         ↓
THOUSANDS defied authorities
         ↓
Left plantations, headed home
         ↓
Railway & steamer strike
         ↓
STRANDED on the way
         ↓
Caught by police, BRUTALLY BEATEN
         ↓
NEVER reached destination
```

### **💡 Common Pattern Across Groups:**

```
Different groups heard about movement
         ↓
Interpreted "Swaraj" based on OWN grievances
         ↓
Peasants: Land reforms, no rent, no begar
Tribals: Forest rights, end of forced labor
Workers: Freedom to move, return home
         ↓
Chanted Gandhi's name
         ↓
Linked to ALL-INDIA agitation
         ↓
But visions NOT defined by Congress programme
         ↓
Imagined Swaraj = End of ALL suffering
```

 **Key Insight** : Local struggles + National identity = Complex movement

## 🧂 SALT MARCH & CIVIL DISOBEDIENCE MOVEMENT (1930)

### **Why Salt?**

Gandhi found in **salt** a powerful symbol that could  **unite the nation** .

#### **Strategic Brilliance:**

| Aspect                          | Significance                                          |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| **Universal consumption** | Rich AND poor use salt                                |
| **Essential item**        | One of most essential foods                           |
| **Salt tax**              | Burden on everyone                                    |
| **Government monopoly**   | Over production                                       |
| **Symbol**                | Revealed**most oppressive face**of British rule |

---

### **Gandhi's 11 Demands (31 January 1930)**

**Letter to Viceroy Irwin:**

| Type                          | Examples                              |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| **General interest**    | Reduce military expenditure, salaries |
| **Specific to classes** | Industrialists: tariff protection     |
|                               | Peasants: reduce land revenue         |
|                               | All:**Abolish salt tax**        |

 **Strategy** : Make demands **wide-ranging**

∴ All classes identify with them

∴ Everyone brought together in **united campaign**

 **Ultimatum** : If demands not fulfilled by **11 March** → Civil disobedience

 **Irwin's Response** : **Unwilling to negotiate**

---

### **THE SALT MARCH (12 March - 6 April 1930)**

#### **Details:**

| Aspect                       | Details                                |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| **Start**              | Gandhi's ashram,**Sabarmati**    |
| **End**                | **Dandi**(Gujarati coastal town) |
| **Distance**           | **240 miles**                    |
| **Duration**           | **24 days**                      |
| **Daily pace**         | ~10 miles/day                          |
| **Initial volunteers** | **78**trusted volunteers         |
| **Joined by**          | **Thousands**along the way       |

#### **During the March:**

* **Thousands came** to hear Gandhi at every stop
* Gandhi explained **meaning of swaraj**
* Urged **peaceful defiance** of British

#### **6 April 1930 - Dandi:**

* Gandhi **ceremonially violated the law**
* **Manufactured salt** by boiling sea water
* **CIVIL DISOBEDIENCE MOVEMENT BEGINS**

---

| **Method**   | Passive withdrawal           | **Active law-breaking**                              |

---

#### **Actions Across Country:**

|                                 | Village officials resigned                |

```
Situation: Violent clashes, mass arrests
         ↓
Gandhi decides to call off movement
         ↓
Enters pact with Irwin
```

#### **Terms:**

| Gandhi Agreed                                  | Government Agreed                    |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Participate in**Round Table Conference** | Release**political prisoners** |
| (Congress boycotted 1st RTC)                   |                                      |

#### **Outcome:**

```
December 1931: Gandhi goes to London (2nd RTC)
         ↓
Negotiations BROKE DOWN
         ↓
Returned disappointed
         ↓
Back in India: Government began NEW repression
  • Ghaffar Khan in jail
  • Jawaharlal Nehru in jail
  • Congress declared ILLEGAL
  • Measures to prevent meetings, demonstrations, boycotts
```

## 🚧 LIMITS OF CIVIL DISOBEDIENCE

### **3.3.1 DALITS ("Untouchables")**

#### **Background:**

* From  **1930s** : Called themselves **"dalit"** (oppressed)
* Long ignored by Congress (fear of offending **sanatanis** - conservative high-caste Hindus)

#### **Gandhi's Position:**

| Action                        | Purpose                                                          |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Called them**"Harijan"**      | Children of God                                                  |
| Organized**satyagraha** | For temple entry                                                 |
|                               | Access to public wells, tanks, roads, schools                    |
| **Cleaned toilets**     | Dignify work of bhangis (sweepers)                               |
|                               | Persuade upper castes to give up "sin of untouchability"         |
| **Declared**            | Swaraj won't come for 100 years if untouchability not eliminated |

#### **Dalit Leaders' Different Approach:**

**Dr. B.R. Ambedkar:**

* Organized dalits into **Depressed Classes Association (1930)**
* **Different political solution** to problems

**Demands:**

1. **Reserved seats** in educational institutions
2. **Separate electorate** for choosing dalit representatives
3. **Political empowerment** = solution to social disabilities

#### **Clash at Round Table Conference:**

```
2nd Round Table Conference
         ↓
Ambedkar demands SEPARATE ELECTORATES for dalits
         ↓
British government CONCEDES
         ↓
Gandhi begins FAST UNTO DEATH
         ↓
His reasoning: Separate electorates would
  • Slow down integration into society
  • Divide Hindu society
         ↓
Ambedkar accepts Gandhi's position
         ↓
POONA PACT (September 1932)
```

#### **Poona Pact Terms:**

| What Dalits Got                                          | What They Didn't Get                    |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| **Reserved seats**in provincial & central councils | **NOT separate electorates**      |
| (Later: Schedule Castes)                                 | Voted in by**general electorate** |

#### **Result:**

* **Dalit participation** in Civil Disobedience **LIMITED**
* Especially in Maharashtra & Nagpur (strong dalit organization)
* Dalit movement remained **apprehensive** of Congress-led national movement

## 📚 KEY TERMS GLOSSARY

| Term                           | Meaning                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Satyagraha**           | Non-violent resistance based on truth and soul-force; passive resistance is active, not weak |
| **Forced recruitment**   | Colonial state forcing people to join army without consent                                   |
| **Hartal**               | Strike; closing of shops and workplaces as protest                                           |
| **Begar**                | Forced unpaid labor that villagers had to provide                                            |
| **Boycott**              | Refusal to deal with people, participate in activities, or buy things; form of protest       |
| **Picket**               | Demonstration blocking entrance to shop, factory, or office                                  |
| **Khilafat**             | Movement to defend temporal powers of Ottoman Khalifa (Islamic spiritual head)               |
| **Swaraj**               | Self-rule, independence; meant different things to different groups                          |
| **Purna Swaraj**         | Complete independence (not dominion status)                                                  |
| **Civil Disobedience**   | Breaking colonial laws as form of protest (beyond non-cooperation)                           |
| **Dalit**                | Oppressed; term used by "untouchables" from 1930s                                            |
| **Harijan**              | "Children of God"; Gandhi's term for untouchables                                            |
| **Separate Electorates** | System where specific communities vote only for their own representatives                    |
| **Reserved Seats**       | Seats set aside for specific communities, but voted by general electorate                    |
| **Communalism**          | Politics based on religious community identity rather than national/territorial identity     |
| **Dominion Status**      | Self-governing nation within British Empire (like Canada); not complete independence         |

## 🧠 MEMORY TECHNIQUES

### **Timeline Mnemonic:**

**"Gandhi Comes Royally, Kills Nationalist Concerns, Slowly Dawns Civil Liberation Quite Peacefully"**

* **G**andhi returns (1915)
* **C**hamparan/Kheda (1917)
* **R**owlatt Act (1919)
* **K**hilafat + Non-Cooperation (1920-21)
* **N**on-Cooperation withdrawn (1922)
* **C**onflicts within Congress (1920s)
* **S**imon Commission (1928)
* **D**emand Purna Swaraj (1929)
* **C**ivil Disobedience - Salt March (1930)
* **L**imits exposed (1930-34)
* **Q**uit India (1942)
* **P**artition & Independence (1947)

### **Gandhi's Satyagraha Experiments:**

**"CAK" = Champaran, Ahmedabad, Kheda**

* **C**hamparan (1917) - Peasants vs plantation
* **A**hmedabad (1918) - Mill workers
* **K**heda (1917) - Revenue relaxation

### **Non-Cooperation Participants:**

**"STAP-W" = Students, Teachers, Peasants, Tribals, Plantation Workers**

### **Civil Disobedience Social Groups:**

**"RPB-WW-DM" = Rich Peasants, Poor peasants, Business, Workers, Women, Dalits, Muslims**

### **Why Movements Withdrew:**

**"VNF" = Violence, Negotiations, Failure**

* Non-Cooperation: **V**iolence (Chauri Chaura)
* Civil Disobedience 1st: **N**egotiations (Gandhi-Irwin Pact)
* Civil Disobedience 2nd: **F**ailure (lost momentum)

## 📊 COMPARISON TABLES

### **Non-Cooperation vs Civil Disobedience**

| Aspect                       | Non-Cooperation (1921-22)  | Civil Disobedience (1930-34)          |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| **Method**             | Refuse cooperation         | Refuse cooperation + Break laws       |
| **Trigger**            | Rowlatt Act, Khilafat      | Economic depression, Simon Commission |
| **Symbol**             | Boycott, Khadi             | Salt (universal, concrete)            |
| **Leadership unity**   | Khilafat-Congress alliance | More Congress-dominated               |
| **Withdrawal reason**  | Violence (Chauri Chaura)   | Negotiations, then lost momentum      |
| **Key feature**        | First mass movement        | More organized, targeted law-breaking |
| **Women's role**       | Emerging                   | Large-scale participation             |
| **Communal relations** | Hindu-Muslim unity attempt | Growing communal tensions             |

### **Gandhi vs Ambedkar on Dalits**

| Aspect                         | Gandhi                                                      | Ambedkar                             |
| ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| **Approach**             | Social reform (change hearts)                               | Political empowerment first          |
| **Method**               | Temple entry, social boycott of caste practices             | Organize politically, demand rights  |
| **Term for dalits**      | "Harijan" (children of God)                                 | "Dalit" (oppressed)                  |
| **Separate electorates** | Opposed (would slow integration)                            | Demanded (necessary for power)       |
| **Priority**             | Hindu unity, end untouchability                             | Dalit political power, secure rights |
| **Result**               | Poona Pact (1932): Reserved seats, NOT separate electorates | Accepted compromise reluctantly      |

### **Congress vs Muslim League Vision**

| Aspect                    | Congress                             | Muslim League                                       |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------- |
| **Nation based on** | Territory, citizenship               | Religious community                                 |
| **Democracy**       | Majority rule (one person, one vote) | Communal representation (safeguards for minorities) |
| **Electorates**     | Joint electorates                    | Separate electorates                                |
| **Vision of India** | Secular, united India                | Safeguards for Muslims; later: Pakistan             |
| **Key leader**      | Gandhi, Nehru                        | Jinnah, Iqbal                                       |
| **Fear**            | Division on religious lines          | Hindu majority domination                           |

## 🎓 CRITICAL ANALYSIS POINTS

### **Strengths of National Movement:**

1. **Mass mobilization** - Brought diverse groups together
2. **Non-violence** - Moral high ground
3. **Symbols & culture** - Created collective identity
4. **Leadership** - Gandhi's mass appeal
5. **Flexibility** - Paused when necessary

### **Weaknesses/Limitations:**

1. **Class bias** - Reluctant to support poor peasants, workers fully
2. **Gender** - Women participated but not empowered
3. **Caste** - Dalits remained marginalized
4. **Religion** - Couldn't prevent communal divide
5. **Strategy** - Withdrawals demoralized some activists
6. **Hindu symbols** - Alienated minorities

### **Unresolved Contradictions:**

1. Unity vs diversity of aspirations
2. Non-violence vs revolutionary nationalism (HSRA)
3. Mass participation vs elite control
4. Secular nationalism vs religious symbols
5. Political freedom vs social transformation
